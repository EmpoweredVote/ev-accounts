**Question:** "How should your community's law enforcement relate to federal immigration
enforcement?"

**Orientation:** standard. Rung 1 is the least cooperation (refuse all detainers, share no status
information), rung 5 the most (local police actively assist and support detention). The rungs order
**how far local law enforcement cooperates with federal immigration enforcement**.

**Levels with a lever:** local. The lever is the sheriff's jail and booking
policy, the police department's policy, county jail contracts, and council or county-board
ordinances on cooperation. State law can require or forbid cooperation, but this topic has **no state
role**: a state legislator's act on it → `scope-unavailable` (see hard cases).

**Asked at:** local (`compass_topic_roles`, CA_0302).

**Synonyms:** "ICE detainer" (an I-247 request to hold a person), "judicial warrant", "administrative
warrant", "hold request", "notification request", "sanctuary" or "welcoming" ordinance, "trust act",
"287(g) agreement" (jail model, task-force model), "intergovernmental service agreement" (IGSA, a
contract to house federal detainees), "Secure Communities", "8 U.S.C. 1373", "civil immigration
enforcement".

1. **"Refuse all ICE detainers; prohibit local employees from sharing immigration status information
   with federal agencies"**
   - Means: the community honours no detainer of any kind, and its employees may not share anyone's
     immigration status with federal agencies.
   - Operative clauses: [a] refuse **all** detainers; [b] prohibit employees from sharing immigration
     status information.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: an ordinance or jail policy that refuses every detainer,
     including those with a judicial warrant, **and** bars status sharing. Compound: one side only →
     `compound-partial` (V4.2). "All" is an absence clause: the text must say it.
   - Levels that hold a lever: local.
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 2 because most sanctuary policies refuse detainers **without a
     judicial warrant**. A policy that still honours a court-ordered hold is rung 2, not rung 1
     _(proposed)_.

2. **"Comply with ICE detainers only when they are ordered by a court"**
   - Means: the jail holds a person for immigration agents only when a judge has signed a warrant or
     order.
   - Operative clauses: [a] comply with detainers **only** when ordered by a court.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: a jail policy or ordinance that honours detainers backed by a
     judicial warrant and declines the rest.
   - Levels that hold a lever: local.
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 1: see rung 1.
   - Commonly confused with rung 3 because both limit cooperation. Rung 2 states a **detainer** rule;
     rung 3 states a **resources** rule (no proactive enforcement).

3. **"Follow federal law as required but do not use local resources for proactive immigration
   enforcement"**
   - Means: do what federal law requires, and nothing more: local officers do not seek out
     immigration violations.
   - Operative clauses: [a] follow federal law as required; [b] no local resources for proactive
     immigration enforcement.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: a policy that bars officers from asking about status or
     arresting on civil immigration grounds, together with a statement of compliance with what federal
     law requires.
   - Levels that hold a lever: local.
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - A policy that bars proactive enforcement and says nothing about detainers → `direction-only`:
     rungs 1 and 2 bar proactive enforcement too, and the detainer rule is what separates them (V4,
     H14) _(ruled 2026-10-01)_.
   - "We follow the law" with no policy named → `rhetorical`.

4. **"Honor ICE detainers and share information proactively when federal agencies request it"**
   - Means: the jail honours detainers, and local agencies give federal agents the information they
     ask for.
   - Operative clauses: [a] honour detainers (not only court-ordered ones); [b] share information
     when federal agencies request it.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: a jail policy or ordinance that honours detainer requests
     **and** provides release dates or status information on request. Compound: one side only →
     `compound-partial` (V4.2).
   - Levels that hold a lever: local.
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 5 because a 287(g) jail agreement includes honouring detainers. Rung
     5 needs local officers **acting** in enforcement or support for detention; responding to
     requests is rung 4.

5. **"Direct local police to actively assist with immigration enforcement and support federal
   detention operations"**
   - Means: local officers take part in immigration enforcement, and the community helps federal
     detention.
   - Operative clauses: [a] local police actively assist immigration enforcement; [b] support federal
     detention operations.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: entering or renewing a 287(g) agreement (evidences [a]) **and**
     a contract to hold federal detainees or support for a detention facility (evidences [b]); own
     words calling for both. Compound: one side only → `compound-partial` (V4.2) _(proposed)_.
   - Levels that hold a lever: local (sheriff, county board, city council).
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 4: see rung 4.

**Hard cases:**
- **State law on local cooperation** (a state mandate to cooperate, or a state ban on it). This topic
  has no state role, so a state legislator's vote or bill → BLANK `scope-unavailable`, even when the
  bill's content matches a rung. Worked case: IN SB 76 (2026), a multi-provision state bill; the blind
  label rested on two of its clauses, and adjudication read rung 4 on the merits but recorded
  `scope-unavailable` (gold, excluded from certification).
- **A local official under a state mandate.** Complying with a state law that requires or forbids
  cooperation is not the official's own position → `direction-only` at most _(proposed)_.
- **Opposing a detention facility** shows only that the person rejects rung 5 [b] → `direction-only`.
- **Legal-defence funds, driver's licences, and "welcoming city" proclamations** do not speak to law
  enforcement's cooperation → `adjacent` or `rhetorical`.
- **Resolutions condemning or praising federal raids** → `rhetorical` unless they set a local policy.
- **Budget votes** that fund a jail or a 287(g) unit among other items → V4 `multi-subject`.
