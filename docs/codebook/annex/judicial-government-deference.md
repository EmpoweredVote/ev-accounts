**Question:** "When elected officials or a government agency make a decision, how closely should
courts review it?"

**Orientation:** off-axis. Rung 1 is the least deference (the government must prove its authority),
rung 5 the most (overturn only for a plain and serious violation). The rungs order **who gets the
benefit of the doubt when the law is unclear** — a burden of proof in judicial review, not more or
less government action. The same person may want close review of one agency and deference to
another; never infer a rung from which policies the person favours or which side won.

**Levels with a lever:** judicial. The lever is a judge's standard of review
in rulings on government decisions. Legislators and executives do not review decisions as a court →
`off` (V2) _(proposed)_.

**Asked at:** judicial (`compass_topic_roles`, CA_0302).

**Synonyms:** "standard of review", "de novo", "deference", "Chevron deference", "Loper Bright"
(2024, which ended Chevron deference in federal courts), "Skidmore", "Auer" or "Kisor" deference,
"arbitrary and capricious", "abuse of discretion", "substantial evidence", "rational basis", "clear
statement rule", "major questions doctrine", "nondelegation", "ultra vires", "presumption of
regularity", "judicial restraint".

1. **"When the law is unclear, courts should side with the individual and require the government to
   prove its decision was clearly within its legal authority."**
   - Means: when the law is unclear, the government loses unless it proves clear authority.
   - Operative clauses: [a] when the law is unclear, side with the individual; [b] the burden is on
     the government to prove its decision was **clearly** within its authority.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: own words or an opinion the person wrote that states, as a
     general rule, that unclear authority is read against the government.
   - Levels that hold a lever: judicial (judges).
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 2 because both lean toward the individual. Rung 1 puts the
     **burden** on the government; rung 2 only overturns when the legal basis is **doubtful**.
   - A **clear-statement rule for one class of decision** (for example, major questions only) is
     narrower than "when the law is unclear" → `direction-only` (V4.2 "Broader than the instrument")
     _(proposed)_.

2. **"When the law is unclear, courts should lean toward the individual and overturn decisions whose
   legal basis is doubtful."**
   - Means: unclear law tilts toward the individual, and doubtful decisions fall.
   - Operative clauses: [a] lean toward the individual; [b] overturn decisions whose legal basis is
     doubtful.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: an opinion the person wrote that overturns a decision because
     its basis is doubtful **and** states the tilt as a rule, plus something that excludes rung 1
     (the person does not put the full burden on the government).
   - Levels that hold a lever: judicial (judges).
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 1: see rung 1.

3. **"Courts should not favor either side. When the law is unclear, neither the government nor the
   individual gets the benefit of the doubt."**
   - Means: the court decides the best reading of the law itself, with no presumption for either side.
   - Operative clauses: [a] favour neither side; [b] no benefit of the doubt to either.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: own words or an opinion the person wrote that says the court
     must decide the best reading of an unclear law itself and give the agency's reading no weight
     as such _(proposed)_.
   - Levels that hold a lever: judicial (judges).
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 2 because rejecting deference to agencies is often described as
     protecting the individual. Rejecting deference alone is rung 3 unless the passage also tilts
     unclear law toward the individual _(proposed)_.

4. **"When the law is unclear, courts should lean toward the government and uphold its decision
   unless it clearly broke the law."**
   - Means: unclear law tilts toward the government; the decision stands unless clearly unlawful.
   - Operative clauses: [a] lean toward the government; [b] uphold unless it clearly broke the law.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: own words or an opinion the person wrote that defends
     deference to a reasonable agency reading of an unclear law (Chevron-style deference), plus
     something that excludes rung 5 (review is still real) _(proposed)_.
   - Levels that hold a lever: judicial (judges).
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 5 because both defer. Rung 5 is "almost entirely".

5. **"Courts should defer to elected officials and agencies almost entirely, overturning a decision
   only for a plain and serious violation of the law."**
   - Means: courts almost never overturn government decisions.
   - Operative clauses: [a] defer almost entirely; [b] overturn only for a plain and serious violation.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: own words that the court should overturn only plain and serious
     violations, as a general rule across agencies **and** elected officials.
   - Levels that hold a lever: judicial (judges).
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 4: see rung 4.

**Hard cases:**
- **A standard of review that binding law sets** (rational-basis review, arbitrary-and-capricious
  review) is the law's choice. A ruling that applies it as required does not show the judge's posture
  → `direction-only` at most. A concurrence or dissent that argues **for** a different standard is
  the judge's own position _(proposed)_.
- **Outcome is not posture.** A ruling for or against the government in one case does not set a rung;
  code the standard the opinion states _(proposed)_.
- **Police searches and criminal cases.** The referent is decisions by elected officials and agencies.
  A Fourth Amendment or criminal-procedure ruling is about the coercive state in one case → `adjacent`
  _(proposed)_.
- **Only one branch.** A passage about agencies only, or elected officials only, meets the rung for
  that referent; rung 5 names both, so one side → `compound-partial` _(proposed)_.
- **Judicial ethics.** Candidates may not promise outcomes. A questionnaire answer on deference to
  agencies is `statement-answer` (V3); "rein in government overreach" with no standard named →
  `rhetorical`.
- **A written opinion** the judge authored is `record` (`record_kind = author`); the instrument is the
  case name and docket. Joining another judge's opinion is `vote` _(proposed)_. On a unanimous
  appellate panel, the **author** is coded on the opinion (own words); a judge who only **joined** it
  falls under `near-unanimous` _(ruled 2026-10-01)_.
- **A record from a lower court** is `pre-seating`: valid for that office only (V5). It counts for
  the current seat only when the rung's lever is the same at both courts (an opinion that shows the
  judge's method, the judge's own sealing or access practice), and the coder names that lever _(ruled 2026-10-01)_.
