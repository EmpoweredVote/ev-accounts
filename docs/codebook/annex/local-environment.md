**Status:** draft (2026-10-01). Lines marked _(proposed)_ are a drafter's reading, not yet ruled;
lines marked _(ruled 2026-10-01)_ carry an operator ruling (Chris Andrews). No

**Question:** "How should your community balance new development with environmental preservation?"

**Orientation:** standard. Rung 1 puts environmental protection first and blocks harmful
development, rung 5 removes local environmental rules beyond state and federal law. The rungs order
**how strict local rules are when development and the local environment conflict**. The particular
good (trees, open space, wetlands, creeks) is evidence that places a person; it is not what the rungs
differ on.

**Levels with a role:** local (`compass_topic_roles`). The lever is local land-use approvals and local
environmental ordinances (tree, wetland, creek, hillside, open space). State environmental-review law
and federal law are a floor that local officials cannot lower. State and federal officeholders hold
no lever here → `scope-unavailable`.

**Synonyms:** "tree ordinance", "heritage tree", "tree canopy", "riparian buffer", "wetland setback",
"critical areas ordinance", "open-space preservation", "conservation easement", "environmental
review", "mitigation", "no net loss", "in-lieu fee", "environmental impact report", "greenbelt",
"habitat conservation plan".

1. **"Make environmental protection the overriding priority — block or sharply limit development that
   would harm the local environment"**
   - Means: protecting the local environment comes first, and harmful development is stopped or
     tightly limited.
   - Operative clauses: [a] environmental protection as the overriding priority; [b] block or sharply
     limit harmful development.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: a moratorium or ban on development in a sensitive area; a vote
     to deny a project on environmental grounds **plus** own words that protection comes first.
   - Levels that hold a lever: local.
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 2 because a denial is also a strong demand for proof. A **denial**
     or **ban** is rung 1 [b]; a rule that lets the project go ahead **once it proves no harm** is
     rung 2. One project denial alone may rest on other grounds (traffic, design) → `direction-only`
     unless the passage names environmental harm _(proposed)_.

2. **"Lean strongly toward protection — put the burden on development to prove it will not damage
   the environment before it can proceed"**
   - Means: development may go ahead only after it shows it will not harm the environment.
   - Operative clauses: [a] lean strongly toward protection; [b] the burden of proof is on the
     developer, before approval.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: an ordinance that requires a showing of no harm, full
     mitigation or no net loss before approval; own words putting the burden on the project.
   - Levels that hold a lever: local.
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 1: see rung 1.
   - Commonly confused with rung 3 because both set standards. Rung 2 makes the project **prove no
     damage**; rung 3 sets **consistent standards** the project must meet, with room on how
     _(proposed)_.

3. **"Apply consistent environmental standards while giving developers reasonable flexibility on
   implementation"**
   - Means: the same rules apply to every project, but developers can choose how to meet them.
   - Operative clauses: [a] consistent standards; [b] reasonable flexibility on implementation.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: an ordinance that sets one standard (for example a canopy or
     buffer requirement) and lets the developer choose among ways to meet it. Compound: one side only
     → `compound-partial` (V4.2).
   - Levels that hold a lever: local.
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - **An option to pay instead of meeting the standard** (an in-lieu fee) relaxes the standard; it is
     not "flexibility on implementation". It leans toward rung 4 → `direction-only` _(proposed)_.

4. **"Lean toward development — treat environmental cost as a manageable trade-off and approve
   projects with clear economic benefit"**
   - Means: development usually wins when it brings clear economic benefit; the environmental cost is
     accepted and managed.
   - Operative clauses: [a] lean toward development; [b] environmental cost treated as a manageable
     trade-off; [c] approve projects with clear economic benefit.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: a vote to approve a project over a stated environmental
     objection, with own words citing its economic benefit; own words that economic benefit outweighs
     the local environmental cost.
   - Levels that hold a lever: local.
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 3 because most approvals are routine. An approval vote with no
     environmental objection on the record shows nothing about the trade-off → `direction-only`
     _(proposed)_.

5. **"Remove local environmental restrictions beyond what state and federal law requires"**
   - Means: the community keeps no environmental rule of its own; only state and federal law apply.
   - Operative clauses: [a] remove local environmental restrictions; [b] beyond what state and
     federal law requires (the floor stays, nothing above it).
   - Establishing evidence looks like: a vote or own words to repeal the community's own environmental
     ordinances as a set. [b] is a limit clause: repealing **one** local rule does not show that the
     person wants none above the floor (V4.2 "Silence is not a clause") → `direction-only`.
   - Levels that hold a lever: local.
   - Known chair-shaped instruments: _(none on file)_.
   - Commonly confused with rung 4 because both favour development; rung 5 removes the rules,
     rung 4 weighs each project under them.

**Hard cases:**
- **Climate, energy, waste and pollution measures** (emissions targets, clean energy, plastic bans,
  recycling) do not speak to development versus the local environment → `adjacent`. They belong to
  other topics (`climate-change`, `city-sanitation`).
- **Park funding and acquisition** with no development decision → `adjacent` _(proposed)_.
- **State law that removes local environmental rules** is a state act; a state officeholder holds no
  lever here (`scope-unavailable`). For a local officeholder, a resolution for or against such a law
  → V2 `adjacent` (H12), unless the passage states their own position on local rules _(proposed)_.
- **State environmental-review suits and appeals** filed by residents are not the officeholder's act
  → V1 fails.
- **Budget and omnibus votes**, and **general plans** → V4 `multi-subject`.
- **"Smart growth" and "sustainable development"** with no rule named → `rhetorical`.
