**Question:** "Should schools have police officers on campus?" _(ruled 2026-10-19, Q5)_. A substantive change
from revision 1 ("What role should police officers play in schools?"): Draft C measures presence, not role.

**Guidance:** "Whether schools should have no police officers, officers at some schools, or an officer at
every school."

**Levels with a lever:** school (board contract, MOU, own police department), local (city or county council
that funds and staffs the officers), state (statute). Federal: own words only (codebook V2 "No-lever level").

**Synonyms:** "school resource officer" (SRO), "school safety officer", "school police department",
"memorandum of understanding" (MOU), "school-based law enforcement", "police-free schools",
"counselors not cops", "armed guard", "guardian programme", "school marshal".

**Reading.** The rungs order **how much police presence schools have**: how many schools have an
officer, and, once every school has armed security, whether that security must be a sworn police
officer. The officer's **role** (discipline, arrests, use of force) is not on this ladder.

**L1 poles (internal):** *No police officer is stationed in any school* ↔ *A sworn police officer is
placed in every school.*

1. **"Remove police officers from all schools"**
2. **"Remove police officers from some schools"**
3. **"Fund or assign police officers to some schools"**
4. **"Place a police officer or armed security staff member in every school"**
5. **"Place a police officer in every school"**

Draft C has no nested rung and no rung read from silence. Every boundary is a stated scope (all /
some / every) or a stated person (police or armed staff / police).

### Definitions

- **"Police officer"** means a sworn law-enforcement officer: a school resource officer, a district
  police officer, or a commissioned peace officer employed as school security.
- **"Armed security staff member"** means an armed person the law or the body places in a school for
  security who is not a police officer: a school guardian, a school marshal, an armed trained employee,
  an armed contracted guard. An unarmed safety officer is neither.
- **"All schools" / "every school"** is stated when the act names every school (or campus, or facility)
  the body governs or serves, or lists each of them. For a school board, its schools. For a city or
  county council, the schools its officers serve under the programme. For a state, every school in the
  state.
- **Role is not a clause** _(ruled 2026-10-16, Draft C)_. School-discipline terms, arrest power,
  use-of-force rules and training rules are on **no rung**. A record's role terms neither raise nor lower
  its placement. A record whose only officer content is a role term (a limit on what officers in schools
  may do, with no stationing or removal) → `direction-only` on the side it limits.

### Rung by rung

- **Rung 1** is met by ending or defunding the officer contract or programme for all the schools the
  body governs or serves, or by a rule that forbids stationing police officers in schools. Moving the
  money to counselors is not needed and does not change the rung.
- **Rung 2** is met by an act that removes police officers from **some** schools (named schools, a grade
  band, a school type) while the body keeps them at others. The record must state the removal. A renewal
  that lists fewer schools than the contract it replaces states it, if the earlier contract is in the
  sources. A cut in officer numbers that leaves every covered school still covered → `direction-only`
  (the side against police). A removal whose record does not say whether officers stay anywhere →
  `direction-only`.
- **Rung 3** is met by any act that funds or assigns police officers to schools and does not state every
  school: an officer contract or MOU, an officer line in a single-subject vote, a district police
  department's staffing, a grant programme whose money is for officers. A renewal that keeps or adds
  schools without reaching every school → 3. A grant that may be spent on officers **or** other security
  (cameras, counselors) → `direction-only`. A law that only decides which body may contract for officers
  stays `adjacent` (H12, ruled 2026-10-01).
  - **A grant that funds an existing every-school duty is not "some schools"** → `direction-only`
    _(ruled 2026-10-16, R2)_. Example: Kentucky HB 622 conditions aid on one SRO for each campus under
    KRS 158.4414. It states neither "some schools" nor a placement of its own. The rung-3 grant line
    above is for grants with no every-school duty behind them.
  - **A penalty posting** (an officer posted only at a school that broke a safety rule, such as Tennessee
    SB 274's door-lock rule) → `direction-only` _(ruled 2026-10-16, R3)_. It is an enforcement measure,
    not a choice of which schools have officers.
  - **A posting triggered by crime on campus is not a penalty posting** → 3 _(ruled 2026-10-19, S3)_.
    Example: Wisconsin AB 69 requires an armed SRO at a school whose incident and arrest count crosses a
    threshold, and funds the officers. The trigger is crime at the school, not a breach of a school's own
    duty, so it is a choice of which schools have officers: "some schools". The separator from R3 is the
    trigger.
- **Rung 4** is met by a duty or contract that places a police officer **or** an armed security staff
  member in every school, where the body may meet it with armed non-police staff as a **standing
  choice**.
- **Rung 5** is met by a duty or contract that places a police officer in every school.
- **Waivers and exceptions** _(ruled 2026-10-16, Q2, carried over)_. A duty stated for every school meets
  "every school" even when the law lets a body claim an exception for lack of funds or qualified staff,
  adopt a stopgap under it, or meet the duty "as funds and qualified personnel become available". **The
  default duty sets the rung:** a duty whose default is a police officer sits on 5 even when its
  exception allows armed non-police staff as a stopgap; a duty whose default lets the body choose armed
  staff sits on 4. A duty that allows shared officers across schools as a standing choice does not state
  every school → 3.
- A duty that names "an officer" without saying sworn or not → `direction-only` (neither person is
  stated).
- **More than one officer per school** (a staffing level above one) matches no rung → `adjacent`.
- **Armed-staff programme with no every-school duty** (a guardian programme a district may opt into) →
  `adjacent`. Rung 3 is police only.

### Bodies acting under a higher-law duty (ruling (b); V4.1 "Small bodies", limb 2)

Use the state entries under "Higher-law duties by state" below for the duty text and its dates.

- **Police-only duties: compliance on every rung, rung 3 included** _(ruled 2026-10-16, Q3)_. Texas
  (s. 37.0814, from 2023-09-01) and Kentucky (KRS 158.4414, from 2022-07-14) default to a police officer at
  every campus. A board's or council's officer contract there → `direction-only`. A compliance contract is
  not read as "some schools" because it lists campuses: the list is how the board meets the state's
  every-school duty.
  - Reassigning officers among campuses under such a duty is compliance or staffing, not a removal from
    some schools → `direction-only` _(ruled 2026-10-16, R1: Keller ISD's Bear Creek reassignment under
    s. 37.0814)_.
- **Menu duties: choosing police is the body's own choice** _(ruled 2026-10-16, U1)_. Under a duty the
  body may meet with police **or** armed non-police staff (Utah s. 53G-8-701.5, from 2024-05-01; Florida
  s. 1006.12, whose four options include guardians and guards), the every-school clause itself is
  compliance. What the board chooses beyond it is its own:
  - choosing police officers **for named schools** → 3 (U1; this amends the Q3 line for menu duties only);
  - choosing police officers **for every school** → 5;
  - choosing armed non-police staff → `direction-only` (compliance: the law already put armed security in
    every school).
- **A duty that applies only once a body chooses officers is not a higher-law officer duty**
  _(ruled 2026-10-19, S1)_. Example: Virginia s. 22.1-280.2:3 requires an MOU where a school division
  uses SROs, but never requires an officer. Virginia is not in the duty list below. A Virginia board's
  vote to place SROs, or to approve the MOU, is the board's own choice and is coded on the rungs (3 for
  named schools).
- In any duty state, the body's other provisions beyond the law are coded on their own: a staffing level
  above the statutory floor → `adjacent`; role or discipline terms → no rung (role is not a clause). This
  replaces, for Draft C, revision 1's reading of statutory discipline terms. A term the statute fixes
  (the Kentucky MOU discipline sentence, Utah s. 53G-8-703(2)) is never the board's own provision.
- A body that removes officers in breach of a state duty is coded on the removal (1 or 2). That is not
  compliance.

### Unchanged from revision 1 and Drafts A/B

- **Single-subject.** A law that bundles a guardian or armed-staff programme with officer provisions is
  `multi-subject` (a2, ruled 2026-10-16; kept unchanged for the Draft C test, Q4). A physical-security
  bundle (cameras, locks, metal detectors) is not. A city or county budget with an officer line stays
  `multi-subject`.
- **Study, safety audit, task force** → `study-directive`. **Physical security** alone → `adjacent`.
- **Supersession** by contract line: the newest act on the same contract governs.

### Evidence by rung and level

Lever: **school** (board contract, MOU, own police department), **local** (city or county council that
funds and staffs the officers), **state** (statute). **Federal**: own words only.

| # | School level | Local level | State level |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | A board vote that ends the officer contract for its schools, or forbids stationed officers | A council vote that ends the school-officer programme | A statute that forbids stationing police in schools |
| 2 | A board vote that removes officers from some schools and keeps them at others | A council vote that cuts the programme to fewer schools | A statute that forbids police in some schools (e.g. by grade) |
| 3 | An officer contract or MOU for named schools with no police-only state duty behind it, or police chosen for named schools under a menu duty | A single-item vote funding officers for named schools | A grant whose money is for school officers, with no every-school duty behind it |
| 4 | A board policy placing armed staff or officers in every school, its own choice (no state duty) | An MOU staffing every school with officers or armed guards | A mandate for every school met by police **or** armed staff at the body's choice |
| 5 | A contract for a police officer in every school (no state duty), or police chosen for every school under a menu duty | An MOU placing a police officer in every school | A mandate for a police officer in every school (including with a funds-or-staff stopgap) |

## Higher-law duties by state (codebook 0.6, V4.1 "Small bodies", limb 2)

A school board that acts under one of these duties is carrying out the state's rule, not stating its
own position (ruling (b), 2026-10-16; see "Bodies acting under a higher-law duty" above). Each entry quotes the operative text as read on the
official source on 2026-10-16. Check the statute's own history line before applying it to a vote in a
year this section does not cover.

### Florida — s. 1006.12, Florida Statutes (safe-school officers)

- **Duty:** each school board and superintendent must put one or more safe-school officers at every
  school facility, charter schools included. The board picks the mix of four options: school resource
  officer (sub. 1), school safety officer (sub. 2), school guardian (sub. 3), school security guard
  (sub. 4).
- **Operative sentence:** "each district school board and school district superintendent shall partner
  with law enforcement agencies or security agencies to establish or assign one or more safe-school
  officers at each school facility within the district, including charter schools." The same text adds:
  "The school district may implement any combination of the options in subsections (1)-(4)".
- **Effective:** 2018-03-09. The "safe-school officers at each school facility" duty was added by
  s. 26, ch. 2018-3 (CS/SB 7026), approved by the Governor and filed 2018-03-09; s. 52 of the act says it
  "shall take effect upon becoming a law" (no separate date for s. 1006.12). The 2018 wording was
  narrower ("partner with law enforcement agencies"); "or security agencies" and the charter-school text
  came later.
- **In force:** all votes 2023-2026 (the 2025 statutes carry it; section history runs through
  s. 5, ch. 2025-58).
- **Waiver:** none in the text. The four options are the flexibility.
- **URL:** https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/1006.12

### Texas — Education Code s. 37.0814 (armed security officer)

- **Duty:** the board sets the number of armed security officers per campus and "must ensure that at
  least one armed security officer is present during regular school hours at each district campus."
  The officer must be a district peace officer, a school resource officer, or a commissioned peace
  officer employed as security personnel under s. 37.081 (sub. b).
- **Good-cause exception (sub. c-d):** if the district cannot comply because of funding or the
  availability of qualified personnel, the board "may claim a good cause exception", and must adopt an
  alternative standard it can meet. The alternative may be a school marshal, or a district employee or
  contractor who has completed school safety training and carries a handgun under written district
  authorisation.
- **Effective:** 2023-09-01. Added by Acts 2023, 88th Leg., R.S., ch. 896 (H.B. 3), s. 10.
- **2025 amendment:** H.B. 121 (89th Leg., R.S.), s. 2, effective 2025-09-01 (the bill history shows
  "Effective on 9/1/25"; applies beginning the 2025-2026 school year). It (i) lets an employee or
  contractor alternative qualify by completing district-approved training within 90 days of starting
  instead of the handgun-instructor course, and (ii) adds sub. (d-1): a claimed exception "expires on
  the first anniversary of the date the exception is claimed", and the board must then re-evaluate and,
  if still unable to comply, renew both the claim and the alternative standard. So a Texas board vote in
  2025-26 to claim or renew an exception is compliance paperwork under the statute, not a position.
- **URLs:** https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/ED/htm/ED.37.htm#37.0814 ·
  https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB00121F.htm

### Kentucky — KRS 158.4414 (certified school resource officers)

- **Duty:** boards, superintendents and law enforcement "shall cooperate to assign one (1) or more
  certified school resource officers to serve each campus where one (1) or more school buildings are
  used to deliver instruction to students on a continuous basis" (sub. 1). Sub. 2: "Local boards of
  education shall ensure, for each campus in the district, that at least one (1) certified school
  resource officer is assigned to and working on-site full-time". The officer must be armed (sub. 7).
- **Waiver (sub. 2):** if funds and qualified personnel are not available for every campus, the board
  meets the duty "on a per campus basis, as approved in writing by the state school security marshal",
  until an officer is assigned.
- **Contract terms fixed by the statute:** an MOU with the employing agency must state that the officer
  "shall not be responsible for school discipline matters that are the responsibility of school
  administrators or school employees" (sub. 5; sub. 6 is the equivalent for officers the board employs).
  Under Draft C this term is on no rung and is never the board's own provision (role is not a clause).
- **Effective:** the 2022 amendment (2022 Ky. Acts ch. 189 (H.B. 63), s. 1) took effect 2022-07-14;
  it set an August 1, 2022 assignment deadline and removed "as funds and qualified personnel become
  available" from sub. 1. Ch. 189 also lets boards create their own police departments (new sections).
- **Later amendments:** 2025 Ky. Acts ch. 86, s. 7, effective 2025-06-27, adds subs. (3)-(4): from
  2026-2027 a board unable to meet sub. 2 may, with the marshal's approval, employ guardians under
  KRS 158.4431, as a stopgap that does not replace the officer. Apply that only to votes in 2026-27
  or later. (Earlier amendments: 2023 ch. 104, 2024 ch. 165.)
- **URL:** https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=56273

### Utah — Utah Code s. 53G-8-701.5 (school safety personnel)

- **Duty:** "An LEA shall ensure each school within the LEA has the following school safety personnel:
  (i) a school safety and security specialist ... and (ii) based on the results of the needs assessment
  ... at least one of the following: (A) a school resource officer; (B) a school guardian; or (C) an
  armed school security guard" (sub. 2(a)). An LEA is a school district or charter school.
- **Alternatives:** the three options are the flexibility, as in Florida. Sub. 3 also lets an LEA apply to
  the state security chief for "an approved alternative" to the section's requirements (factors include
  size, staffing, location and funding).
- **Contract terms fixed by the statute:** s. 53G-8-703(2) says what an SRO contract must contain: the
  officer will "emphasize the use of restorative approaches to address negative behavior"; a designation
  of offences the officer may refer to juvenile court, must confer on, or must refer to a school
  administrator; students' rights on searches, questioning, arrests and privacy; and the officer's school,
  hours and duties. These are statutory, so they are not the board's own provision. A board term that
  goes further is.
- **Effective:** 2024-05-01. H.B. 84 (2024 G.S., "School Safety Amendments"), s. 27, repealed and
  re-enacted s. 53G-8-701.5; s. 54 sets May 1, 2024. Sub. 2(e) (2(f) in the version effective
  2026-07-01) leaves the compliance timeline to the state security chief; the date that timeline set
  was not read.
- **Later amendments:** the version effective 2026-07-01 keeps sub. 2(a) unchanged. It adds a three-year
  assessment cycle and requires a guardian to carry the firearm on duty.
- **Apply to:** Utah board votes on or after 2024-05-01. Before that date there was no officer duty (for
  example Alpine School District, 2019).
- **URLs:** https://le.utah.gov/~2024/bills/static/HB0084.html ·
  https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title53G/Chapter8/53G-8-S701.5.html
