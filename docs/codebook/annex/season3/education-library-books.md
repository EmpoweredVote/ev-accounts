**Question:** "How should schools handle challenges to books in libraries and classrooms?"

**Orientation:** standard. Each rung names **the most a challenge can do to a book**, one act per rung.
Poles (L1, stored in the revision's `rationale`, not shown to voters): *a challenge cannot remove or restrict a
book* ↔ *a challenge removes a book for all students with no review.*

**Levels with a lever:** school (the board's reconsideration policy) and state (a statute or rule that sets the
challenge process for every district). A city or county council governs public libraries, not school
libraries → `adjacent` unless the act covers school collections _(proposed)_.

**Asked at:** federal, state, local, school (`compass_topic_roles`, CA_0302). Own words only at: federal
(codebook V2 "No-lever level").

**Synonyms:** "reconsideration policy", "request for reconsideration", "challenged material",
"review committee", "media specialist", "collection development", "weeding", "harmful to minors",
"obscene", "sexual conduct", "freedom to read", "Library Bill of Rights", "parental opt-out",
"restricted list", "classroom library".

**Furthest act** _(ruled 2026-10-15, Q2)_. A record that states the acts of two neighbouring rungs sits on the
rung of its **furthest** act: an opt-out beside a review that can remove → 3; a review that pulls the book
meanwhile → 4; a content bar beside an opt-out → 2. Each lower act must still be stated; nothing is inferred
from silence (V4.2).

1. **"Bar schools from removing or restricting a book because someone objects to its content"**
   - Means: a challenge to a book's content cannot remove it or limit access to it.
   - Operative clause: [a] a bar on removing or restricting a book because of an objection to its content.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: a board policy or statute that forbids removing or restricting a book
     because of a challenge to its content.
   - Levels that hold a lever: school; state.
   - A bar named on viewpoint, ideas or author identity only is narrower than [a]: beside a review that can
     still remove → 3; with no process stated → `direction-only` _(proposed)_.
   - Weeding a worn or out-of-date copy is not removal "because someone objects" _(proposed)_.

2. **"Answer a challenge by letting the parent restrict the book for their own child"**
   - Means: the response to a challenge is that the parent may restrict the book for their own child; the
     book is not removed for others.
   - Operative clause: [a] a parent's right to restrict their own child's access, offered as the response to a
     challenge.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: a board policy or statute whose challenge remedy is the parent's
     restriction for their own child, with no removal process.
   - Levels that hold a lever: school; state.
   - A standing opt-out (a restriction list open at any time, not the response to a challenge) does not meet
     [a] → `adjacent` _(proposed; Stage 3 finding 3 — moving standing opt-outs onto this rung is an annex
     change that needs a ruling)_.
   - A checkout-record notice alone → `adjacent`.

3. **"Remove a challenged book for all students after a review body finds it unsuitable"**
   - Means: a challenged book is removed for everyone only after a reviewer finds it unsuitable.
   - Operative clause: [a] removal for all students follows a review finding.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: a reconsideration policy or statute where a reviewer decides and removal
     follows its finding.
   - Levels that hold a lever: school; state.
   - **Any reviewer meets it** (c-lib): a committee of any make-up, the board, an administrator, a librarian,
     a state agency. A board that may remove only on a committee finding meets it.
   - A state agency that reviews each submitted book before adding it to a removal list meets it (gold
     D21-shamp-library).
   - Commonly confused with rung 4: if the book is pulled **during** the review, it is rung 4.

4. **"Pull a challenged book from all students until a review decides"**
   - Means: a challenge removes or restricts the book for everyone from filing until the review decides.
   - Operative clause: [a] removal or restriction for all students from the challenge until the review decides.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: a reconsideration policy or statute that removes or restricts a
     challenged book pending review.
   - Levels that hold a lever: school; state.
   - "Pull" includes restricting the book for all students (for example, parental permission for everyone)
     until the review decides.
   - **A pull for one class of challenge** (for example, objections to sexual content only) **meets [a]** —
     "a challenged book" is general in the kind (L3) _(ruled 2026-10-15, Q4; supersedes the Season 2 annex
     line that coded it `direction-only`)_.
   - A policy silent on access during review does not reach rung 4 (V4.2). Text that requires approval
     "before returning" a book presupposes a removal without stating one → not [a] (Escambia 2025 directive).

5. **"Remove a challenged book for all students with no review"**
   - Means: a challenge alone removes the book for everyone; no review is needed.
   - Operative clause: [a] a challenged book is removed for all students with no review.
   - Establishing evidence looks like: a policy or statute that removes a reported or challenged book with no
     review.
   - Levels that hold a lever: school; state.
   - "No review" must be stated (silence rule, V4.2).
   - Commonly confused with rung 4: rung 4's removal ends when a review decides; rung 5's does not depend on
     one.

**Hard cases:**
- **Content-rule laws** _(Q3 default kept 2026-10-15)_. A law that orders removal of every book meeting a
  statutory content test ("harmful to minors", described sexual conduct) sets **what** may be removed, not
  what a challenge does → `direction-only` by itself. If the act also states a challenge process, that process
  decides the rung.
- **Removal lists** _(provisional, Stage 3 settlement 2026-10-15; Chris may overrule)_. A removal list compiled
  by another body is not a challenge in this district. An act that removes listed books, with or without
  review, is coded like a content-rule law: `direction-only` by itself; the challenge process the act states
  decides the rung.
- **State law and school boards.** A state law that sets the challenge process for every district states the
  rule → `on-question`. A law that only decides which body hears challenges → `adjacent` (V2, H12)
  _(ruled 2026-10-01)_. A state law is the legislator's act, not a school-board member's.
- **Removing a legal defence** for librarians or teachers (criminal liability for distributing material) →
  `direction-only` _(proposed)_.
- **Ratings, labels, vendor rules and checkout-record access** for parents → `adjacent` _(proposed)_.
- **Assigned texts in a course** are in scope ("classrooms"). A curriculum standard that names no challenge
  process → `adjacent` (curriculum has its own topic).
- **A vote on one book** → `direction-only`, unless the vote adopts or applies a stated process _(proposed)_.
- **Budget or omnibus votes, and bundled state book laws** → `multi-subject` under the codebook 0.5
  single-subject test.
- **Small bodies.** The small-body vote rule (bodies of ten or fewer) is a separate codebook 0.6 proposal. Until
  it is ruled into the codebook, apply the codebook's tally test as written.
