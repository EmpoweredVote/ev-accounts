# Bob Blumenfield — 2026 LA Mayoral Candidate / CD3 Council Member

**Party:** Democrat
**Current office:** LA City Council District 3 (resigned to run for mayor); President pro tempore since September 20, 2024
**Represents:** West San Fernando Valley — Canoga Park, Reseda, Tarzana, Winnetka, Woodland Hills
**Elected to council:** 2013, reelected 2017, 2022
**Prior office:** CA State Assembly AD40 (2008–2012), then AD45 after redistricting
**Running for:** LA Mayor 2026 (resigned council seat)
**State Senate 2028 campaign:** FPPC ID# 1483728 (bobblumenfield.com currently oriented toward this, NOT the mayor's race)

## Verified Stances (2026-05-06)

### housing — 4
Voted AGAINST December 2024 proposal allowing mixed-income apartments near transit in single-family zones ("too one-size-fits-all"). Opposed 220-unit affordable project in Reseda ("large scale apartments should not be wedged in-between single family homes by right"). Philosophy: density concentrated in Warner Center only; ADUs and developer-led solutions elsewhere. Prefers reducing mandates over mandating large-scale affordable construction.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Blumenfield, https://www.bobblumenfield.com/housing

### fossil-fuels — 2
Backed campaign to PHASE OUT oil drilling in LA (active cessation of existing extraction). Champions LA Green New Deal with fossil fuel transition. Pension divestment from climate-damaging companies. Aligns with stopping new permits and winding down existing production.
Sources: https://www.bobblumenfield.com/parks-and-enviornment, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Blumenfield

### climate-change — 3
Led city pension fund divestment from climate-damaging companies; supports LA Green New Deal (renewable energy, locally sourced water, stronger building standards); backed phasing out oil drilling in LA; advocates fair worker transition. Strong commitment at city level but not emergency-declaration or all-activity ban.
Sources: https://www.bobblumenfield.com/parks-and-enviornment, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Blumenfield

### homelessness-response (city-level) — 3
"Balance of compassion and reasonable limits." Built 5 interim housing sites 2020–2021 (Willows Bridge Home 80 beds, Sycamore/Sunflower 225+ beds, Project Roomkey hotels, 2 Safe Parking sites). Opposes encampments in parks, sidewalks, bus shelters. Housing-first + enforcement model.
Sources: https://www.bobblumenfield.com/homelessness, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Blumenfield

### residential-zoning (city-level) — 4
"Focusing more dense development in the Warner Center, he is protecting the traditional single family suburban character of the surrounding neighborhoods." Voted against citywide transit-adjacent upzoning. Supports ADUs and senior housing but opposes broad multifamily upzoning.
Sources: https://www.bobblumenfield.com/housing, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Blumenfield

### public-safety-approach (city-level) — 3
Supports traditional LAPD + fire services. Restored engine company to Fire Station 73. Also advocates for CAHOOTS-style co-responder model (social workers for mental health/non-violent calls). Invested $100K in LAPD traffic enforcement. Mixed traditional + reform approach.
Sources: https://www.bobblumenfield.com/public-safety, https://www.bobblumenfield.com/safe-neighborhoods

### local-environment (city-level) — 2
First Cool Pavement neighborhood (urban heat island); LA Green New Deal champion; pension divestment; oil drilling phase-out; 5 new parks in CD3; LA Conservation Corps cleanup partnerships. Ambitious beyond typical city baselines.
Sources: https://www.bobblumenfield.com/parks-and-enviornment, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Blumenfield

### transportation-priorities (city-level) — 3
Bike lanes, new traffic signals, crosswalks, $100K LAPD traffic enforcement, Reimagine Ventura Boulevard pedestrian improvements. "Dedicated to traffic reduction efforts in the Valley." Balanced multi-modal but car-compatible — no strong transit-first agenda.
Sources: https://www.bobblumenfield.com/safe-neighborhoods, https://www.bobblumenfield.com/meet-bob

### economic-development (city-level) — 3
$100M+ in Sherman Way Great Street and Reseda Rising projects; first publicly owned ice skating rink; Reseda Theater restoration; Reimagine Ventura Blvd. Supports healthy business climate with social justice goals. Balanced public-private approach.
Sources: https://www.bobblumenfield.com/delivering-for-the-west-sfv, https://www.bobblumenfield.com/meet-bob

### city-sanitation (city-level) — 3
LA Conservation Corps partnerships for litter reduction, LA River monitoring, street tree trimming programs, voluntary storage for unhoused. Standard city maintenance investment without major new sanitation policy.
Sources: https://www.bobblumenfield.com/parks-and-enviornment, https://www.bobblumenfield.com/homelessness
