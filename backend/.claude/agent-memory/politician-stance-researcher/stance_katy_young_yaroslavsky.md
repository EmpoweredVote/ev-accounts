## Politician Profile
- **Office**: LA City Council District 5 (elected November 2022)
- **Party**: Democrat
- **Background**: Former legislative director/deputy for LA County Supervisor Sheila Kuehl; former general counsel and director of government affairs at Climate Action Reserve; UCLA Law JD; daughter of former Supervisor Zev Yaroslavsky
- **Key roles**: Budget and Finance Committee Chair; Vice-Chair of Energy & Environment Committee; LA Metro Board of Directors member

## Verified Stances

### housing → 4
Two concrete restrictive-housing votes:
1. 2024 motion to block expedited affordable housing in Historic Preservation Overlay Zone (HPOZ) — chief of staff called a 70-unit affordable apartment "egregious"
2. Voted YES (8-5-2, Aug 2025) on SB 79 implementation with transit-oriented density carve-out — accepted density near transit but protected historic neighborhoods
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Yaroslavsky

### climate-change → 3
Pre-council career at Climate Action Reserve (carbon markets nonprofit) and helped create LA County Office of Sustainability and Measure W (2018 Safe, Clean Water Program) as Kuehl deputy. Market-mechanism approach to climate, not emergency declaration.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Yaroslavsky

### fossil-fuels → 3
Negotiated closure of West Pico Drill Site (Jan 2025) — strong local action against urban drilling — but overall career at Climate Action Reserve focused on carbon offset markets rather than banning all drilling. No documented stance on new statewide drilling permits.
Source: https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/councilmember-katy-yaroslavsky-announces-closure-west-pico-drill-site

### transportation-priorities → 3
Sits on LA Metro Board; named to Transportation Committee (2023). Voted YES on SB 79 transit-oriented implementation and introduced motions to accelerate TOD density. No documented bike lane installations comparable to Raman/Nazarian in CD5.
Sources: https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/statement-councilwoman-yaroslavsky-sb-79-implementation-vote; https://cityclerk.lacity.org/lacityclerkconnect/index.cfm?fa=ccfi.viewrecord&cfnumber=25-0002-S19

### homelessness-response → 3
Voted YES (15-0, Nov 2023) on Good Neighbor Policy for interim housing (CF 23-0844) and on LA Alliance shelter/services settlement (CF 23-1054). May 2025 budget: cut Inside Safe 10%, moved interim housing to reserves "for additional oversight." Shelter-and-services approach with fiscal discipline.
Sources: CF 23-0844, CF 23-1054, councildistrict5.lacity.gov budget statement

### residential-zoning → 4
2024 HPOZ motion blocking fast-tracked affordable housing (chief of staff: "egregious"). Supports SB 79 transit-oriented density (March 2026 statement). Homeowner-preservation-first; accepts density only in transit corridors.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Yaroslavsky; https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/statement-councilwoman-yaroslavsky-sb-79-implementation-vote

### city-sanitation → 3
May 2025 budget emphasized restoring street cleaning, tree trimming, sidewalk/streetlight repairs as "basic, everyday services." Championed $65M Solar Surge (streetlights, March 2026). Strong maintenance-of-existing-services orientation.
Sources: https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/budget-chair-yaroslavsky-revised-budget-disciplined-collaborative-and-strengthens-delivery-core; https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/los-angeles-city-council-approves-65-million-solar-surge-fix-streetlight-outages

### local-immigration → 2
June 2025: co-introduced motion to sue Trump administration over immigration raids ("These actions undermine the fundamental civil rights of our residents"). June 2023: mover of resolution supporting SB 85 expanding refugee/asylee services. Strong anti-ICE enforcement; no open-borders advocacy.
Sources: https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/councilmember-katy-yaroslavsky-introduces-motion-sue-trump-administration-over-unlawful-conduct; CF 23-0002-S49

### rent-regulation → 4
Feb 2025: voted NO on AB 246 (wildfire rent gouging protections) alongside conservative bloc (Lee, McOsker, Park, Blumenfield, Rodriguez) — 7-6 failed adoption. 2024 HPOZ housing block also reflects homeowner/landlord-friendly orientation. No documented rent protection initiatives from her office.
Source: https://cityclerk.lacity.org/lacityclerkconnect/index.cfm?fa=ccfi.viewrecord&cfnumber=25-0002-S3

### growth-and-development → 3
Supports SB 79 TOD near transit; negotiated West Pico Drill Site closure with an eye toward residential development. Protects HPOZ historic neighborhoods. Moderate pro-growth with selective neighborhood-preservation controls.
Sources: https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/statement-councilwoman-yaroslavsky-sb-79-implementation-vote; https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/councilmember-katy-yaroslavsky-announces-closure-west-pico-drill-site

### local-environment → 2
Jan 2025: negotiated West Pico Drill Site closure ("major victory"). July 2025: introduced Polluters Pay Climate Superfund resolution ("If you made the mess, you help pay to clean it up"). March 2026: $65M solar streetlight initiative. Career: Clean Power Alliance, Measure W, Climate Action Reserve. Voted YES on SB 71 (CEQA transit exemption). Proactive enforcement approach beyond market mechanisms.
Sources: https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/councilmember-katy-yaroslavsky-announces-closure-west-pico-drill-site; https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/los-angeles-city-council-votes-back-polluters-pay-climate-superfund-act

### public-safety-approach → 3
May 2025 budget: $15M LAPD increase, 150+ restored civilian positions, $80M for fire equipment. April 2026 police hiring vote with fiscal conditions: "If we add officers above the adopted plan, those costs have to be paid for responsibly." Measured expansion with fiscal guardrails.
Sources: https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/city-council-approves-plan-continue-police-hiring-using-existing-savings; https://councildistrict5.lacity.gov/budget-chair-yaroslavsky-revised-budget-disciplined-collaborative-and-strengthens-delivery-core

### redistricting → 2
Feb 15, 2023: voted YES (12-1-2) on CF 23-0002-S1 establishing an Independent Redistricting Process and new commission for LA City following the 2022 tape scandal. The single NO vote was Kevin de Leon. The measure created an independent citizens' commission with bipartisan structure — aligns with stance 2 (independent commissions with equal representation from both major parties).
Source: https://cityclerk.lacity.org/lacityclerkconnect/index.cfm?fa=ccfi.viewrecord&cfnumber=23-0002-S1
