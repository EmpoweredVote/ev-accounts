Terry Taplin, Berkeley City Council Member District 2 (Elmwood/Claremont/Rockridge/West Berkeley), served on Berkeley Rent Stabilization Board before council. Chairs Facilities, Infrastructure, Transportation, Environment, and Sustainability Committee.

**Stances scored (2026-06-01):**
- housing=2 (introduced "housing as a human right" resolution; Affordable Housing Overlay; 790+ units funded)
- homelessness=2 (enforcement contingent on shelter; services-first orientation; SCU)
- homelessness-response=2 (housing+shelter+wraparound care; not enforcement-first)
- rent-regulation=2 (served on Rent Stabilization Board; opposed ballot measure weakening Measure Q ADU protections; supports removing loopholes; favors replacement units for demolished rent-controlled housing)
- residential-zoning=4 (Affordable Housing Overlay: +6 stories as-of-right for 100% affordable; +1 story in SFR; significant pro-density reform)
- climate-change=2 (Vision 2050 climate-smart infrastructure; transportation = 60% of Berkeley's emissions; chairs environment committee)
- local-environment=3 (NACTO design guide resolution; Ninth Street Greenway; environmental standards with developer flexibility)
- transportation-priorities=1 (self-described "mobility and environmental justice activist"; 51B BRT; NACTO Urban Street Design Guide resolution; Bicycle Plan + Pedestrian Plan investments; Vision Zero; modal shift)
- public-safety-approach=2 (SCU for mental health; "Police officers are not social workers"; centers social services and crisis response alongside police; violence prevention programs)
- local-immigration=1 (Berkeley unanimously passed sanctuary city ordinance; Taplin is progressive council member with no opposition to sanctuary framework)
- civil-rights=2 (zero tolerance for racist policing; racial equity champion; housing as human right; equity framing in transportation; Office of Racial Equity)
- growth-and-development=3 (supports housing production with affordability conditions; Affordable Housing Overlay links density to 100% affordable requirement; not pure market deregulation)
