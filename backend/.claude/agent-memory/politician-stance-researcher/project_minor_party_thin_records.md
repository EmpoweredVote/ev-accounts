Pattern confirmed across multiple SC 2026 House minor-party candidates
(Margo Ellis/Alliance -450104, Dayna Alane Smith/SC Workers Party -450202,
Jessica Ethridge/Libertarian -450402 — see `_SKIPS.md` in
`backend/data/stance-research/sc-2026-house/` for full trails):

Minor-party nominees (Libertarian, Alliance, Workers Party, etc.) in SC's
2026 US House races typically have:
- a bare-bones campaign website (often 3-5 broad thematic planks: "cost of
  living," "foreign policy," "national debt" — general enough to sound
  right to any reader but NOT specific enough to pin to a compass 1-5 scale
  anchor without inferring beyond their actual words)
- no Ballotpedia Candidate Connection survey response
- no local news coverage of their specific candidacy (coverage clusters on
  the major-party/incumbent race narrative)
- sometimes a MORE detailed record from a PRIOR run for a different office
  (e.g. Ethridge's 2022 SC Lt. Governor run had a real Facebook post on
  civil asset forfeiture + cannabis decriminalization) — but that prior
  record may not map to any topic in the CURRENT topic set (no drug-policy
  or asset-forfeiture key exists among the 24 federal compass topics), so
  even a real, well-sourced quote can still be a dead end for scoring.

**Why:** these are volunteer/shoestring campaigns without paid comms staff,
running against non-competitive odds in a US House general.

**How to apply:** don't over-invest search effort chasing 24/24 coverage for
a minor-party nominee — check their own site (incl. sitemap.xml trick),
Ballotpedia, smarter.vote/civoren aggregators, and 2-3 targeted DDG searches;
if that's thin, write the honest-skip trail and move on rather than
stretching general rhetoric (e.g. "reduce foreign spending" → scored as a
Ukraine-aid position) to hit a topic anchor it doesn't actually support.
