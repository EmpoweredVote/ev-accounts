## Natalie Pinkney — SL County Council At-Large C (D, elected Nov 2024, seated Jan 2025)
First Black woman and first Black person elected county-wide in SL County history.
Prior: South Salt Lake City Council (5 years, former Vice Chair).

Topics scored: housing=2, homelessness-response=2, jail-capacity=2, public-safety-approach=2, local-immigration=2, transportation-priorities=2, local-environment=2, growth-and-development=2, residential-zoning=2, economic-development=2
Skipped: city-sanitation (no evidence)

Key evidence:
- Supported $507M Justice & Accountability Bond (Nov 2024, 8-1 council vote, failed with 52% NO) — treatment/transitional housing approach
- Joint Democrat caucus statement opposing ICE mega-detention center (Jan 2026): "Serious and valid concerns are being raised about the rumored plan to house 7,500 ICE detainees in a building that was not meant for human habitation... detention on this scale and in this setting is inhumane and risks violating the civil liberties of those being detained."
- Direct quote on homelessness: "we can either incarcerate or we can house them and either way, we're going to have to pay for it"
- Supports transit-first development, bundling UTA transit passes with apartment leases
- Direct quote on zoning: "increase our supply, streamline process, and also, understanding the qualities of each neighborhood"
- Stated "we must take bold action now to preserve the Great Salt Lake and prevent ecological collapse"

Primary sources: saltlake.citycast.fm podcast (best source), kslnewsradio.com/2146059/, utahfoundation.org/news/meet-the-candidate-natalie-pinkney/, kuer.org voter guide Oct 2024

## Jiro Johnson — SL County Council District 1 (D, appointed Aug 2025)
Background: Assistant Director, Salt Lake County Public Defender Office (180 employees).
Former: Salt Lake Legal Defender Association trial attorney since 2015.
First Black and Japanese-American SL County Council member. Named Minority Leader Jan 2026.

Topics scored: housing=2, homelessness-response=2, jail-capacity=2, public-safety-approach=2, local-immigration=2, economic-development=3
Skipped: city-sanitation, transportation, local-environment, growth-and-development, residential-zoning (insufficient evidence for any of these)

Key evidence:
- Named housing his "first issue" upon joining council: "District 1 has a 'serious unhoused crisis'"
- Focus on "treatment-based alternatives to incarceration" for mental illness/substance use
- Stated on tax increase: "The $87 per year we're asking for the average household, that's not nothing. We're hoping the things we provide as a county makes up for that increase."
- Joint Democrat caucus statement opposing ICE mega-detention center (Jan 2026)
- 2026 budget secured "Criminal Justice staffing increases to help reduce recidivism" and "funding to help address the housing affordability crisis"
- On childcare closure: "It's just absolutely wrong... We can all chip in 23 cents a year for these families"

Primary sources: sltrib.com 2025-06-12 (appointment article), fox13now.com (tax increase), saltlakecounty.gov/newsroom (2026 leadership)

## Laurie Stringham — SL County Council At-Large A (R, elected 2020, LOST GOP convention 2026)
Background: Technical Theater teacher, Skyline High School, Sandy UT native. 21 yrs Kearns Oquirrh Recreation Board. Lost 2026 GOP nominating convention to Kathleen Anderson.

Topics scored: housing=3, homelessness-response=3, jail-capacity=3, public-safety-approach=3, transportation-priorities=3, growth-and-development=3, residential-zoning=3, economic-development=3
Skipped: local-immigration (no documented position), city-sanitation, local-environment (no specific evidence)

Key evidence:
- Supported $507M Justice & Accountability Bond with bipartisan 8-1 council vote (Nov 2024)
- On bond: "We can solve some problems by maybe trying something different" and it would "help us get that recidivism number down"
- After bond failed: "We're going to have to put something back on the ballot because we can't afford to just pay it."
- On homelessness: "Our system has failed the most vulnerable of our communities and we must fix it. Their lives are too valuable."
- On public safety: "Public safety is the number one responsibility of government."
- On housing: "We need to relook at our zoning laws, infrastructure and other resources that are available to make responsible decisions."
- On transportation: "more east-to-west corridors, the completion of Mountain View Corridor highway, more transportation options on the east bench, and expanded public transportation—before we continue to develop more"
- Vice Chair and Republican Majority Caucus Leader in 2025

Primary sources: lauriestringham.com/issues (best), ballotready.org/people/laurie-stringham, fox13now.com (bond coverage)
