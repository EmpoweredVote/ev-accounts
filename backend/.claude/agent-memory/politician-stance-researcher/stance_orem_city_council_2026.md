## Orem City Council — Research Notes (2026-05-29 and 2026-05-30)

### Politicians Covered

**Karen McCandless** — Mayor of Orem (sworn in Jan 2026, defeated incumbent Dave Young by 518 votes). Background: 13 years on Orem City Council (2001-2014), CEO of Community Action Services and Food Bank 2017-2025, served Utah Homelessness Council 2022-2024. Declined party endorsement — stated "local leadership should be about service, not sides." Dark money pledge (no 501(c)4 money).
- housing=4 (opposes high-rise/high-density apartments; supports missing middle)
- growth-and-development=3 (infill + neighborhood input)
- residential-zoning=3 (limited upzoning with community process)
- transportation-priorities=3 (anti-BRT on State Street; pro active transportation)
- economic-development=3 (4-part strategy: small biz roundtables, employer recruitment, UVU partnership, regional partnerships; hired new economic dev director May 2026)
- homelessness-response=2 (services-first; former CEO of primary UT County homelessness agency; led warming center public-private partnerships)
- local-environment=2 (air quality as top-4 governance priority; active transportation advocate)
- public-safety-approach=3 (balanced — no reform stance, no punitive expansion; root-cause social services orientation)
- skipped: jail-capacity, local-immigration, city-sanitation, rent-regulation (all — no evidence)

**Chris Killpack** — City Council member (sworn in Jan 2024, first term). Area Service Manager at Day Wireless. Endorsed by Stronger Orem PAC (more pro-development than Stand For Orem). Declined interview request from Stand For Orem during 2023 campaign. Notably sparse on policy specifics.
- housing=3 (voted YES Wilkerson Farm rezoning May 2025 AND State Street apartment settlement — pro-development voting record)
- growth-and-development=3 (responsible growth rhetoric + pro-development votes)
- residential-zoning=3 (moderate — some density OK near UVU)
- transportation-priorities=3 (multimodal; active transportation master plan)
- public-safety-approach=4 (first responders resource focus)
- economic-development=3 (infrastructure focus)
- skipped: jail-capacity, local-immigration, city-sanitation, homelessness-response, local-environment, rent-regulation (all — no evidence)

**Crystal Muhlestein** — City Council member (sworn in Jan 2026). Background: 6 years on Orem Natural Resources Advisory Commission, Utah County Good Governance Advisory Board, Utah County Republican Party HD56 chair. Endorsed by Stand For Orem PAC + Orem FOP + Orem Firefighters Union.
- housing=4 (explicitly pledged to vote NO on high-density housing)
- growth-and-development=4 (Orem 95% built out; infrastructure first; commercial over residential expansion)
- residential-zoning=4 (strong opposition to apartment upzoning; Stand For Orem platform)
- transportation-priorities=3 (anti-BRT but pro active transportation + regional collaboration)
- public-safety-approach=4 (FOP + Firefighters Union endorsed; first responders top priority)
- economic-development=3 (pro-business but community-values oriented)
- homelessness-response=3 (insufficient evidence — conservative fiscal approach implied but no direct evidence)
- local-environment=3 (6 years Natural Resources Advisory Commission; CARE Tax supporter; moderate stewardship)
- skipped: jail-capacity, local-immigration, city-sanitation, rent-regulation (all — no evidence)

**Quinn Mecham** — City Council member (sworn in Jan 2026). Background: former chair Orem Natural Resources Advisory Commission (6 years); fiscal conservative; on Planning Commission. Endorsed by betterorem.com PAC.
- housing=3, growth-and-development=3, residential-zoning=3 (case-by-case, neighborhood input; open to some density)
- transportation-priorities=3 (multimodal; regional partnership focus)
- economic-development=3 (commercial revitalization + larger employer recruitment)
- local-environment=2 (6 years as chair NRAC + CARE Advisory Commission)

**LaNae Millett** — City Council member (serving prior term, NOT new Jan 2026). Anti-high-density champion; stopped State Street Master Plan (10,000 apartments). Endorsed by Utah Taxpayers Association + Utah County Republican Party + FOP + Firefighters.
- housing=4, residential-zoning=4, growth-and-development=4 (strongly anti-density)
- transportation-priorities=4 (cars-first; anti-BRT)
- public-safety-approach=4 (FOP + fire union)
- economic-development=4 (grants without new taxes; Heart of Orem district)
- homelessness-response=4 (neighborhood protection framing; no services expansion)
- local-environment=3 (tree planting, Utah Lake Commission; stopped road widening)
- rent-regulation=4 (UT state law preempts rent control; Millett's property-rights deference implies opposition)

**Jeff Lambson** — City Council member. Property-rights-deferential moderate; voted YES Wilkerson Farm + State Street apartment settlement. Supported ADUs.
- housing=3, residential-zoning=3, growth-and-development=3, transportation-priorities=3, public-safety-approach=4, economic-development=3

**Jenn Gale** — City Council member. Pro-development bloc; voted YES Wilkerson Farm; serves UVU Business Resource Center board.
- housing=3, residential-zoning=3, growth-and-development=3, transportation-priorities=3, public-safety-approach=3, economic-development=3

### Key Context
- Utah Code §57-20-1 **preempts all cities from rent control** — no Orem official can implement it, so rent-regulation topic requires explicit advocacy for changing state law to score
- Orem has a **small city holding facility** (not a jail); Utah County handles longer-term detention — jail-capacity is a county not city matter; individual Orem council members rarely have documented positions on it
- **Local immigration enforcement**: Orem Police Department not listed among the 11 Utah agencies with 287(g) agreements (as of Sept 2025 SL Tribune report); no documented positions by any Orem officials on local ICE cooperation
- The **State Street Master Plan** debate (10,000 apartments proposed vs. opposed) is THE defining local policy fault line separating housing=3 officials (pro-development bloc: Killpack, Gale, Lambson) from housing=4 (anti-density bloc: Millett, Muhlestein, McCandless)
