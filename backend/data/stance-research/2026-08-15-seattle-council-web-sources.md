# Seattle city offices — stance re-research from web sources (2026-08-15)

**Why this exists.** The Task 10 pass (migs 1754 + 1759) searched **one source class**: Legistar
roll-call and ordinance PDFs. All 13 source URLs on its 11 rows were Legistar attachments. For
Seattle's 11 city offices that produced **2 rows on 1 of 11 officials** (Strauss). The operator
called it: "blank" there meant *no roll-call-provable chair*, not *no evidenced position*.

This pass runs the standard web-source-first method over the same officials. Result: **42 rows across
11 of 11 officials**, plus 5 documented blanks.

## The source class that changed the outcome

answers to specific policy questions. This is the same shape as the washcodems.org questionnaire that

## Cross-check: the questionnaires were tested against the record

A campaign promise abandoned in office is not a sincere chair-match. The September 2025 comprehensive
plan vote is the sharpest available test, and it **corroborated rather than undercut** the 2023
questionnaires:

- **Saka** said density belongs "along transit corridors and in urban villages" (2023) → in 2025 he
  sought to block docketing of neighborhood-center expansion. Consistent with chair 3.
- **Rivera** said "I do not support abolishing single-family zoning" (2023) → in 2025 she proposed
  cutting the Bryant and Wedgwood centers roughly in half. Consistent with chair 2.
- **Rinck** promised parking reform and more centers (2025) → proposed restoring 8 growth centers and
  parking reform in the same year. Consistent with chair 4.
- **Strauss** sits at `local-environment`=3 from Ord. 126821 → in 2025 he **opposed** the strict tree
  preservation amendment. Independent corroboration of an existing row from a different source class.

## 🔑 Findings worth carrying to other cities

1. **`housing` chair 2 is REACHABLE at city level and was ruled unreachable at state level.** The WA
   legislative sweep found chair 2 unreachable because it needs rent caps **and** inclusionary units
   **and** public funding, and zero inclusionary-zoning instruments exist in the state corpus.
   Seattle's **MHA is inclusionary zoning**, so a city official who supports rent stabilization, MHA
   on middle housing, and dedicated affordable-housing funds holds all three limbs. Dionne Foster
   does. *Unreachability is a property of a chair meeting a CORPUS, not of the chair text alone.*
2. **The `taxes` destination rule discriminated three officials who all "support progressive
   revenue".** Wilson lists new taxes to fund her priorities → chair 1. Foster frames local
   progressive revenue as the answer to *federal cuts* — preserving what exists → chair 2. Juarez
   voted against JumpStart → blank. Identical vocabulary, three different chairs.
3. **A vague candidate yields few rows, and that is the method working.** Saka's questionnaire is
   mostly non-answers ("collaboration and accountability", "a balanced and equitable approach"). He
   gets 2 rows. The method did not manufacture coverage from a record that has none.

## Documented blanks (researched, ladder cannot reach)

- **Juarez / `taxes`** — one of only two councilmembers to vote **against** the 2020 JumpStart payroll
  tax. That refutes chairs 1 and 2, but a single No vote states no alternative, so chairs 3–5 are
  unevidenced. Direction is not a chair.
- **Kettle / `transportation-priorities`** — his 2026 amendment cuts the transit measure from 0.3% to
  0.2% and halts city-funded bus service growth, reserving 0.1% for "any emergent transportation
  investment". The reporting itself notes his intentions are vague, and chair 3 versus chair 4 turns
  on what he would fund instead. Not determinable.
- **Hollingsworth / `residential-zoning`** — her 2023 questionnaire preferred Comp Plan "Alternative
  5, as it allows the most amount of housing"; as 2025 land use chair she presided over a council that
  **punted on housing expansion** and she voted for the strict tree amendment. Her individual votes on
  the density amendments were not obtained. Under-determined between the stated position and the
  record — do not seat either without the roll call.
- **Lin / `local-environment`** — his position is "trees **and** housing": add canopy in
  rights-of-way and public space, remove tree-retention burdens from private housing development. No
  chair describes adding public green space while reducing private-lot preservation duties. Ladder
  gap, logged.
- **Evans / `judicial-police-accountability`** — the ladder asks whether the office defends employees
  or holds them accountable. Her answer is about *prevention* (expanding precinct liaison attorneys to
  reduce claims). It does not answer the defend-versus-accountability question.
