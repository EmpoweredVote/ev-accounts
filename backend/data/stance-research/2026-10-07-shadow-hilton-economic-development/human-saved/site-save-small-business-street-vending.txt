A CALIFORDABLE PLAN TO RESTORE LOCAL CONTROL OVER STREET VENDING
Stop the street vendor war on small business and abolish the state sales tax on restaurant meals.

California's small businesses are being punished for doing the right thing.

Restaurants and local retailers pay rent, wages, taxes, insurance, utility bills and permit fees. They pass inspections and follow health and safety rules. But a street vendor selling the same products can set up nearby without those costs. If the vendor is operating illegally, the consequence may be little more than an administrative fine.

Once again, Sacramento rewards people who break the rules and punishes people who follow them. Even worse, the people following the rules are paying for a government that refuses to enforce them.

There must be a statewide ban on street vendors that directly compete with brick and mortar businesses. Enough is enough: it is time for the government to be on the side of the hard-working, law-abiding majority. It is time to save small business in California.

THE PROBLEM

In 2018, Sacramento passed SB 946 and took street-vending policy away from local communities.

The law applies to charter cities as well as general-law cities. It prevents cities from limiting the number or location of vendors unless they can prove an objective health, safety or welfare concern. It specifically says that harm to existing brick and mortar businesses does not count. Even repeat violations are generally subject only to capped administrative fines.

SB 972 later downgraded violations of food-safety laws by sidewalk vendors and compact mobile food operations to administrative offenses. Cities are left to deal with the consequences, but Sacramento will not let them use the tools needed to solve the problem.

Restaurants are already fighting to survive. The Los Angeles Times counted more than 100 notable restaurant closures in Los Angeles in 2024 and more than 100 again in 2025. Federal figures show that California restaurants and other eating places lost more than 28,000 jobs across those two years. Street vending is one of many pressures on restaurants. Sacramento has made a brutal business environment worse by protecting direct competitors that do not carry the same costs.

BECERRA CHOSE STREET VENDORS OVER SMALL BUSINESS

Xavier Becerra has made his choice. Speaking to NALEO, he said that you do not get “real street tacos” from a long-standing restaurant, but from the guy who makes them in his little cart.

That restaurant pays rent, wages, taxes, insurance and permit fees. Becerra told its customers to go somewhere else. He chose street vendors over the legal restaurants that create jobs and follow the rules. Steve Hilton, who as a small business owner started and ran restaurants himself, will stand with small business.

THE PLAN
1. BAN UNFAIR COMPETITION WITH BRICK AND MORTAR BUSINESSES

Steve Hilton will send the Legislature a bill banning street vendors from directly competing with brick and mortar businesses. The law will establish a protected buffer around restaurants and retailers when a vendor sells substantially similar food or merchandise. Cities will be free to create larger protected areas or prohibit street vending altogether.

Street vending may continue outside those protected areas where local rules allow it. The statewide rule is simple: street vendors will no longer be allowed to set up next to an existing business and steal its customers while avoiding the costs that real small businesses are required to pay.

2. REPEAL SACRAMENTO'S RESTRICTIONS AND RESTORE LOCAL CONTROL

The same bill will repeal Chapter 6.2 of the Government Code, created by SB 946, and the related restrictions on local enforcement. Cities will once again be able to regulate the number, location and operating hours of vendors and consider the harm done to existing businesses.

Cities will be able to require permits, revoke them, impose meaningful penalties and impound equipment when someone repeatedly violates the law or continues operating without the required permits.

3. ENFORCE EXISTING LAWS NOW

While the bill moves forward, Steve will direct state agencies to enforce existing tax, food-safety, labor and business laws. His administration will also support charter cities that challenge Sacramento's restrictions in court on the grounds that managing local streets and sidewalks is a municipal responsibility.

4. TAKE IT TO THE VOTERS IF NECESSARY

If the Legislature refuses to act, Steve will lead a statewide ballot initiative to Save Small Business in California, impose the ban on unfair competition, repeal Sacramento's restrictions and restore local enforcement power.

The Sacramento politicians created this mess. If they refuse to fix it, California voters will.

5. ABOLISH THE STATE SALES TAX ON RESTAURANT MEALS

Steve will abolish the state sales tax on restaurant meals. That means removing the 3.9375 percentage points that currently go to the state General Fund while leaving local sales taxes and dedicated local revenue in place. Californians will save nearly $4 on every $100 they spend at restaurants.

Federal household spending data indicate that the average Western household spends $4,708 a year on food away from home. At that level, Steve's tax cut would save the average household about $185 a year, or $15 a month.

The California Department of Tax and Fee Administration reported $105.8 billion in taxable transactions at full-service and limited-service eating places in 2025. Based on those sales, abolishing the state General Fund portion of the tax will cost approximately $4.2 billion a year.

We will account for that cost as part of Steve's broader Califordable agenda. Operation Zero Waste will cut spending elsewhere across state government to offset this and other Califordable tax relief. We will not raise another tax to pay for it.

A BETTER WAY FORWARD

California should be on the side of people who work hard, create jobs and follow the law. Today, those people pay the bills while Sacramento protects competitors who avoid them. Same old story under Democrat rule: the people who do the right thing are punished, those who do the wrong thing are rewarded.

We will end the street vendor war on small business, abolish the state sales tax on restaurant meals and put government back on the side of hard-working, law-abiding Californians, to help save small business and lower costs for Californians.
