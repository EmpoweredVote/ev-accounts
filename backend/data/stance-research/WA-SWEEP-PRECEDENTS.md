# WA sweep — seating precedents

Every chair decision made in the Washington 147-legislator sweep, written so a later session can
decide the same way without re-asking the operator. Each entry gives the **ruling**, the **evidence
that satisfies it**, and the **migration that set it**.

Operator ratified everything marked ✅ on 2026-08-15. Where the operator was asked and chose, that is
recorded — those are the ones not to relitigate.

---

## 0. The standing method (✅ operator-approved)

**Cohorts are allowed, with a per-member screen first.** Read one instrument properly, then seat
everyone who sponsored it — but before writing, screen every member for an instrument of their OWN
that would evidence a *different* chair on the same ladder. Blanket writes bury stronger records.

**The cohort rule, stated precisely.** The corpus rule is that *one instrument* cannot seat its
co-sponsors in different chairs. It does **not** say a member may not hold a stronger instrument of
their own. So the pattern is: instrument A sets the floor for all its sponsors; instrument B raises
it for the subset who also signed B. This has now happened three times — migrations 1766, 1768, 1769.

**Co-sponsorship counts as much as authorship.** A prime sponsor and a co-sponsor of the same bill
get the same chair; only the reasoning text differs ("Prime sponsor of" / "Co-sponsor of").

**The screen has changed an outcome once in six cohorts** (1766). Run it anyway; the one time it
fires is the whole point.

## 1. Direction is not a chair — and the fallback is BLANK, not the next chair along

The single most important precedent, and the one most likely to be eroded by a fresh session wanting
coverage.

When a person's documented position is *broader* than the neighbouring chair, that chair does not
describe a softer version of their view — it describes a **different** view. Seating them there is
wrong, and "least extreme option the reasoning supports" is an explicitly forbidden tiebreaker.

- ✅ **abortion (mig 1767).** SJR 8204 makes abortion a fundamental right with **no gestational
  limit**. That refutes chairs 2–5 outright — every one sets a limit these members reject. Chair 2
  ("through the second trimester") is not a weaker version of their position. So 18 senators were
  **blanked** rather than parked at chair 2, on the operator's explicit choice.

## 2. `taxes`

### ch1 vs ch2 — the DESTINATION of the revenue decides, never the adverb ✅
Set by mig 1759 (Mosqueda = 1), applied in migs 1762 (Fitzgibbon = 2) and 1770 (26 senators = 2).
Bills do not characterise
their own magnitude, so "significantly" vs "moderately" is unusable.

- Revenue funds **new** investment → **chair 1**.
- Revenue **preserves existing services** or offsets other tax cuts → **chair 2**.
- Fitzgibbon: HB 2724 §1(6) says the intent is to "maintain and preserve essential governmental
  services" and §202 spends it offsetting sales and B&O relief → chair 2, despite a 9.9% new tax.

### An intent section's ASPIRATIONS are not a destination ✅
Set by mig 1770 (ESSB 6346, the enacted millionaires' tax, 26 senators at chair 2).

The destination test above asks where the revenue goes. Answer it from the **operative** sections —
§202 "distribution of tax revenues" and the parts that spend — not from §1. ESSB 6346's §1 adds three
chair-1-sounding promises the House companion lacked, and **not one has an operative section** in 110
pages: breakfast and lunch "for all children served without charge" (the standalone free-meals bill,
SB 5352, died), a "city and county fiscal health account" the legislature "intends to create", and
"increase state funding for K-12 education". Parts IX–XI are titled TAX RELIEF. The only new
dedicated money is §202(2)'s five percent to the pre-existing fair start for kids account, from 2029.
Reading §1 alone seats 26 senators at chair 1 on programmes that do not exist.

### When a member's OWN record states both destinations, the cohort instrument governs ✅
Also mig 1770, and the first time the screen found a genuine conflict *inside* one member's record.

Two of the 26 primary-sponsored a tax on large companies dedicated to a **new** programme — chair 1's
description exactly: C. Wilson's SB 5799 (0.4% B&O surtax on social media platforms creating the
youth behavioral health account) and Saldaña's SB 5638 (excise tax on excess hospital executive pay
to "increase Washingtonians' access to health care"). Each also authored a **larger** instrument
stating the opposite destination in its own words — Wilson's enacted SB 5813 funds "ongoing support"
of K-12, early learning and higher education; Saldaña's SB 5796 says "maintain and preserve essential
services" verbatim. Both stayed at chair 2.
🔑 This is not the forbidden "least extreme option" tiebreaker, and the difference matters: chair 2 is
**positively evidenced** for all 26 by §1(6) of the act each of them sponsored. The rule is that a
member whose own record documents both destinations has not been discriminated by the ladder, so the
instrument they signed governs. If a later reader disagrees, those are the two rows to move.

### ch4 vs ch5 — BLANK unless an instrument states what happens to services ✅
Set by mig 1764 (Orcutt). Both chairs carry a consequence clause about public services; tax-cut bills
state one only rarely.

- Ed Orcutt: Finance ranking member, **eight** primary tax bills including cutting the state sales
  tax 6.5%→6.0%, and still **blanked**. No instrument pairs a cut with a service reduction, there is
  no expenditure-limit bill in 69 substantive sponsorships, and one of his tax bills *restores* a
  low-income credit.
- ⚠ Known consequence: the cut side of this ladder may be unreachable for legislators generally,
  which would bias the corpus by party. Logged in the trouble-spot file; not yet solved.

## 3. `local-immigration`

### ch4 vs ch5 = PERMISSIVE vs DIRECTIVE ✅
Set by mig 1766. The cleanest discriminator found in the sweep; reuse it.

- Bills whose operative verb is "**may**" → **chair 4** ("honor ICE detainers and share information
  proactively"). SB 6264 deletes the word "not" from three prohibitions; SB 5818 lets DOC transfer
  people on a detainer.
- Bills that **mandate** → **chair 5** ("direct local police to actively assist … and support federal
  detention operations"). SB 5002 bans sanctuary policies, says agencies "**shall** use best efforts
  to support the enforcement of federal immigration law", and requires every county jail to contract
  with a federal immigration agency.

### Scope: this ladder IS used for state legislators ✅
The generated reference warns it is "usually the WRONG choice for a … statewide official". Overridden
deliberately in 1766: these bills legislate on precisely what the ladder asks — whether local law
enforcement cooperates with federal immigration enforcement.
⚠ **Inconsistency to resolve:** `transportation-priorities` was NOT used for state legislators on the
same kind of reasoning (its chairs say "communitywide"/"local transportation policy"). One of these
two decisions should probably change. Flagged, not decided.

The general `immigration` ladder was considered and **rejected** for these bills — it asks about
immigration levels and public services, which none of them changes.

## 4. `rent-regulation`

### Chairs 2–5 turn on rent-control SCOPE only ✅
Set by mig 1763. Eviction and just-cause bills **cannot discriminate** among chairs 2–5; they bear
only on chair 1's third clause ("just-cause eviction requirements"). HB 1915 and HB 2699 were both
read in full and both correctly failed to move anyone.

### ch2 chosen over ch3 for EHB 1217 ✅ (operator-approved)
Neither fits cleanly. Chair 2 says "strengthen **existing** rent stabilization" and Washington had
none before the act; chair 3 says "**maintain** current tenant protections", which a first-ever
statewide rent cap plainly does not do. Operator chose **2**, because "maintain" is the more serious
mismatch. §102's exemptions (units under 12 years old, public/nonprofit/LIHTC, owner-occupied)
**refute chair 1**, which requires *all* rental units.

## 5. `climate-change`

### ch2 vs ch3 = a phase-out DATE ✅
Set by mig 1762. Chair 2 requires phasing out fossil fuels **by 2030**. Cap-and-invest, clean-energy
deployment, coal-plant exemptions removed, HFC rules — however aggressive — are **chair 3** absent a
date. Eleven sponsors of HB 2367 seated at 3; Fitzgibbon's four further CCA bills reinforced it.

## 6. `civil-rights`

### ch2 = strengthening enforcement; ch1 needs reparations; ch3 refuted by any broadening amendment ✅
Set by mig 1765. SB 5038 broadens hate-crime liability to bias operating "in whole **or in part**".

- Chair 3 ("**maintain** current civil rights laws") is refuted because the act amends the statute to
  broaden liability.
- Chair 1 is refuted by the bill's own §9: the act "does not confer or expand any civil rights …
  beyond those … that exist".
- Chair 1 is additionally **unreachable corpus-wide**: it requires reparations and an anchored search
  of all 3,411 bills finds **zero** reparations instruments.

## 7. `housing`

### ch3 = targeted help; its three mechanisms are the test ✅
Set by mig 1768. Chair 3 names "subsidies for affordable projects, **first-time buyer assistance**,
and easier building permits". HB 1696's "down payment and closing cost assistance" is the middle one
verbatim. 32 seated at 3.

### ch1 = public OWNERSHIP plus non-means-tested availability ✅
HB 1687 defines social housing as "publicly owned in perpetuity by a social housing developer" and
"available to households of any income level". That combination — public ownership, not restricted to
the poorest — is what separates chair 1 from chair 3's targeted help. 10 seated at 1.
⚠ Recorded weakness: HB 1687 is an *enabling* statute, not a construction programme. If a later
reader decides chair 1 requires the state to build at scale, those ten rows are the ones to revisit.

### ch2 is UNREACHABLE ✅
It requires rent caps **and** inclusionary units **and** publicly funded new housing. Rent caps
exist; **zero** inclusionary-zoning instruments exist in the biennium. Do not spend time on it.

## 8. `residential-zoning`

### ch2 vs ch4 = permissive vs directive, again ✅
Set by mig 1769.
- SB 5471 (counties "**may** provide by ordinance" middle housing where single-family is allowed,
  water/sewer required) → **chair 2**, whose parenthetical "(duplexes, accessory units)" is exactly
  middle housing and ADUs. Chairs 1, 3, 5 each refuted from the text.
- Also sponsoring SB 5184 (cities "**may not require** more than 0.5 parking spaces", statewide,
  mandatory) → **chair 4**, whose second mechanism is "reduce parking requirements".
⚠ Recorded weakness: chair 2 also says "strong design review", which SB 5471 lacks. It survives on
its main clause plus refutation of all three neighbours.

## 9. `trans-athletes`

### ch4 chosen despite permissive/mandatory gap ✅ (operator-approved)
Set by mig 1764. HB 1699 says districts "**may** prohibit biologically male students from competing
with and against female students" in female-designated events; chair 4 says "**require**". Operator
chose **4** anyway, because the position the bill enables is chair 4's position, and chair 3 — the
only chair gesturing at discretion — describes *individual case-by-case* judgement, which the bill
does not create. Chair 5 refuted twice (female-designated events only; permits rather than bans).

⚠ Note this cuts the *opposite* way from `local-immigration` ch4/ch5, where permissiveness kept
members at the lower chair. The difference: there, a directive instrument existed to contrast with;
here, no chair describes "let districts decide", so permissiveness had nowhere to go.

## 9a. `public-safety-approach`

### ch4 = an operative increase in officers; ch5 needs a RANKING against other spending ✅
Set by mig 1771 (HB 1435, 23 sponsors — 16 R and 7 D, the first genuinely bipartisan cohort).

- **Chair 4** is "increase police staffing, equipment, and pay". HB 1435 pays 75% of a new officer's
  entry-level salary and benefits, capped at $125,000 per position, and bars grants for anyone
  employed by a Washington agency in the previous 12 months — so it buys *additional* officers, not
  transfers. Abell's companion bills (HB 1436, HB 1896) supply chair 4's deterrence rationale
  verbatim: violent crime at "25-year highs", the state "last in the nation for law enforcement
  officers per capita", funding more officers "with the goal of reducing violent and property crime".
- **Chair 5** needs the police budget to be "the top spending priority **over other services**".
  Nothing in this record ranks police against other spending, and the companion funding bills are
  *permissive local options* adopted "to avoid placing more burden on local governments". Same shape
  as taxes ch4/ch5: the chair carries a comparative clause that ordinary instruments never state.
- ⚠ Recorded weakness: chair 4 is a compound — staffing, equipment **and** pay. The grant may be
  spent on nothing but salaries and benefits, so the equipment limb is unmatched. It survives on its
  main clause plus refutation of all four neighbours, as residential-zoning ch2 did in 1769.

### Scope: this ladder IS used for state legislators ✅
Same ground as `local-immigration` in 1766 — a state appropriation that pays local and tribal agencies
to put officers on the street legislates on exactly what the ladder asks. ⚠ The `transportation-
priorities` inconsistency flagged in 1766 remains open.

## 9b. A chair is refuted by CONTRADICTION, not by incompleteness ✅

Set by mig 1771, and it is the natural limit of precedent 1 above — read the two together, because
they pull in opposite directions and a session that has only one of them will get the other wrong.

Lauren Davis (HB 1498, domestic violence co-responder grants) and Greg Nance (HB 1809, behavioral
health co-response training) both hold instruments that *look* like chairs 2-3 and both stayed at
chair 4:
- chair 2 wants non-violent calls shifted to **unarmed mental health** responders; HB 1498's advocates
  are "summoned **by law enforcement** to the scene" for victim support, so the officer still takes
  the call;
- chair 3 requires **keeping** public safety funding current, which each of them refuted by sponsoring
  HB 1435.
Their position — more officers *and* more co-response — is **broader** than chair 4, and chair 4 is
still true of them, because it claims no exclusivity. Contrast the abortion blanks in 1767, where
chairs 2-5 each **asserted a gestational limit the members rejected**. 🔑 The test is whether the
chair's text says something the record contradicts. If it does, blank. If the chair is merely a
partial picture, seat it.

## 10. Documented blanks — how to write one

A blank is a **context row with sources and NO answer row**. It must:
- open with "Unable to place on this ladder" (or another phrase the ORPHAN_CONTEXT carve-out
  recognises), **and be true** — the carve-out is earned by the row genuinely being unplaceable, not
  by containing the phrase;
- name the instrument that was read and **the specific clause that failed**;
- carry the sources actually fetched.

Purpose: let a later pass distinguish "researched, ladder cannot reach it" from "never researched".
19 exist so far (Orcutt taxes ×1, abortion ×18).

## 11. Topic assignment — vocabulary is not rationale

A bill is on-topic when it answers the ladder's **question**, not when it shares its **words**.
Correctly kept OFF ladders:
- Dhingra's pregnancy-loss dignity, advance-directive, and rape-causing-pregnancy sentencing bills —
  all mention pregnancy; none answers the abortion-access question.
- SB 5558 (comprehensive-plan update **deadlines**) — a Growth Management Act bill that takes no
  position on growth pace, permitting or fees.
- SB 6254 ("Leveraging AI to improve Washington's regulatory **climate**") — neither `ai-regulation`
  nor `climate-change`; it is about using AI inside government.
Correctly moved TO the right ladder:
- HB 1699 ("Defending **equity** in interscholastic sports") — detector filed it under `civil-rights`;
  its operative text is transgender-athlete eligibility → `trans-athletes`.

## 12. Read the enacted text, never the title or the boilerplate

Three confirmed traps, all of which point at a *wrong chair*:
- "Millionaires tax" reads as taxes ch1; its own §1(6) says preserve existing services → ch2.
- HB 2367 ends "**declaring an emergency**" — WA boilerplate for immediate effect, not climate ch1's
  "declare a climate **emergency**".
- HB 2335 §403 ("…support of the state government and its existing public institutions") is the
  standard emergency clause, not a pro-government fiscal position.

## 13. Mechanics that must not regress

- **Identity is the member ID.** The DB holds an *Elizabeth* Fitzgibbon with her own answers; linkage
  matches on **name only, never name+chamber** (Alvarado sponsored from the House, sits in the Senate).
- **Generate migrations from the cache**; do not hand-type rows. Escape apostrophes — `T'wina Nobles`
  failed a whole migration to parse.
- **Copy the ORPHAN_CONTEXT predicate verbatim** from the CI gate. Migration 1735 once shipped a
  guard asserting the *opposite* property and passed green.
- **Guards must check content**, not that an INSERT ran: the chair value, a phrase from the clause the
  chair was read from, and the source URL. On split cohorts, assert **both sides separately** — a bug
  collapsing everyone onto one chair still satisfies a count guard.
- **Verify on row counts** after applying, not on the apply script's "OK".
- **Anchor every keyword.** Five confirmed over-fires: pa**rent**al, pa**rent**ing, re**lease**,
  p**reparation** (made an unreachable chair look reachable), special-education "**inclusionary**
  practices".
