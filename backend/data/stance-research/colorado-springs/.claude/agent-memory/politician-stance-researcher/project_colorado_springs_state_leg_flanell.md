Ava Flanell, HD14 (El Paso County), Republican. **Vacancy committee appointee, not elected** — seated
2025-10-11 after a "do-over" vacancy election (the first, 2025-09-22, was invalidated by the SoS for
skipping the mandatory 10-day notice period). She succeeded Rose Pugliese, who resigned. A gun-store
owner/firearms instructor (Elite Firearms & Training) — the state 28-topic scale has no firearms
topic, so that entire vein of coverage (a completecolorado.com op-ed urging a veto of SB3, a
constitutional-carry bill HB26-1212) is off-scale and was correctly skipped.

**Result: 1/28 seated (climate-change=4).** This is an honest yield for a legislator ~10 months into
her first partial term — not a sourcing failure. leg.colorado.gov shows only 5 prime-sponsored bills
this session; `coloradopolitics.com/?s=%22Ava+Flanell%22+<topic>` (tried against ~10 topic keywords:
taxes, immigration, abortion, energy, housing, education, redistricting, voting, healthcare, gas) and
`completecolorado.com/author/ava-flanell/` returned nothing usable beyond what's below.

**Seated: HB26-1129 (Gas Utility Service), prime sponsor** — bill excludes residential CO2 emissions
from a utility's PUC Clean Heat Plan accounting, repeals the prohibition on utilities incentivizing
NEW gas hookups, and expands safety-project cost recovery. Read the bill TEXT (leg.colorado.gov's own
WebFetch-summarized version, not the title "Gas Utility Service") to get this. Mapped to
`climate-change`=4 ("let market forces drive any transition"), **not** `fossil-fuels` — the
fossil-fuels ladder's five chairs are all about drilling/extraction permits; this bill is downstream
utility/distribution regulation and doesn't share that vocabulary. When a topic's ladder text names a
specific mechanism (drilling permits), a bill about an adjacent-but-different mechanism (utility
service mandates) belongs on the topic whose question is generic enough to cover it
("What priority should climate change receive in energy and economic policy?") rather than the one
with mechanism-specific chair text it doesn't match.

**CMS-tag trap confirmed a second time** ([[project_colorado_springs_state_leg_bradfield]] found the
first instance): the caucus site tags Flanell's name on `/news/house-republicans-deliver-letter-...`
(the PUC electrification-mandate letter), but the letter's own signature block only names Dan Woog.
Fetching once with a generic prompt returned "she is a co-signer" — only a second fetch with an
explicit instruction to distinguish the letter's own sign-off from a page-bottom tag list caught the
error. **Always ask WebFetch to locate the actual signature block before crediting a co-signature.**
Did NOT cite this letter as her position; used it only as source_url_3 background context for why
HB26-1129 exists (the bill legislatively enacts what the letter asked for), not as her own quote.

**Axis-mismatch skips worth remembering for this legislator specifically:**
- HB26-1105 (prime-sponsored, "Discuss Adoption Information with Pregnant Persons") — a waivable
  24-hour pre-abortion adoption-counseling mandate. Doesn't move any of the 5 `abortion` chairs (all
  of which are about legal-framework-by-trimester/exceptions); a counseling requirement is compatible
  with several of them at once. Skip.
- HB26-1060 (Expand Criminal Jurisdiction for Out-of-State Conduct) — jurisdictional reach for
  cross-state harms, not a sentencing-philosophy bill; doesn't move `judicial-criminal-justice`.
- HB26-1334 (Wildfire Resiliency Code Board standards/timelines) — a procedural code-board bill
  (review deadlines, petition process), not a `fossil-fuels` or `climate-change` position either way.
- A Judiciary Committee floor quote opposing HB[26]-1283 (criminalizing employer confiscation of
  immigrant workers' ID docs / bias-crime for threatening ICE reports) — she voted no and is quoted
  questioning "if this is a problem that needs to be addressed." This is a labor/criminal-code bill
  protecting immigrant workers from workplace retaliation, not a `deportation`-priorities or
  `immigration`-legal-framework bill; skip rather than force it onto either ladder.

No usable personal-record source for a freelance campaign issues page exists — she never ran a
contested general and the FAQ confirms an appointment date, so there is no pre-appointment campaign
record to mine (unlike an elected incumbent). `pikespeakbulletin.org` had one hit (a "will look like
the adults" 2026 GOP-optimism piece) with two on-the-record quotes from her, both about being in the
legislative minority/coalition-building — real quotes, but off-axis for every topic, not used.
`socoinsider.com` returned zero for her (confirmed dead, consistent with prior legislators in this
cohort). `koaa.com/search?q=` returned an unusable generic results shell for her specifically (no
per-article dates/URLs surfaced) — unlike Geitner, where it worked well; treat koaa.com search
reliability as per-subject, not uniformly good across this cohort.
