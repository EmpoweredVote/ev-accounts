Colorado Attorney General: Republican Michael Allen
By Allison Sherry
·
May. 29, 2026, 2:10 am
SHARE:
Illustration by Kevin J. Beaty/Denverite
CPR News' Voter guide for Colorado's 2026 Attorney General primary election.
Read the primary voter guide here

Michael Allen believes crime and affordability have cramped the lifestyle for Coloradans in recent years.

Allen, the El Paso County district attorney, is running for attorney general because he said the state needs a change.

On the campaign trail, he talks a lot about how expensive everything is and how higher crime is partly responsible for driving those costs — particularly in the insurance market.

Allen also touts his track record in prosecuting more fentanyl death cases than any other district attorney in the state. He talks about the successful prosecution of the convicted shooter who killed five people and injured 19 at an LGBTQ+ nightclub in 2022, who is now serving a life sentence

Allen, 54, is a Colorado product, graduating from Arvada West High School. He served as an aviation electronics technician in the U.S. Navy and was honorably discharged in 1998. He got his law degree from the University of Kansas and started his career in a prosecutor’s office there before moving back to Colorado. He started his job in Colorado Springs in 2021.

“While politicians focus on political lawsuits and ballooning budgets,” said Allen, in a campaign video taking aim at the current Democrat in the job, Phil Weiser, who has filed more than 50 lawsuits against the Trump administration. “I’ll bring common sense back to the attorney general’s office.”

Hart Van Denburg/CPR News
District Attorney Michael Allen at a press conference, Monday, June 26, 2023.

The candidate responded in writing to the following questions. They are shared verbatim below.

Affordability: Colorado voters cite affordability and high costs as a top concern, from gas and food prices to housing and childcare costs. What policies would you implement to address this?

“Crime is directly linked to the rising cost of living in Colorado. As District Attorney, I launched Colorado’s first motor vehicle theft prosecution task force, and together we drove down motor vehicle theft by over 50 percent.

Auto insurance rates in Colorado are among the most expensive in the United States, due in no small part to motor vehicle theft. Similarly, I started an organized crime unit that has prosecuted organized retail theft rings that have been ravaging all parts of Colorado.

As attorney general, I’ll protect consumers and attack organized criminal networks that drive up our cost of living.”

Democracy: What is the state of our democracy? What is working and what isn’t working? What would you do to address those problems?

“We are on the verge of losing many of the freedoms essential to maintaining our Constitutional Republic.

As attorney general, I’ll continue my work protecting our constitutional rights, while also safeguarding our fundamental right to engage in free, fair, and open elections. 

All people have inherent human dignity and deserve full protection of the law. 

Together, I and my team prosecuted the Club Q shooter and obtained the second-longest prison sentence in Colorado history. I’ll continue to work to ensure Colorado is a state that protects the civil rights of all citizens.”

Immigration: What needs to be done about federal immigration enforcement? Including border security and tactics by federal officers?

“Colorado citizens must be protected from illegal search and seizure. 

We must ensure that all law enforcement activities, local, state and federal, are conducted according to Constitutional requirements.

As attorney general, I’ll help ensure our citizens know their rights, and that law enforcement officers are trained according to modern standards. The attorney general’s office is responsible for creating and implementing training standards for Colorado law enforcement officers. As district attorney, I have unique experience in overseeing law enforcement’s use of force investigations and will work diligently to hold all levels of law enforcement to an appropriately high standard.”
