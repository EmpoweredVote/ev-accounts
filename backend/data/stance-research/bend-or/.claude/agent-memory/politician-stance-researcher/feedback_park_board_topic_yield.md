On an elected park & recreation district board, expect **~1 stance per 1.5 directors**, and expect the
yield to come almost entirely from one place: the recorded split vote on **city property-tax / SDC
exemptions for affordable-housing projects** (they cost the district park revenue).

**Why:** park districts have no legislative record, and their own written summaries are staff-authored
third-person prose that attributes discussion collectively ("Directors discussed…"). The exemption
question is the rare item that forces a named roll call *and* draws out individual rationales, because
directors must choose between housing and their own revenue. On the Bend wave (2026-07-24) that single
issue produced 4 of 4 stances across 3 of 5 directors; the other four topics in scope produced zero.

**How to apply:** open with `grep -iE "exemption|opposed|split vote|failed, 2-2"` across every board
summary you can pull, then work outward to self-authored voters'-pamphlet statements. Do not grind
through packets hoping for a preservation or climate position — go to the fiscal split first.

## Three traps that look scoreable and are not

- **A district Climate *Adaptation* Plan is not `climate-change`.** Extreme-heat/wildfire-smoke
  resilience is a different axis from the chair texts, which are about renewable-energy transition and
  fossil-fuel phase-out. Directors' contributions at these work sessions are questions, not positions.
- **A recreation scholarship / needs-based assistance program is not `childcare`**, even when the staff
  report says the program provides "essential childcare." It is institutional activity adopted
  unanimously; nobody argued the affordability question. The topic scale note warns about this
  explicitly.
- **Voting *for* a tax exemption does not put someone high on `taxes`.** The motive is housing
  affordability, not cutting taxes or shrinking services — it maps to no chair. Only the *opposing*
  side scores, and only when the director paired the vote with a collections-vs-services rationale.
  Scoring the pro-exemption side 4 would be a pure polarity error.

## The counterintuitive one worth expecting

A career conservation figure can land at **`local-environment` = 3, not 1 or 2.** Bend's longtime
conservationist director moved to *approve* streamlined "clear and objective" development standards
through wildlife-habitat overlay zones, because the package preserved the underlying protections while
making them objective. Chair 3 ("consistent standards + reasonable flexibility") is the honest answer.
Résumé is not a stance — read the motion.
