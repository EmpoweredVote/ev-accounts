## The single most important negative finding

Bend-La Pine adopted **Resolution 1985 — "Resolution Affirming Sexual Identity, Gender Identity,
Expression, and Equity for Transgender and Gender-Expansive Students and Staff"** on 2025-02-11, 7-0.
It is tempting to score `trans-athletes` from it. **I read the full resolution text and it does not
mention athletics, sports, teams, or participation eligibility anywhere.** Per hard rule 4 it measures
a different axis than the trans-athletes question, so `trans-athletes` is skipped for all four people.
I separately searched BoardBook for `OSAA`, `athletic eligibility`, `girls sports` — the only hits are
a deleted athletics-transfer regulation and a 2012 PE-credit regulation. The board has never acted on
transgender athletic eligibility.

Resolution 1985 IS used for `civil-rights`, because a governing body committing itself to
"implementing equity-based policies and practices that actively dismantle barriers to inclusion"
while citing ORS 659.850 is a position on what government should do about social inequality. That
caveat (LGBTQ2SIA+ scope, not race) is written into the reasoning of each row so a reviewer sees
exactly what the evidence does and does not cover. LeGrand is the only one of the three with
independent evidence on the *racial* half of the axis (ODE African American/Black Student Success
advisory group; Afrocentric Program Coordinator; "Restorative Justice & Equity" district role).

## Sources that fought back

- **citizenportal.ai** — the article that surfaced Resolution 1985 in search
  (`/articles/2290798/…transgender-equity…`) is now **dead**: Playwright renders "Article not found."
  It is an AI-summary aggregator anyway, so it would not have been quotable. It did earn its keep as a
  pointer.
- **BoardBook minutes are not HTML.** `/Public/Minutes/2413?meeting=N` redirects to
  `/Documents/CustomMinutesForMeeting/2413?meeting=N`, which is an empty shell wrapping an Apryse
  WebViewer iframe — `innerText` yields nothing but chrome. The real artifact is only discoverable
  from the network log. **Reusable pattern:**
  `https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/<GUID>?org=2413`, where `<GUID>` is the href
  fragment on a BoardBook search result. All cited minutes URLs use this form.
- **BoardBook search is URL-addressable**, which makes it far cheaper than clicking:
  `https://meetings.boardbook.org/Search/Index/2413?q=<terms>&i=4&i=8&i=9` (`i` = agenda items /
  documents / meetings). Supports quoted phrases and `OR`.
- **WebFetch cannot read any of these PDFs** — it returns "binary/compressed, cannot extract" every
  time, but *does* save the file to `tool-results/`. Two working follow-ups: the `Read` tool (vision,
  good for scanned pages) or `pdftotext`, which is present at `/mingw64/bin/pdftotext`.
- **`pdftotext -layout` corrupts the voters' pamphlet** — the two-column layout plus letter-spacing
  turns Olson's bullet into `-c losing achievement gaps…` and hyphen-breaks LeGrand's sentences. Run
  **without** `-layout` to get clean single-line paragraphs; that is what made the LeGrand quote
  string-matchable. This is why Olson has no quote despite having on-axis platform language.
- **oregonlegislature.gov testimony is not indexed by name** in DuckDuckGo — site-scoped searches for
  all four names returned zero. Fischer's HB 4070 testimony was found only because it happened to
  surface in a plain-name search.
- Confirmed dead ends from prior waves, re-confirmed here: `centraloregondaily.com` needs the
  `r.jina.ai` proxy (direct fetch rate-limits); Bend Bulletin and Bend Source both need it too.

## Why Fischer and Olson were dropped — Resolution 1985 cannot discriminate chairs 2 vs 3
Reading the **full** resolution rather than the one quoted clause:
1. It grounds itself repeatedly in **existing** statute ("consistent with Oregon state laws
   including ORS 329.025, ORS 339.115, ORS 339.347(3)", ORS 659A.030, ORS 659.850).
2. It carries an explicit **savings clause**: *"This Resolution is intended to be consistent
   with the District's legal obligations under federal and state law. Accordingly, this
   Resolution shall be interpreted as not to violate any requirement of federal or state law."*
3. Three of its four operative clauses are **"Reaffirms"**; only clause 2 is forward-looking,
   and "dismantle barriers to inclusion" carries no enforcement mechanism.

A measure that expressly disclaims exceeding existing law is at least as good a fit for
chair 3 ("maintain current civil rights laws while promoting equal opportunity") as for
chair 2 ("strengthen civil rights enforcement and address systemic discrimination").
Same gate that dropped **Boozell climate-change=2** and **Cummiskey economic-development=1**.

A second reason not to lean on it: the vote was **unanimous 7-0**. Scoring it would mint
`civil-rights=2` for all seven directors from a single symbolic institutional act — the
failure mode that killed Riley's local-environment and Kebler's public-safety rows.

- **Cameron Fischer — civil-rights=2 DROPPED.** Sole evidence is a Yea on that unanimous
  resolution plus the title-wording request. The request is a genuine personal initiative,
  but broadening which identities a resolution *names* is expressive inclusion; it says
  nothing about enforcement strength or systemic remedy. No first-person statement of
  position exists in the record.
- **Shirley Olson — civil-rights=2 DROPPED.** Moving the resolution is real sponsorship and
  the strongest personal act of the three. But her own pamphlet platform — closing
  "achievement gaps for under-represented student groups", "Cultural Liaisons to engage
  families" — is *precisely* chair 3's "promoting equal opportunity". Chair 2's defining
  content (strengthened enforcement, systemic discrimination) appears nowhere in her words.

**Do NOT silently re-add either row.** To justify chair 2 for Fischer or Olson, a reviewer
needs first-person evidence about enforcement or systemic remedy — e.g. a contested vote, a
questionnaire answer, or forum remarks. The Nov 2025+ BoardBook minutes and any future
contested-race pamphlet statement are the places to look.
