## The four substantive items, and why each fails

### 1. BL2026-1498 — drones as first responders → the near miss, and the instructive one

🔴 **THE SCOPING NOTE PREDICTED THIS WOULD BE THE FIRST CHAIR-3/4 EVIDENCE IN NASHVILLE. THAT
PREDICTION WAS MADE FROM THE TITLE AND IT WAS WRONG.** "Use of drones as first responders" sounds
expansionary. The enacted text is **net-restrictive**: it narrows the §13.08.080.E exception so it no
longer covers ongoing use or pilot programs, bounds a DFR program to active-shooter, hostage,
missing-person and ≤10-day declared-emergency deployments, requires surveillance equipment on the
drone to be **disabled unless on the scene of its own call**, and adds a new subsection L **banning
facial recognition** acquisition, integration, use or authorization outright, plus autonomous and
third-party-operated UAVs. Metro Legal's own Agenda Analysis says the same.
→ *Read the enacted text, never the title.* The scope was built on a title and had to be corrected.

He sponsored it (seq 8 of 12) **and** voted Yes on a divided 27-3 third reading. That is real
evidence — it excludes chairs 3, 4 and 5 cleanly.

🔑🔑 **BUT IT DOES NOT SEPARATE CHAIR 1 FROM CHAIR 2, AND THAT IS DEMONSTRABLE RATHER THAN ASSERTED.**
The ordinance is a hybrid: it *bans* facial recognition (chair 1's instrument) while *permitting*
drones under council approval and enumerated circumstances (chair 2's). Both chairs vote for a
tightening bill. The proof is in the sponsor list itself — **Sean Parker, Ginny Welsch, Emily
Benedict and Sandra Sepulveda co-sponsored this same ordinance, and all four are seated at chair 1**
on their own questionnaire words (ev-accounts #495). Sponsorship here marks the restriction side of
the room, not a rung on it.

### 2. BL2026-1391 — data centers, seq 26 of 33
The ordinance reaches `data-centers` and Brenda Gadd was seated at chair 3 from it (#502). Harrell's

⚖ The line used across this pass: a prime or near-lead sequence (≲3) evidences lead involvement; a

### 3. BL2024-427 — Choose How You Move transit referendum, seq 11 of 32
Adopts the transit improvement program and calls the November 2024 referendum. The program's own text
covers "all roadway users, whether they drive, ride transit, walk, roll, or bike" — sidewalks,
signals, service and safety. That sits **between** `transportation-priorities` chair 1 (prioritize
transit, ped and cycling; reduce parking requirements) and chair 2 (invest equally in roads and
multimodal), and he supplies no rationale to break the tie at seq 11 of 32.
⚠ Mayor O'Connell **is** seated at chair 1 off this same program. That is not an inconsistency: his
row rests on his own interview describing what he backs — dedicated transit funding, 24/7 service,
BRT — not on a sponsorship signature.

### 4. RS2024-473 — supporting MNPS's refusal to arm teachers, seq 18 of 37

`education-school-police` is about stationing officers, not arming staff.
