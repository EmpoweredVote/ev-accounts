## 🔴 The two structural gaps

### 1. Criminalization of Homelessness — 9 councilor rows have NO vote, and it is not a search failure

Probed every wording (`camping`, `encampment`, `sleeping`, `trespass`, `Impact Reduction`) against
four different new councilors, **with no date filter**: zero results for all of them.

Portland's public camping restrictions were adopted **2023-06-07 and 2024-05-08** — before this
council was sworn in — and enforcement was **resumed administratively**, not by a council vote. So the
nine councilors who owe this topic have never voted on it.

⚠ Ryan is the exception and legitimately so: both camping votes are in **his own** record from his
2020-2024 term. Ryan's row is recoverable; the other nine are not, from votes.

This is the single largest gap in the cluster (9 of the 11 unrecoverable rows).

## ⚠ Quality caveats — coverage is not the same as good coverage

These matter more than the headline number.

1. 🔴 **Near-unanimity limits what the evidence can say.** The eight Local Immigration Enforcement rows
   would all rest on the *same three unanimous votes*, producing eight near-identical chair-1 rows.
   That is legitimate evidence of each person's position but has **zero power to distinguish them** —
   and writing eight near-identical reasonings is its own quality problem. Same for Affordable Housing
   (10 rows, 4 shared documents).

2. **Two topics rest on a single document each** — Rent Regulation (2025-045) and Novick's
   Environmental Protection (2026-063). One vote is thin ground for a five-point chair.

3. 🔴 **"Adopting an action plan" does not locate someone on a scale.** This is exactly why
   Smith/Homelessness was retired in 1608 rather than repaired. Both remaining Homelessness Response
   rows (Avalos, Novick) rest on the same 2025-499 vote and will hit the same wall.

   ✅ **WORKED 2026-08-07 AND THE PREDICTION HELD — neither row is restorable. No migration written.**
   The scope table above said 2/2 covered; that was the same kind of false positive as School
   Vouchers, and it is now corrected to **0/2**. What was checked:
   - **Votes.** Avalos, Novick *and* Smith have **identical** records — the same four in-term items,
     all Yea: 2025-499 (action plan + KPIs), 2025-058 and 2025-479 (two committee appointments), and
     2025-175 (Portland Street Response as a co-equal branch). Searched homeless / shelter / houseless
     / Safe Rest / day center / outreach / reunification / Joint Office / encampment / Impact Reduction
     / supportive housing / Street Response. Two of the four are pure appointments; one adopts a
     governance framework. **None addresses the thing this topic asks about** — whether the primary
     strategy is housing-first, shelter capacity, or enforcement.
   - ⚠ **2025-175 was NOT reused to chair this topic.** It grounds their Public Safety rows because it
     concerns first-responder structure. Borrowing it here would be the Mejia error — a defensible
     chair resting on evidence that does not speak to the question.
   - **Official pages.** Avalos and Novick's councilor pages score **homeless 0, shelter 0, camping 0**.
   - **WW's genuine Fall 2024 endorsements** (live, 200 — the real articles, not the fabricated
     questionnaires). D1 mentions homelessness **once, about Dunphy**, and camping once, **about
     Smith**. D3's three mentions are one non-positional line about Novick — he "acknowledges that
     local officials have been ineffectual in stemming the tide of homelessness," which is compatible
     with *any* chair — plus two about other candidates. Also an endorsement board's paraphrase, not
     the candidates' own words.

   ⚠ **Side-finding, not acted on**: WW's D1 piece characterises **Loretta Smith**'s 2024 platform as
   not going "much beyond employing more police and enforcing the city's camping ban." That sits in
   mild tension with her Public Safety chair 2 (mig 1609, from her Yea on Portland Street Response as
   a co-equal branch). Her chair was left at 2: an in-office, per-member roll call outranks a 2024
   endorsement board's paraphrase, which is this cluster's founding rule. Flagged for the operator
   rather than acted on unilaterally.

4. 🔴 **CORRECTED 2026-08-07 — this claim was WRONG.** I wrote that 2025-175 (Portland Street Response
   as a co-equal branch) "splits the council… all nine Public Safety rows can be chaired from it with
   genuine variation." It does split the council — but between **Dan Ryan (Nay)** and everyone else,
   and **Ryan is not among the nine**. All nine owed councilors voted **Yea**, so on the decisive vote
   it is unanimous and discriminates nothing.

   Real variation turned up on two *other* documents, found only by widening the search:
   **2026-042** (report on police officer recruitment goals) — Nay: Avalos, Kanal, Morillo, Koyama
   Lane, Green; Yea: Dunphy, Smith, Pirtle-Guiney, Zimmerman. And **2026-069** (appointments to the
   Community Board for Police Accountability) — **Zimmerman alone voted Nay**. Also 2025-160 (police
   union benefits), where **Avalos alone voted Nay**.

   🔑 **The lesson: a vote that "splits the council" splits it between particular people, and the
   split is worthless unless it falls inside the cohort you are chairing.** Check who is actually in
   the set before calling a vote a discriminator.

   Outcome: migration 1609 chaired all nine at **2**, with each reasoning carrying that person's own
   distinguishing votes. See its header for why Zimmerman is 2 and not 3.
