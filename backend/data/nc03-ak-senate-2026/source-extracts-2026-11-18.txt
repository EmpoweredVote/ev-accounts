2. NC-03 — the recount window, from the statute, not from a news summary
================================================================================================
N.C. Gen. Stat. § 163-182.7, "Ordering recounts". Read 2026-11-18.
https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_163/GS_163-182.7.html

  "(c) Mandatory Recounts for Ballot Items Within the Jurisdiction of the State Board of
   Elections. - In a ballot item within the jurisdiction of the State Board of Elections, a
   candidate shall have the right to demand a recount of the votes if the difference between
   the votes for that candidate and the votes for a prevailing candidate are not more than the
   following: (1) For a nonstatewide ballot item, one percent (1%) of the total votes cast in
   the ballot item ... The demand for a recount must be in writing and must be received by the
   State Board of Elections by noon on the second business day after the county canvass."

A U.S. House contest is nonstatewide and within the State Board's jurisdiction, so the threshold
is 1% of 239,614 = 2,396 votes. THE MARGIN IS 258. Murphy was entitled to demand a recount and
the window has closed; press reporting put the deadline at noon on Tuesday 2026-11-17, and AP
called the race at 20:45Z the same day — about four hours after noon Eastern.

🔴 WHAT IS RECORDED AND WHAT IS NOT. That the deadline passed without a demand is NOT asserted
anywhere in CC_0454: no primary source was read that says so. What is asserted is the margin,
the threshold, the concession, and AP's call. The seat is recorded as `called`, never `certified`.

3. Alaska — ranked-choice tabulation and the certification target
================================================================================================
Alaska Division of Elections home page, read 2026-11-18.
https://www.elections.alaska.gov/

  "Ranked choice tabulation starts November 18, for required races."
  "Target date to certify election: November 25"

▶ "FOR REQUIRED RACES" IS THE LOAD-BEARING PHRASE. A ranked-choice contest whose leader clears
50% of first-choice votes is decided in round one and needs no transfers. Peltola clears it on
both denominators (see ak-division-of-elections-2026-11-18.json), so the U.S. Senate contest is
not one of the required races and no later round can change it.

⚠ The same page carries "Department of Law Responds to Lawsuit from Mary Peltola Campaign"
(2026-11-06) and "Superior Court Upholds Division of Elections' Handling of Internal Absentee
Ballot Reports" (2026-11-11). There is active litigation around this election. Nothing read
suggests it disturbs the count recorded here, and nothing here asserts that it does not.
