Submit a Video or Picture Northland leaders see momentum at capitol, Mayor Reinert says Dillon Morello WDIO --> Duluth Mayor Roger Reinert says this year’s Northland lobbying trip to the Minnesota Capitol was one of the most effective he has seen in two decades of public service, crediting strong regional unity and a focused set of priorities. “This really was one of the best I’ve seen,” Reinert said, noting he’s made the trip as a city councilor, a state lawmaker and now mayor. “Kudos to the chamber — it was so well organized. But kudos to the Northland, too. Duluth, our near neighbors, the Range, even folks from Lake County — all speaking with one voice.” Reinert said that unified front matters more as representation continues shifting toward the Twin Cities metro in the legislature. “As the legislature becomes more metro‑centric, having that one Northland voice is impactful,” he said. “That’s something I really saw this year.” The mayor also met directly with Gov. Tim Walz for what he described as a rare block of uninterrupted time. “To get a half hour of one‑on‑one time with the governor — that was a gift on his part,” Reinert said. “We talked about the water treatment plant. We talked about the seawalls for the Lot D project. Both are critical infrastructure.” But Reinert said most of the meeting focused on how regional centers like Duluth serve far more people than live within city limits. “Duluth is providing services that serve multiples of our population,” Reinert said. “The governor said he hears the same thing from Rochester, from Mankato, from Fargo‑Moorhead. We need tools that let local leaders serve regional populations at less expense to their constituents.” Reinert said major funding wins already secured — including money for the Blatnik Bridge replacement and $23.3 million for the Aerial Lift Bridge — helped the Northland delegation sharpen this year’s message. “It allowed us to focus on the water plant and the seawalls,” he said. “We weren’t going through a laundry list of ‘nice‑to‑haves.’ These are critical must‑haves for our region.” He also highlighted partner priorities such as airport improvements, hangars for the 148th Fighter Wing, upgrades at the Lake Superior Zoo and the Union Gospel Mission’s project. “We had hundreds of Northlanders there,” Reinert said. “The expo had record attendance. The breakfast was really well-attended.” Reinert said being away from home helped local leaders connect more deeply. “Many of us joked that we had to go a couple hours away from home to have that time to bond,” he said. “But it worked.” Reinert said the biggest takeaway is how essential regional collaboration will remain as political power continues to concentrate in the metro. “As we lose legislative power through redistricting, speaking together becomes more important,” he said. “This year showed what happens when the Northland shows up unified.” For Related Stories: Duluth St. Louis County --> --> WDIO-DT FCC Public Inspection File WIRT-DT FCC Public Inspection File Contact Us | Public Information FCC Applications WDIO Terms of Use DMCA Notice Hubbard Television Group Privacy Policy Your Privacy Choices Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information Unsubscribe from Newsletter Any person with disabilities who needs help accessing the content of the FCC Public file should contact Vicki Kaping at vkaping@wdio.com or (218) 727-6864 This website is not intended for users located within the European Economic Area. &copy; WDIO-TV, LLC A Hubbard Broadcasting Company WDIO Follow &times; Get our headlines by email Sign up for our newsletter and never miss the local stories that matter most.
