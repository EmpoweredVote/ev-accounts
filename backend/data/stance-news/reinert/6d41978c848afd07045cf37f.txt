Submit a Video or Picture Duluth council reviews proposed property tax levy ahead of deadline By WDIO --> Kyle Aune/WDIO The Duluth City Council is entering the final stretch of budget discussions as it faces a Sept. 30 deadline to adopt a maximum property tax levy for 2027. During a council work session Monday night, city leaders reviewed Mayor Roger Reinert’s proposed levy increase of 2.65%, which would serve as the maximum amount the city could levy next year. Under Minnesota law, cities must set a maximum levy by Sept. 30, though the final levy can be lowered before the budget is adopted by the end of December. Reinert said the proposed increase is lower than the 3.4% rate of inflation over the past year and below last year's levy increase. He also noted it would mark the third consecutive year Duluth has had the lowest property tax levy increase among its peer communities. The proposal comes as the city faces a $5.5 million budget deficit and less than 1% growth in its tax base. Reinert said rising home values and property taxes continue to present affordability challenges for residents. According to city figures, the average home value in Duluth has increased from roughly $172,000 a decade ago to nearly $289,000 today. Despite those challenges, Reinert said the proposed budget maintains city services without eliminating positions or reducing staff. “Within our $118 million general fund levy, we're still delivering on our core city services. There aren't any layoffs. There aren't any staff reductions,” Reinert said. Council members did not publicly debate the budget proposal during Monday's work session. The council will continue reviewing the plan before voting on the maximum levy ahead of the Sept. 30 deadline. City officials emphasized that the city's levy is only one part of a resident's overall property tax bill. Approximately 43% of the bill goes to St. Louis County, 26% goes to local school districts, and the remainder is distributed among other taxing authorities. Reinert also addressed ongoing discussions among councilors regarding a potential code of conduct for City Council meetings. While noting he has not been directly involved in crafting the proposal, the mayor expressed support for efforts to encourage respectful debate and professionalism during meetings. He said council members often face difficult and contentious decisions and that modeling respectful behavior can help set a positive example for the broader community. The city must adopt its final 2027 budget by Dec. 31.
