Credit: MinnPost photo by Winter Keefer Dozens of Endeavor Air flight attendants braved the heat Wednesday during a demonstration at Minneapolis-St. Paul Airport, demanding better pay from parent company Delta, waving signs and chanting, “Hey hey, ho ho, corporate greed has got to go!” The demonstration was spurred by the recent software update glitch that delayed and canceled flights and stranded hundreds of Endeavor flight attendants who were already disgruntled with pay and benefits that they claim are inferior to what Delta employees receive. Talk among flight attendants grew during the disruption, said Cathy Crain, vice president of Local 48 of the Association of Flight Attendants Minneapolis/Cincinnati. She was part of the group protesting outside of MSP Airport Terminal 1. People were telling Crain, “Now’s the time,” and asking for cards of support to sign, she said. Endeavor Air is owned by Delta and serves Delta connection flights, but Endeavor workers say they don’t get the same pay and benefits that regular Delta employees receive. Local Endeavor Air flight attendant Cathy Crain protesting for better wages. Credit: MinnPost photo by Winter Keefer Looking at Delta, Crain notes the company ranked No. 1 for customer satisfaction in a year-long JD Power survey of passengers who traveled on a major North American airline. “You see them as sort of the top echelon of carriers. Then to have that happen and have crew members so impacted by desperate times — desperate times call for desperate measures. Now is the time and the rallying time really was that we need the union,” she said. “This is our time. I really saw a huge momentum shift. I hope that continues to carry through.” Questions for Endeavor Air were directed to Delta, which sent the following statement: “As these flight attendants are employed by Endeavor and represented by AFA, details related to pay and working conditions should be discussed and addressed between Endeavor and AFA.” Since Endeavor is a subsidiary of Delta, the disruption of services trickled down to Endeavor, said Trina Johnson, president of the Local MSP Endeavor Association for Flight Attendants. Several crews were stranded without a hotel and no clarity on when they could get a connection back. Resources for stranded airline workers came through “eventually” and “with pressure,” Johnson said. Trina Johnson, president of local MSP Endeavor AFA, chanting with a crowd of picketing airline workers. Credit: MinnPost photo by Winter Keefer For example, the union worked with Endeavor to get an extended grace period granted for a “not available for play” policy of the airline, which disciplines workers if they aren’t where they’re supposed to be when a flight is preparing to take off. Workers who were stranded due to the technical problems needed to be assured they wouldn’t be reprimanded. There was also a temporary moratorium on travel benefits for pleasure, Johnson said, “so if you were on vacation, good luck trying to get home.” There was a complete embargo on flight benefits starting on the first day of the technology crash, she noted. However, some workers help take care of elderly family members and travel for that, so eventually it was loosened. Now that things are back up and running, the embargo has been lifted. The demonstration goes far beyond the technology issues that impacted workers, said Johnson. Flight attendants are required to be to work an hour before their flight takes off but aren’t paid for that hour. They must be on the aircraft 45 minutes before departure and start boarding 35 minutes before departure, she said. Endeavor Air worker Dawn Sadler picketing outside of MSP Airport Terminal 1 demanding living wage and better treatment by Delta, the subsidiary owner. Credit: MinnPost photo by Winter Keefer “But we are not paid until that door closes, so if you boarded everyone but you sit there for three hours … if you get to a point where you have to take everyone off, you just start all over again. So if you can live off $2 an hour, great, but most people can’t,” Johnson said. Endeavor flight attendant Kaela Berg, a state representative, chanted with her colleagues during the picket. She said she’s spent about 21 years in the labor movement, which led her to becoming a legislator. She currently serves as the vice chair of the House Labor Committee. Just this year, Berg helped spearhead an amendment to the Paid Family and Medical Leave Act so that the law now includes airline workers. As to why she was picketing Wednesday, Berg said: “We’re just highlighting that we do the same job, have the same crazy hours, and leave our families and our homes the same amount of times as Delta Airline flight attendants do, but for a fraction of the pay.” During the technology shutdown, Berg was switched between flights and “running in a bunch of different directions,” though she said she had it better than others. In the worst case scenarios, people were stuck away from their families and not getting paid for that time, she said. Endeavor Air flight attendant and state House Rep. Kaela Berg picketing at a protest for better wages. Credit: MinnPost photo by Winter Keefer “There's no real way to get home. Everybody's scrambling to figure out how we do this. Meanwhile, yes, we're not getting paid,” she said. Flight attendant Laura Ford brought snacks to the rally. She said she wants to make sure her coworkers have something to eat. Just this year, she said she was able to get off of food stamps and knows many newer employees who don’t make a wage high enough to cover food costs. “A lot of people don’t understand we only get paid for maybe 50% of the work we do,” she said. For her next week of work, Ford said she has a 44-hour schedule spanning five days but will only get paid for 28 of those hours. “So that's actually very good. That's a very good trip. The best trip I've had in a long time,” she said. Winter Keefer Winter Keefer is MinnPost's Metro reporter. Follow her on Twitter or email her at wkeefer@minnpost.com .
