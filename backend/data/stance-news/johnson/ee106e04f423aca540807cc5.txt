Sign up WASHINGTON — Minnesota Democrats say they will come to the aid of GOP House Speaker Mike Johnson when the U.S. House considers an effort to oust him next week. After six weeks of threatening to do so, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene said she will finally force a vote to remove Johnson from his post. She is joined by two “Freedom Caucus” members, Reps. Tom Massie of Kentucky and Paul Gozar of Arizona, in her effort to force a vote on a “motion to vacate the chair,” or remove the speaker. To help the embattled speaker, House Democrats plan to support a “motion to table” next week. If it is approved, as expected, the legislation would put aside Greene’s effort and derail a vote that would likely result in Johnson’s removal. Reps. Angie Craig, D-2 nd District, and Betty McCollum, D-4 th District, said they would support a motion to table. “The Freedom Caucus has held the House hostage for this entire session of Congress — it's time to move past extremist brinkmanship and actually work together to govern for the American people,” Craig said in a statement. “If Republicans put forward the motion to vacate and try to oust Speaker Johnson, I will vote to table it.” Meanwhile, Rep. Dean Phillips, D-3 rd District, is leaning toward voting to table the motion to vacate, and Rep. Ilhan Omar, D-5 th District, is undecided. So, it’s possible that all four Minnesota Democrats will help a very conservative GOP leader. Johnson’s leadership job was put into jeopardy because Greene and other far-right members of the U.S. House were angered that the speaker negotiated with Democrats to pass certain bills, including a budget bill and funding for Ukraine. Agreements with Democrats led to the ouster of former Speaker Kevin McCarthy six months ago, which touched off several chaotic weeks in the U.S. House as the GOP sought a candidate to replace him. While Democrats did not help McCarthy, they see good reasons to help Johnson. “While Speaker Johnson and I agree on very few things, he did the right thing in bringing to the House Floor a vote on national security supplemental funding for Ukraine and humanitarian aid for Gaza,” McCollum said. “In doing so, he rejected the MAGA extremists within his conference, who have brought nothing but chaos since they took over the House in January 2023.” Democrats rallied around the embattled GOP speaker after House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., and other Democratic leaders released a joint statement urging them to do so. “We will vote to table Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene’s Motion to Vacate the Chair. If she invokes the motion, it will not succeed,” the Democratic leaders said in a statement. Minnesota Dems take tough vote After Democrats offered House Speaker Mike Johnson a lifeline, the party was angered that the GOP leader forced Democrats to take a tough vote. As part of a GOP-led effort to politicize campus unrest, Johnson held a vote Wednesday on the Antisemitism Awareness Act, legislation that expands the federal definition of antisemitism. The bill was approved 320-91, with Reps. Ilhan Omar, D-5th District, and Betty McCollum, D-4th District, the only Minnesota lawmakers casting “no” votes. Advocates of the legislation said it would empower the federal government to crack down on anti-Israel protests on campuses by codifying a definition of antisemitism that encompasses not just threats against Jews, but also certain criticisms of Israel itself. Rep. Mike Lawler, R-N.Y., the bill’s lead sponsor, said the legislation would give the U.S. Education Department the tools to hold college administrators accountable if they fail to address antisemitism on their campuses. But the legislation prompted a heated debate on the House floor. In a speech before the vote, McCollum said she “condemns antisemitism in all its forms.” But she said the new legislation “would stifle free speech and curtail legitimate criticisms of the Israeli government’s actions.” “This bill violates First Amendment rights to share and debate ideas and express peaceful dissent,” McCollum said. “It is too broad and could lead to colleges and universities banning student groups that aim to provide safe refuge, community, and space to discuss issues that are important to them based on the opinion or statement of one student.” It’s not clear whether the U.S. Senate will take up the Antisemitism Awareness Act. Stauber wins on bill lifting mining ban Rep. Pete Stauber, R-8 th District, had a win this week as the U.S. House approved his bill that would reverse the Biden administration’s mining ban on 225,504 acres of Superior National Forest. That’s something the Iron Range congressman has sought since the ban was imposed in January of last year. The bill, approved by the U.S. House on a largely partisan vote — 210-203 — would also require the re-issuance of every mineral lease in the forest. “Instead of flat-out rejecting any mining project from developing the Duluth Complex located within the Superior National Forest, it is important to allow project-specific reviews that are based on the facts and science to take place,” Stauber said in a statement. Stauber’s bill &#8230; is a giveaway of America’s public lands to a foreign mining company and sets a dangerous precedent for the conservation of America's most sensitive ecosystems,” said Boundary Waters Executive Director Ingrid Lyons. “By attempting to undo Secretary Haaland’s prudent decision to ban sulfide-ore copper mining in the watershed of the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness, (the bill) directly contradicts the robust science, favorable public opinion, and sound legal basis of the Secretary’s decision.” Stauber has also been at loggerheads with Rep. Betty McCollum, D-4 th District, on the issue. McCollum has sponsored legislation that would codify the mining ban. While the GOP-led U.S. House approved Stauber’s bill, it’s not likely the Democratic controlled U.S. Senate will take up the legislation. Your questions and comments I received several comments about a story on the increasing attacks by Rep. Tom Emmer on Rep. Ilhan Omar over campus protests. Here’s what one reader said: “Rep. Omar still walked the high road when she said last week that the demonstrators were legitimately complaining about Israel in Gaza and U.S. response to what many see as the slaughter of Palestinian women and children. Emmer’s complaints about demonstrators being ‘pro-Hamas’ and the Democratic Party as ‘pro-terrorist’ are so overtly unhinged and ridiculous that it projected Emmer mainly dwelling in the social sewer and it lifted Omar head and shoulders above, as a stateswoman deserving of respect and deference. "But for Omar to follow up that sordid exchange with questioning if some people (her political polar opposites) were “pro-genocide” means she headed straight to the same lowly regions more often frequented by the mud-slingers and simple-minded of the right (or left, for that matter).” Please keep your comments, and any questions, coming.
