When Valerie Mack, a tenant at Blaisdell Housing on 2119 Pillsbury Ave. in Minneapolis, moved a trash can in her apartment building, rodents started “flying out.” For someone else, that sight might have been a nasty surprise – but for Mack and other residents at properties owned by Investment Property Group (IPG), it was just the latest in a pattern of mismanagement. On March 13, Mack and other tenants of various IPG properties gathered in front of their building for a press conference. At the conference, tenants, with the support of housing justice organizers from Inquilinxs Unidxs Por Justicia/United Renters for Justice, shared their experiences living in what they say are unsanitary and unsafe buildings. “There was mold growing throughout my ceilings, there were roaches (and) rodents,” said Adrianna Mixon as she described her experience living in an IPG-owned building while pregnant with her daughter. Mixon was able to move to a different property, also owned by IPG. The move, however, did not improve her living conditions. When pest control never showed up and Mixon did not receive any communication about pest control’s arrival, she took matters into her own hands. “There was mold growing throughout my ceilings, there were roaches (and) rodents,” said Adrianna Mixon, left, as she described her experience living in an IPG-owned building while pregnant with her daughter. Credit: MinnPost photo by Deanna Pistono “I have a whole cat in my house right now that doesn’t need to be there, because they told me months ago that there was going to be pest control coming out (to the property),” she said. Mack, who has lived at 2119 Pillsbury for 14 years, has also had to take extra steps to mitigate the poor conditions in her building. “I play security,” said 69-year old Mack. “I walk these hallways. I take pictures. I look at the rodents. I look at dog mess. I look at human feces.” Mack also cleans the garage, where, she added, she pays $50 to park. Valerie Mack: “I walk these hallways. I take pictures. I look at the rodents. I look at dog mess. I look at human feces.” Credit: MinnPost photo by Deanna Pistono Michael Hru, another longtime resident of the property, said when IPG took over management of the building in October 2022, there was a decline in his quality of life. “The Monday after the Thanksgiving of 2024 up until the Monday before Christmas, that door right there (at 2119 Pillsbury Ave.) was open 24 hours a day,” said Hru. “To IPG and whoever manages this, security is not a concern for them. There are people in the hallways, there are people breaking into people’s cars in the garage, they’re sleeping in the laundry rooms.” Michael Hru: “To IPG and whoever manages this, security is not a concern for them.” Credit: MinnPost photo by Deanna Pistono According to Yasmin Isse, another long-time tenant, when residents’ cars were broken into, management told them to move their cars and fix them within 24 hours. Communication between staff and tenants has been lacking or nonexistent during this time, even as residents have brought up their grievances. Isse claimed that people wouldn’t answer her calls, even when she went a whole year without heating. MinnPost also attempted to reach IPG, but our requests for comment were not returned. Part of the issue with communication, said Hru, is the large rate of turnover in management. “There’s been as much turnover in (IPG’s) office as in a fast food restaurant,” Hru said. “Between the people in administration in the office and the maintenance people, there’s been at least 20 of them coming and going. People come here and they’ll work for maybe six weeks and then they’ll leave.” According to Yasmin Isse, a long-time tenant, when residents’ cars were broken into, management told them to move their cars and fix them within 24 hours. Credit: MinnPost photo by Deanna Pistono IPG was also criticized for threatening tenants with eviction from their properties. Inquilinxs Unidxs Por Justicia/United Renters for Justice organizer Farhan Badel said IPG issued more than 20 eviction notices to people living at the property on Feb 13. “Most of the tenants who received these notices are the same tenants who’ve been asking for repairs consistently,” said Badel, adding that in October, tenants signed a petition to meet with management that was ignored. This is not the first time IPG has come under fire for poor practices, or for threatening eviction. In 2023, an investigation under Attorney General Keith Ellison found that 13 apartments owned by IPG in Hennepin County had charged tenants exorbitantly for utilities, including fees above $2,000. Under Minnesota law, Ellison’s office stated, these fees were illegal, as disclosures regarding utility costs were not made to tenants before they were expected to pay these large fees. Later that year, IPG and Ellison came to a temporary agreement , which included the provision that IPG tenants at 11 properties would no longer be threatened with eviction for not paying utility fees. A lawsuit filed against IPG by the attorney general in October 2023 remains pending. Farhan Badel: “Most of the tenants who received these [eviction] notices are the same tenants who’ve been asking for repairs consistently.” Credit: MinnPost photo by Deanna Pistono After the press conference’s initial remarks from selected tenants, tenants and organizers bearing protest signs walked across the street to IPG’s offices at 2200 Blaisdell Avenue. Their goal was to deliver a letter outlining the demands of tenants – for the property owners to address existing repair and maintenance issues in the buildings, stop threatening evictions, set up a 24/7 maintenance line and implement necessary security measures, and to meet with the tenants themselves. The new property manager for Blaisdell Apartments, Tina Johnson, who the tenants had not yet met, greeted them after tenants and organizers made their way to her basement office. Johnson, who had only started working as manager recently, accepted their letter, saying she was ready to work with them. Now, the tenants and organizers wait to hear from IPG. In the letter, tenants gave the property owner a week to respond to their demands. When asked about next steps, Edain Altamirano, organizing director at Inquilinxs Unidxs Por Justicia/United Renters for Justice, said if management refused to speak with them or address concerns that the tenants wanted to move forward with an escrow lawsuit. “Take a walk through the garage,” said Hru. “They haven’t even painted the lines to separate the parking spots since they’ve been here.” “We used to be safe, two years, three years ago,” said Isse. “We pay our rent, we’re nice people, but we need a safe place and we are not safe at all.” Deanna Pistono Deanna Pistono is MinnPost's Race & Health Equity fellow.
