Is it also turning blue? An open state Senate seat pits candidates with two starkly different backgrounds in a district long represented by GOP stalwart Warren Limmer. by Matthew Blake 09/03/2026 10/02/2026 Share this: Email a link to a friend (Opens in new window) Email Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Share on Bluesky (Opens in new window) Bluesky Share on X (Opens in new window) X Print (Opens in new window) Print Credit: Taylor Canas/MinnPost Warren Limmer left Maple Grove Republicans scrambling. After more than 30 years in the state Senate, the GOP lawmaker retired in February, right before the legislative session began. “It was a real shock to the community,” Limmer said in a phone interview. “No one was expecting me to retire.” Limmer said he did minimal recruiting for his replacement to represent District 37, which also represents smaller towns to the west of Maple Grove, including Medina, Corcoran and Greenfield. A handful of Republican lawmakers informally asked state Rep. Kristin Robbins, R-Maple Grove, to vie for the seat. But Robbins was campaigning for governor until May. Into the breach stepped Brady Hoag, a political neophyte with a work history as a corporate headhunter. “Brady was the only candidate who was seeking endorsement,” Limmer said. “He certainly did a lot of campaigning and worked hard to get the attention of delegates.” After winning the party endorsement and primary, Hoag is up against Kristy Janigo, the DFL nominee who works for Hennepin County and touts her experience helping veterans. Maple Grove’s population has soared 50% since 2000 to about 74,000 residents. Related: Democrat Jake Johnson, a teacher, tries to solve the difficult math in a red district The city’s The Shoppes at Arbor Lakes is a time capsule to a pre-Amazon era when midsized Midwestern municipalities had vibrant malls. And national companies including Boston Scientific have recently opened regional hubs in the city. As people and money have poured in, Maple Grove’s politics have, by some indications, moved to the left. District 37 voters went for Kamala Harris and Tim Walz over Donald Trump and JD Vance in the 2024 presidential election. On the other hand, if Limmer ran again he would likely be the Kalshi betting favorite to win. Limmer garnered 55% of the vote in 2022. Here is what to look for in this state Senate race, which is one of a handful that could tilt party control in the Legislature. Who is Kristy Janigo? Janigo spent six years with the North Dakota National Guard and leads with her commitment to veterans. She is the legislative chair for two veterans nonprofits and was a Hennepin County veterans service officer for three years. This includes working with lawmakers like Sen. Aric Putnam, DFL-St. Cloud, on funding to provide free meals to veterans. It also entails helping people make claims with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. She worked on product design for Target and later got a doctorate from the University of Minnesota in sustainable design. Janigo served as a faculty member in the fashion department of Arts Institute International Minnesota, the since shuttered Minneapolis college. She later pivoted into the public sector and since January has been doing site inspections and financial audits of behavioral health providers who get Hennepin County contracts. This experience, Janigo argued, is directly relevant to the pressing issue of Medicaid and child care providers accused of defrauding the state of Minnesota. “I’m literally doing the work on the frontlines to prevent waste and fraud,” she said. If elected state senator, Janigo said she would like to pass a law that would make it easier to liquidate real estate assets from convicted fraudsters. Janigo is also a member of the Maple Grove City Council, where she has so far unsuccessfully pushed for community engagement liaisons with the city’s police department. She did help establish the role of city forester, who oversees Maple Grove’s two tree preservation areas. Janigo floated one day combining these two interests. “I really like public safety so someday maybe I’ll be a natural resources cop or something,” Janigo said. “I’m interested in a lot of things.” State Senate candidate Brady Hoag, left, took part in a debate Aug. 18, 2026, at the Maple Grove Government Center moderated by Mark Steffenson, the city's mayor. Hoag, a Republican, hopes to replace longtime GOP Sen. Warren Limmer, who is retiring. Credit: Taylor Canas/MinnPost Who is Brady Hoag? Hoag has spent his career in the private sector, helping corporations including Wells Fargo recruit executive talent into Minnesota. He currently is a Minnesota-based partner for the Carlson Group, an executive search firm based in Des Moines. Like Janigo, Hoag has spent years in Maple Grove and has a pronounced Minnesota accent. At an August debate in the City Council chambers of Maple Grove’s gleaming City Hall, Hoag declared, “I’m running because I believe our state is not heading in the right direction.” He specifically noted that the state is running a structural deficit, which means spending is now outpacing revenue. (Outgoing Walz administration officials would surely reply that Minnesota, nonetheless, still has a $3 billion or so budget surplus). Hoag also claimed at the debate, held by the North Metro Mayors Association, that Minnesota is a “case study in fraud mismanagement that gets studied in board rooms and business schools across the country.” The line got applause, though Hoag did not elaborate. Hoag declined to comment for this story. A campaign spokesperson said he is staying focused on voter interactions. Troy Olsen, government relations consultant for the North Metro Mayors Association, said Hoag is a bit of a mystery to some District 37 voters, because, unlike Janigo, he has not previously held public office. Limmer expressed confidence in his Republican heir. “He is very knowledgeable of the relationship between government and business,” Limmer said. “And I think I can say that he is very concerned about the job market in the state of Minnesota.” Asked if he differed from Hoag on any issues, Limmer replied, “We really haven’t had that detailed of a discussion about issues, besides taxes and affordability.” Robbins also praised Hoag, stating that she has gotten to know the candidate and his wife, Laura Hoag, over the last few months. “He is a terrific candidate who works hard, connects well with people, and understands what voters in our district want,” Robbins said. What do voters in the district want? Unlike District 3 , whose tourism and mining economies face a profoundly uncertain future, or District 41 east of the Twin Cities, where concern lingers over safe drinking water, there is not an overarching issue bedeviling District 37. The biggest concern expressed by voters I chatted with after the debate were congestion and construction. Hoag said at the debate that he wants funding taken away from public transit projects like the Blue Line extension and put toward highways. Janigo said she would like to see the state adopt recommendations from the I-94 Corridor Coalition , a group representing local governments from Maple Grove west to St. Cloud. She also wants to explore bus rapid transit. Janigo said at the debate “that the issues I hear the most about are affordability and fraud,” which Hoag also mentioned as his two key issues. Besides laws that might help liquidate assets from fraudsters, Janigo also called for more site visits to social service providers and careful implementation of the state's new Office of Inspector General, a hyped office that is unlikely to have jurisdiction over the Department of Human Services. Hoag suggested firing people who knew about the fraud. Hoag’s affordability policies include using available state mechanisms to lower property taxes, and conformity with a temporary federal law that ends taxes on tips and overtime. Janigo mentioned finding ways to lower the cost of health care, something Minnesota is traditionally good at , though some solutions would involve cooperation with the Trump administration.
