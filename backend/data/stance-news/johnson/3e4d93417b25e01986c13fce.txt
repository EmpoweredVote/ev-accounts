Submit a Video or Picture Johnson, Kehr outline contrasting visions in House District 8A forum By WDIO --> Johnson, Kehr outline contrasting visions in House District 8A forum Johnson, Kehr outline contrasting visions in House District 8A forum Candidates seeking to represent Minnesota House District 8A debated government oversight, housing, homelessness, environmental issues and economic development during a public forum Tuesday night in Duluth. Incumbent Democratic Rep. Pete Johnson and Republican challenger Allan Kehr II met at The Garden in Canal Park for a discussion hosted by the Duluth Area Chamber of Commerce and the Duluth News Tribune. A significant portion of the forum focused on state fraud prevention efforts. Johnson pointed to recent actions by lawmakers to strengthen oversight of public funding, including the creation of a state Office of Inspector General. He said improving accountability systems and modernizing government technology will help ensure taxpayer dollars are used appropriately and make investigations more effective. Kehr agreed that fraud remains a major concern but questioned whether the new oversight structure will operate with enough independence. He said the state should prioritize recovering misspent funds and ensuring government watchdogs remain insulated from political influence. The candidates also weighed in on large-scale data center developments, including the proposed Google facility in Hermantown. Johnson said communities deserve greater transparency when such projects are considered. He argued residents should have access to detailed information about potential impacts, including energy consumption, noise, lighting and environmental concerns before decisions are made. Kehr said he is still researching the issue and wants to gather more information before taking a firm position. He acknowledged the importance of understanding both the economic opportunities and potential community impacts associated with major technology investments. Downtown Duluth's future development was another topic of discussion. Johnson described downtown as a vital economic and cultural hub, advocating for strategies that could convert underused commercial spaces into housing. He said successful redevelopment will require cooperation between government agencies, private investors and community organizations. Kehr emphasized a greater role for private-sector groups, faith organizations and families in addressing community challenges, including homelessness. While government can play a role, he said local organizations are often better positioned to deliver effective support services. The candidates also offered differing perspectives on wildfires and climate policy. Drawing on his experience as a firefighter, Johnson said changing environmental conditions have contributed to more severe fire risks and stressed the need for stronger preparation and additional resources for emergency response agencies. He also supported government reimbursement for local fire departments that assist with wildfire response efforts. Kehr called for further study of factors contributing to wildfire activity and said policymakers should also examine forest management practices when considering long-term solutions. In his closing remarks, Johnson highlighted child care as one of the state's most pressing challenges, arguing that accessible child care is essential to supporting workers, families and the broader economy. Kehr thanked organizers and attendees, encouraging continued public engagement in the election process. House District 8A includes portions of Duluth and nearby communities. The race is among several competitive legislative contests that could help determine the balance of power at the Minnesota Capitol following the November election.
