Memo | A recap of a week’s worth of Washington political news, journalism, and opinion, delivered with an eye toward what matters for Minnesota. Sign up WASHINGTON — Much of this city’s political class is on the way, or getting ready to be on the way, to Chicago for the Democratic National Convention that kicks off on Monday. Scores of Minnesota DFLers are also traveling to that city on the shores of Lake Michigan, as are fellow Democrats from all of the nation’s states and territories. I am heading to Chicago, too. The last time I covered a national party convention in Chicago was in 1996, when Bill Clinton and Al Gore were nominated for reelection. The city was welcoming and well prepared to handle the crowd bent on politicking and partying. Like the 1996 convention, the candidates who will be at the top of the Democratic ticket this year, Kamala Harris and Tim Walz, are a foregone conclusion, having been officially nominated in a virtual roll call of delegates last week. But the convention has its purpose, anyway. It will serve to strengthen party unity, Harris and Walz hope, and the candidates will have a valuable platform to reach out to voters in prime-time addresses. Chicago seems to be an ideal city for high-level politicking. It has hosted Republican and Democratic national conventions 25 times, many more than any other city. (Baltimore comes in second with 10 conventions hosted in that city). While the main goal of each convention is the same — to help propel the party’s candidates to victory — every convention is different. Logistics — and the heat — in Houston in 1992 was terrible. But New York’s convention that same year was great, with everything centrally located and with the best post-convention parties. My hotel at the 2012 convention in Tampa, Florida, was right on the beach, which influenced my opinion of that event, even though I did not have much time to take advantage of the beautiful location. My only regret at the 2008 convention in St. Paul is that the Minnesota State Fair was going on at the same time and I was too busy to visit. Walz has a busy week Gov. Tim Walz and Sen. JD Vance have agreed to debate on Oct. 1, locking down a matchup between the vice-presidential candidates. On Wednesday, Walz agreed on X to the debate in CBS’s New York City studios. “See you on October 1, JD,” he wrote. Meanwhile, Vance said he agreed to debate Walz on CNN on Sept. 18 and looked forward to participating in two debates with Walz. “The American people deserve as many debates as possible, which is why President Trump has challenged Kamala to three of them already,” Vance posted on X. “Not only do I accept the CBS debate on October 1st, I accept the CNN debate on September 18th as well. I look forward to seeing you at both!” But it doesn’t look like Walz will be the first vice presidential nominee to agree to two debates. "The debate about debates is over,” the Harris-Walz campaign said in a statement Thursday. “Donald Trump's campaign accepted our proposal for three debates — two presidential and a vice-presidential debate. Assuming Donald Trump actually shows up on September 10 to debate Vice President Harris, then Governor Walz will see JD Vance on October 1 and the American people will have another opportunity to see the vice president and Donald Trump on the debate stage in October.” The statement also said, “The more they play games, the more insecure and unserious Trump and Vance reveal themselves to be to the American people. Those games end now.” The CBS debate will be moderated by “CBS Evening News” anchor Norah O’Donnell and “Face the Nation” host Margaret Brennan. Meanwhile, Walz was dispatched by the Harris-Walz campaign to crisscross the country this week in a dizzying array of fundraisers and events. On Monday, in his first solo event as a vice-presidential candidate, Walz traveled to Los Angeles for the American Federation of State, County and Municipal Employees 46th International Convention. To a very friendly crowd (organized labor lobbied heavily for Walz to be chosen as Harris’ running mate), the governor responded directly for the first time to claims by Donald Trump’s campaign that the governor had exaggerated his military record to suggest he had served in combat when he had not, and left his Army National Guard unit to run for Congress to avoid his unit’s deployment to Iraq. “I am damn proud of my service to this country,” Walz said. “And I firmly believe you should never denigrate another person’s service record.” The next day, Walz delivered a speech at a Harris-Walz fundraiser in Denver and another one in Boston. He spoke of his use of “weird” to describe Trump and Vance in his remarks in Boston. “I don’t name-call people,” the governor said. “This thing about describing the behavior of Donald Trump and his running mate as weird and out of touch with the rest of us, that was an observation, not name calling.” Walz also traveled to Newport, Rhode Island, and Southampton, New York, to raise money for his campaign this week. Klobuchar: A ‘ripoff’ has ended The Biden administration released the newly negotiated prices of 10 widely prescribed medications this week, the results of months of negotiations between Medicare and drug manufacturers. The costly medications, which will now cost a lot less, are prescribed to treat heart failure, blood clots, diabetes, arthritis and Crohn’s disease, among other medical conditions. Eliquis, Xarelto, Januvia, Jardiance, Enbrel, Imbruvica, Entresto and Stellaris. But the newly negotiated prices won’t go into effect until 2026. When they do, Medicare recipients enrolled in its drug coverage, known as “Part D,” will save an estimated $1.5 billion in out-of-pocket costs. Still, Sen. Amy Klobuchar, D-Minn., called the effort to lower some of the cost of pricey medication “a game changer.” For decades, Congress declined to allow Medicare to bargain with the nation’s pharmaceuticals over the price of the drugs it provides the nation’s seniors through Part D coverage. However, President Biden’s Inflation Reduction Act allowed for these negotiations. “It should never have happened in the first place,” Klobuchar said of Congress’ refusal to allow the negotiations during a Thursday press conference. Other provisions of Biden’s law capped Medicare patients’ expenses for insulin and their yearly out-of-pocket drug cost. As a proponent of lowering the cost of pharmaceuticals for all Americans, Klobuchar said that “for years, I have been saying that people are getting ripped off.” She said the reason legislation that would bring down the cost of drugs has struggled to win congressional approval was because pharmaceutical companies “have three lobbyists for every member of Congress.” “But this is ending,” she said. More drug prices will be negotiated by the federal government in the coming years. Biden has called for Medicare to negotiate the prices of 500 drugs over the next decade. In case you missed it: In Tuesday’s election, U.S. Rep. Ilhan Omar won a competitive primary and former NBA player Royce White won another race that will pit him against Sen. Amy Klobuchar in November’s elections. Minnesota’s conservative activists showed their strength , even if they did not win in every attempt to knock off a more moderate Republican. It’s up to former state Sen. Ann Johnson Stewart to keep the Minnesota state Senate in DFL hands . Your questions and comments Quite a few readers continued to write in about the choice of Gov. Tim Walz as Kamala Harris’ running mate. But some were not completely happy. “His national news presence was the deciding factor,” one reader wrote. “However, there are those of us very much against his stand on pro-weed into law! Thus, there is an entire new office at the capitol in charge of drugs, mainly weed. Be prepared to see a lot more combinations of alcohol and weed mixed for highway deaths.” Another reader weighed in on the question of who would play Walz on Saturday Night Live. “The person who can play Tim Walz on SNL doesn't have to look like Walz, but has to capture his joy and energy,” the reader wrote. “In a CNN interview, Al Franken said Chris Farley, had he still been with us, would have been a good choice. Among present SNL cast members, my choice would be Bowen Yang.” Please keep your comments, and any questions, coming. I’ll try my best to respond. Please contact me at aradelat@minnpost.com . Ana Radelat Ana Radelat is MinnPost's Washington, D.C. correspondent. You can reach her at aradelat@minnpost.com or follow her on Twitter at @radelat .
