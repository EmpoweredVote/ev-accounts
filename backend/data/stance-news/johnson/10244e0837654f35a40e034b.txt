Memo | A recap of a week’s worth of Washington political news, journalism, and opinion, delivered with an eye toward what matters for Minnesota. Sign up WASHINGTON – Another contentious week in the nation’s capital featured a bizarre cabinet meeting in which President Donald Trump’s top officials seemed more keen on heaping praise on their boss than in governing. Not to say the meeting did not have eyebrow-raising moments. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth defended himself against allegations he violated the U.S. Code of Military Conduct by ordering a strike against two survivors of a U.S. attack on a Venezuelan boat. Hegseth said he authorized and watched the initial attacks but did not watch the second round. Meanwhile, Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem alleged there was rampant fraud in the program for immigrant visas in Minnesota, without providing any proof, and blamed Gov. Tim Walz. "You told me to look into Minnesota and their fraud on visas and their programs — 50% of them are fraudulent, which means that that wacko Gov. Walz either is an idiot or he did it on purpose," Noem told Trump, who at times during the more than two-hour meeting seemed to nod off. Related: Federal authorities plan operation in Minnesota focusing on Somali immigrants, AP source says Yet Trump was animated at the end of the meeting when he attacked Walz and Rep. Ilhan Omar, D-5th District , calling her and Minnesota’s Somalis “garbage.” That racist rant was followed by reports the Trump administration would follow up with stepped-up deportation efforts in the Twin Cities, where most Minnesota Somalis reside, using “strike teams” of federal agents from across the nation. Trump followed up on his derogatory comments about Minnesota’s Somali community by issuing an order to stop taking immigration applications from people from Somalia and 18 other nations, including Afghanistan, Burma, Burundi, Chad, Cuba, the Republic of the Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Haiti, Iran, Laos, Libya, Sierra Leone, Sudan, Togo, Turkmenistan, Venezuela, and Yemen. Federal immigration authorities also announced a hold on “all pending asylum applications, regardless of the alien’s country of nationality.” Trump said the crackdown was needed because a national from Afghanistan, who had served the CIA while in his nation, had shot two National Guard members, one of them fatally, about two blocks from the White House. During the cabinet meeting, Trump blasted Democrats in general for complaining about inflation, saying his administration isn’t getting the credit it deserved for what he insists are falling prices. “The word affordability is a con job by the Democrats,” Trump said. “The word affordability is a Democrat scam." Marching toward a health care cliff While Trump maintains “affordability” isn’t a thing, affordability in health care will consume Congress next week. A vote is expected in the Senate to extend Affordable Care Act subsidies that help millions of Americans, including 90,000 in Minnesota, purchase health insurance. Those expanded subsidies, put in place during the pandemic, will expire on Dec. 31 and were the reason most Democrats would not support the reopening of the federal government after a 43-day shutdown without their renewal. Senate Majority Leader John Thune, R-S.D., promised a vote on the subsidies before mid-December, which broke the logjam. But there is no clear path to the renewal of those subsidies, whose expiration will result in sharp increases in health care premiums that will leave millions of Americans uninsured. Senate Democrats are expected to insist on a three-year extension of the subsidies. Those Democrats need 13 GOP votes to succeed and that’s considered highly unlikely. But most Republicans in the Senate either don’t want to extend the subsidies or want them pared down. Other Republicans also want to rope off abortion services from subsidized health plans and/or seek new regulations to fight “fraud” in state ACA exchanges. Minnesota’s exchange is known as MNSure. The effort to extend the ACA subsidies appears even bleaker in the House. Although Trump seemed to support a limited extension of the subsidies last week, that idea was scuttled by House Speaker Mike Johnson. On Thursday, a group of 35 House Republicans and Democrats released a plan to scale back and extend the ACA subsidies for two years. None of the 15 House Republicans who have signed on to that effort are from Minnesota. Neither has any Minnesota Democrat. In any case, the bipartisan plan is considered a long-shot effort since Johnson insists on the support of a majority of GOP House lawmakers for a bill before he would put it on the floor. There’s also an effort underway to secure a majority of signatures of House lawmakers on a “discharge” petition that would force a vote on a three-year extension of subsidies. That maneuver rarely succeeds but was used successfully to force a vote on the release of Justice Department files on the disgraced financier Jeffrey Epstein. Republicans may propose their own plans next week as alternatives to renewing the enhanced subsidies. They largely focus on promoting health savings accounts and have been derided by Democrats as a “concept of a plan,” which Trump gave as a response to a debate question on his proposal to reform health care. Related: Minnesota’s Affordable Care Act marketplace is about to become unaffordable. What happened? The prospect of skyrocketing premiums for many Americans holds political peril for GOP lawmakers in next year’s midterm elections, especially those in swing districts. A Kaiser Family Foundation (KFF) survey released Thursday found that more than eight in 10 (84%) enrollees in ACA plans — including nearly all Democrats and about seven in 10 Republicans — say Congress should extend the tax credits. If the tax credits are allowed to expire, most enrollees who want to see the credits extended think either Trump (41%) or congressional Republicans (35%) deserve most of the blame, the KFF survey said. KFF also said about two-thirds of ACA enrollees say they have either “not much” or “no confidence” in Trump and congressional Republicans to address health care costs for people like them. In other news: ▪️ We shared an Associated Press story about a federal plan to ramp up immigration enforcement in the Twin Cities Somali diaspora . The move comes as President Trump doubled down on his criticism of Minnesota’s sizable Somali community. ▪️ In light of that federal crackdown, we also ran an AP backgrounder on the turbulent history of Somalia, including how the Twin Cities came to have the largest Somali population in the country . ▪️ Meanwhile, Brian Martucci reported on a multistate lawsuit , joined by Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison, over changes in federal HUD funding that many people use to cover housing costs. ▪️ And we looked at a lesser-known casualty of the pending federal ban on products with hemp-derived THC: many of the CBD products that Minnesotans use to treat anxiety and other ailments. This and that A reader weighed in on the high cost of health care with a shoutout to MinnesotaCare, a state public health insurance program that provides low-cost, comprehensive coverage for Minnesotans with lower incomes who do not qualify for Medical Assistance (Medicaid) or can’t access affordable employer-sponsored insurance. “Everyone agrees, no matter how much they make, health insurance is too expensive. It's the perfect bipartisan issue,” the reader wrote. “And MinnesotaCare is the perfect solution. The cost is on a sliding scale. And it's more than a concept of a plan, it's a proven-effective plan.” Please keep your comments, and any questions, coming. I’ll try my best to respond. Please contact me at aradelat@minnpost.com .
