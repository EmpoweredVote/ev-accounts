Representative Liish Kozlowski, Representative Pete Johnson and Senator Jen McEwen spoke to residents about the recently completed legislative session and answered questions submitted by constituents. A significant portion of the discussion focused on education funding after a resident asked whether "unfunded mandates" from the state were contributing to budget challenges facing local school districts and increasing classroom sizes. Johnson pushed back on the characterization that many recent state policies are unfunded mandates, arguing that several frequently cited programs are funded by the state or provide critical benefits to students and school employees. "Universal school meals, that is something that is funded by the state," Johnson said. He also pointed to state support for school worker unemployment insurance and curriculum training requirements. "When it comes to those unfunded mandates, it's a great buzzword because it makes it sound really, really bad," Johnson said. "But when you talk and break down the things that they are talking about, most of the things that are truly unfunded don't actually cost anything." Johnson noted that special education remains one of the largest financial pressures on school districts, saying federal funding has historically fallen short of promised levels. He also argued that reducing classroom sizes will require additional investment in public education and a broader conversation about tax policy. "If we truly want to have the education system that Minnesota kids deserve, we have to look at that tax structure," Johnson said. Kozlowski echoed the importance of education funding, citing their own experience being raised by an educator and emphasizing the role public schools played in their life. "I wouldn't be here if it weren't for educators," Kozlowski said. They argued that lawmakers have worked to increase investments in special education and compensatory aid while facing ongoing funding challenges at both the state and federal levels. Kozlowski also expressed concern about Duluth's reported school budget shortfall and said governments should fully fund constitutional obligations to public education. McEwen said discussions about school funding often overlook what she described as broader issues of wealth inequality and political priorities. "The money is there," McEwen said. "It is just a question of our political will." The senator highlighted a labor law change passed in 2023 that allows teachers to negotiate class sizes during collective bargaining, something she said was previously excluded from negotiations. Beyond education, lawmakers spent much of the evening reflecting on a legislative session shaped by a narrowly divided Legislature. McEwen noted the Senate operated with a one-vote Democratic-Farmer-Labor majority while the House was governed under a rare 67-67 power-sharing arrangement. Despite those challenges, lawmakers pointed to bipartisan accomplishments, including the passage of a bonding bill and the creation of an Office of Inspector General aimed at improving oversight and addressing fraud concerns in government programs. Johnson said lawmakers also invested nearly $95 million to modernize county systems and strengthen fraud prevention measures. "We passed an OIG bill that's going to be one of the best foundations to help catch and hold those fraudsters accountable," Johnson said. Housing also emerged as a key topic throughout the evening. Kozlowski, who serves as vice chair of the Housing Committee, said addressing affordability and homelessness remains one of their top priorities. "It was really important that we come in and make sure for a home that everybody in Minnesota can afford to live," they said. McEwen said she plans to focus on government transparency in the next session, particularly regarding large development projects such as proposed data centers. She expressed concerns about projects being negotiated behind closed doors before residents are given an opportunity for public input. "I don't think we have enough" transparency, McEwen said. The senator also identified wealth inequality as a central issue she hopes lawmakers will continue addressing in future sessions. The town hall concluded with additional questions on housing assistance, affordable housing for seniors and people with disabilities, child care affordability and health care access.
