Submit a Video or Picture Despite not being on the agenda, data center tensions dominate Hermantown meeting By WDIO --> Hermantown Data Center Public Comments Residents of Hermantown voice concerns or support for the proposed data center. The proposed Google data center continued to dominate public discussion at Tuesdays Hermantown City Council meeting, prompting heated exchanges, calls for city staff resignations, and pleas from both opponents and supporters as the project moves through the review process. Although the data center was not listed as a formal action item, more than two dozen residents used the public comment period to weigh in on the controversial proposal. Tensions rose early when resident Rick Davis criticized the project's proposed location, arguing a hyperscale data center should not be built near residential neighborhoods and environmentally sensitive areas. "This data center is going to destroy our environment, pollute our air, pollute our water, increase noise pollution, destroy our trout streams, destroy our wildlife," Davis said during public comments. Davis was interrupted several times by audience reactions and council leadership, resulting in a five-minute recess after a dispute over speaking time and meeting decorum. Following the break, City Attorney Gunnar Johnson reminded attendees that speakers were expected to direct comments to the council rather than the audience. Mayor Wayne Boucher and council members repeatedly called for order, saying they wanted to hear from all residents while maintaining respectful conduct. The data center proposal has become one of the most divisive issues in recent Hermantown history, drawing concerns from residents about environmental impacts, water usage, infrastructure expansion, and the transparency of discussions that occurred before the project became public. Several speakers accused city leadership of moving too quickly and ignoring community concerns. Candi Mock, a candidate for City Council, urged elected officials to slow the process and consider a moratorium or additional environmental review. "Every step that you take drives a wedge," Mock told council members. "Taking more time with this is a smart move." Others questioned communications between city staff, developers and consulting firms during the project's early stages. Some speakers called for leadership changes within city government, arguing public trust has eroded as details about the proposal have emerged. One resident questioned whether government-to-government discussions with tribal nations were being given enough consideration before future approvals move forward. Another Neighbor told council members residents have repeatedly voiced concerns without seeing meaningful changes in the process. "When people don't feel listened to, emotions run high," they said. "You have the power at any point to bring relief and peace to the people you represent." Not everyone spoke against the project. One Hermantown resident who said he grew up in Duluth and has lived in Hermantown for five years argued the proposal represents an opportunity for economic diversification in a region historically dependent on mining and shipping. "The proposed data center represents exactly the type of diversification we should welcome," the resident told the council. "It would bring substantial private investment, hundreds of construction jobs, many permanent positions and significant growth to the commercial tax base." Supporters also argued the project could help attract new investment to the region while strengthening infrastructure through associated energy and utility improvements. Opponents, however, maintained that the city should pause the process until questions surrounding environmental review, tribal consultation and ongoing litigation are resolved. Several speakers referenced recent comments from state agencies and tribal organizations, urging city leaders to conduct additional analysis before granting future approvals. No formal action on the data center was taken during the meeting. The proposal remains subject to multiple review and permitting processes, including consideration of an Alternative Urban Areawide Review, environmental concerns raised by state agencies, and ongoing legal challenges. As the meeting concluded, one theme remained clear: the debate over the proposed Google data center shows little sign of cooling, and residents on both sides are expected to continue pressing city leaders as the project advances through the approval process.
