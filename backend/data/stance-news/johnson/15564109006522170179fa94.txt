Below are Documenter Glen Johnson's summary and observations from the Minneapolis City Council's Sept. 10 meeting. You can find Glen's full notes here , which include links to the agenda and audio recording. Summary: The committee received a presentation on the mayor’s proposed 2027 budget . Staff presented a budget that addresses a $60 million expected shortfall. Maintaining current service levels would cost $33 million more next year and unbudgeted overtime by the police ($20 million) and fire ($6 million) departments this year added to the gap. The proposed budget would include cuts to departments, including nearly 100 full time equivalent positions, with the property tax levy increasing 11.3% ($61 million). Positions would also be added in this budget: 40 police community safety officers and cadets, 15 firefighters, and 23 contracted Neighborhood Safety positions moved to city staff. The Budget committee will receive presentations from all city departments over the next two months. Council will propose amendments to the budget, which are due before November 18th, with final budget approval on December 15th. The council heard a presentation on workforce optimization from the city auditor. The report was originally presented to the Audit Committee in April , but shared with the Budget committee at the request of the budget chair who stated its information would be relevant when making budget decisions. It compared Minneapolis’ city structure and provided services to benchmark cities to highlight areas to look into for budget savings and improvements. The 11 recommendations included migrating duplicated services fully to the county (property assessment, public health), bringing services into the city structure instead of contracted (Behavioral Crisis Response &#8211; BCR), and increasing risk management beyond a focus on financial risk to include reputational risk and preventing fraud. Observations and follow up questions: Accessibility: Did you face any challenges that made it harder to document the meeting or that may have made it difficult for others to attend? For example: trouble accessing the location, difficulty hearing the discussion, lack of nameplates for elected officials, or the agenda being unclear, disorganized, or incomplete. Auditor’s report on workforce optimization was not included in the agenda. Located the report from the Audit committee in April . The Budget chair asked for a presentation of the findings to the Budget committee. Scene: About how many members of the public attended the meeting? If watching virtually, what was the livestream count (if applicable)? Was anyone protesting outside? Appeared to be mainly staff present, 435 views the day after the meeting. Notable: Do you have any follow up questions or other observations to share? What stood out to you as interesting or confusing? Is there anything you’d like to see reporters look further into? Were there any particularly memorable quotes? Council members had widely varying views on the top priorities in the proposed budget. Some commented on police overspending while others were focused on limiting the property tax levy increase. Related: Minneapolis budget pre-season underway with property taxes at play How to get involved: When is the next meeting for this board/committee? Any upcoming public hearings? Online surveys? The Truth in Taxation public hearing will be December 15th. Other opportunities to comment will be scheduled. The Budget committee will hold additional meetings to hear department budget presentations through late October. The first of these meetings is September 14th at 1:30pm.
