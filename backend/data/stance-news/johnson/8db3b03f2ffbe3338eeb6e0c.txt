Tim Walz and Democrats. by Cleo Krejci 12/09/2025 12/09/2025 Share this: Email a link to a friend (Opens in new window) Email Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Share on Bluesky (Opens in new window) Bluesky Share on X (Opens in new window) X Print (Opens in new window) Print Senate Minority Leader Mark Johnson, R-East Grand Forks, responded to a DFL letter about increasingly divisive political rhetoric with a call for Democrats to condemn comments Gov. Tim Walz had previously made. Here, Sen. Johnson speaks during a news conference at the Minnesota State Capitol Monday, May 19, 2025, in St. Paul, Minn. Credit: Ellen Schmidt/AP Minnesota’s Senate Republican leader said he disagrees with painting a single community with a broad brush but dismissed pressure from Democrats to rebuke President Donald Trump’s racist comments about Somali Americans as “selective partisan outrage.” A Dec. 5 letter signed by 33 state lawmakers in the Senate DFL Caucus challenged their GOP colleagues to “publicly repudiate” the president’s comments that Somali Americans are “garbage” who “contribute nothing” and should “go back to where they came from.” The DFL letter said language like that justifies condemnation specifically because it is a “precursor to violence.” “Minnesota needs leaders who will stand up not only to violence itself, but to the conditions that make violence possible. Silence in the face of dehumanization is not neutrality — it is complicity,” the letter reads. Senate Minority Leader Sen. Mark Johnson, R-East Grand Forks, responded via email to the DFL’s letter on behalf of the Senate GOP Caucus, which shared the email with MinnPost. “Governor Walz has called ICE officers ‘gestapo.’ He said Republicans are ‘Nazis’ and ‘fascists.’ We do not hold a double standard here: Republican leaders yesterday (Dec. 4) said they disagree with any comments that condemn an entire group or community. However, we have yet to hear a Democrat legislator condemn Governor Walz’s comments about fellow Minnesotans,” Sen. Mark Johnson wrote. He described federal agents as “Trump’s modern-day gestapo” during a University of Minnesota commencement speech in May. “We share the concerns you raise about political rhetoric that is dehumanizing and hurtful; the selective partisan outrage is just as concerning,” Johnson wrote. Respectful political rhetoric &#8216;starts at home' “Respectful rhetoric starts at home if we are to address the challenges facing Minnesotans,” Johnson wrote, referencing the state’s budgetary deficit, fraud and rising costs on Minnesotans as issues that need addressing in the upcoming legislative session. “Internal letters that can be used to make headlines are unhelpful to this cause.” State Sen. Julia Coleman, R-Chanhassen, doubled down on the GOP Caucus’ letter in a Dec. 6 Facebook post . Coleman wrote that "painting an entire group of people with the same brush because of the actions or origins of a few is wrong, divisive, and has no place in civil discourse.” “We’ll be happy to sign any joint statement condemning dangerous generalizations the moment you send one to your own party leadership first,” Coleman wrote. “Until then, we’ll assume the concern about ‘harmful rhetoric’ is selective, situational, and reserved exclusively for when the target isn’t a Republican.” (Sen. Jim Abeler, R-Anoka, issued a widely reported letter in which he invited President Trump to meet with him and Somali Americans, saying “‘please do not call them ‘garbage.’”) State Sen. Omar Fateh, D-Minneapolis, called Abeler “the only Republican who has spoken out for the Somali community.” Senate GOP Caucus leaves response to remarks at press conference The response from the Senate GOP Caucus declined to go further to criticize President Trump’s comments than what Republican elected officials during a Dec. 4 press conference on the state budget forecast, where Gov. Walz challenged lawmakers to condemn President Trump’s comments. “In no way do I believe any community is all bad, just like I don’t believe any community is all good,” said Rep. Lisa Demuth, R-Cold Spring and Speaker of the House. “What we need to do is hold the fraudsters in any community accountable for their actions.” Demuth referenced fraud of Minnesota social services funding, which federal prosecutors have said is largely linked to individual Somali Americans . President Trump also made his comments about Somali Americans in Minnesota in the context of that fraud. Speaking after Demuth, Sen. Eric Pratt, R-Prior Lake, said he doesn’t want to condemn any community in its entirety. On Trump’s remarks, he said “it wasn’t said the way that I would have said it.” Related: Minnesota’s immigrant advocates organize and deploy as Trump ramps up political rhetoric, ICE enforcement “But what I will say is I share the president’s frustration at the amount of fraud and corruption that’s effectively going on in this state,” Pratt said. Republicans understand that “you can’t just lump an entire community into one description,” Pratt said. “We all have Somalis that live in our district. They’re our neighbors, our friends.” Demuth’s office declined to elaborate on her comments made during the press conference. The office of the Senate GOP Caucus did not make anyone available for an interview. In response to the Senate GOP Caucus letter, Walz’s office told MinnPost “the President’s attack on Minnesota is reprehensible and should not be difficult for the Senator (Johnson) to condemn.” MinnPost's in-depth, state government coverage goes beyond the legislative session.
