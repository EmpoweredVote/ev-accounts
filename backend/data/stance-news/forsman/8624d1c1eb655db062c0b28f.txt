The Trust Project What is this? DULUTH — City councilors are expected to consider an ordinance on Monday that would place a temporary moratorium on the issuance of local short-term rental licenses. If passed, the moratorium could put new licenses on hold for up to one year, while staff study how best to regulate the growing industry. ADVERTISEMENT Short-term rental licenses have been issued in Duluth since the City Council’s 2012 adoption of a new Unified Development Chapter, opening the door for local property owners to list accommodations via platforms such as those marketing Airbnbs or VRBOs. Many individuals have found it more lucrative to rent out properties on a short-term basis to out-of-town visitors than to lease them for extended periods to residents. Councilors Arik Forsman, Roz Randorf and Tara Swenson sponsored the ordinance, which will be read for a second time Monday night, before it goes to a likely vote. The moratorium would provide city staff to explore several issues, including: Enforcement measures for property owners who operate short-term rentals without proper licensure. How much landlord oversight should be required in close proximity. Whether density restrictions should be put in place to limit the saturation of particularly popular neighborhoods, such as Park Point. Possible new official controls or regulations to be placed on short-term rentals. Improvements to licensing and inspection protocols, including compliance with fire codes. If the ordinance and moratorium are approved, the city would still be authorized to process any pending applications deemed complete and already in the pipeline. City staff also would be authorized to process license renewals for qualified short-term rental properties that are already in operation. Duluth officials have wrestled for years with how best to regulate short-term rental properties, particularly at a time when recent research has revealed a dearth of affordable housing, making worker recruitment a challenge for many local employers. The city has placed a cap on the number of short-term rental licenses that can be issued, with no more than 120 to be allowed in residential neighborhoods, although there are no limits on such rentals in areas zoned as commercial form districts. All told, just over 180 short-term vacation rentals are licensed in the city, although city staff acknowledge many more unlicensed properties are operating below their radar.
