A proposed plan to guide the future of downtown Duluth is on hold after city councilors voted Tuesday night to table the resolution for further review. The Downtown Development Strategy is designed to bring together years of planning efforts into a single action plan aimed at shaping growth in the city’s downtown core. The proposal focuses on four main goals: building more housing, concentrating investment in priority areas, improving and connecting open spaces, and activating downtown through increased use and activity. City officials say the strategy builds on multiple past studies and community-driven initiatives, including the “Imagine Downtown” effort, which gathered extensive public input. During the meeting, councilors emphasized that the plan reflects years of collaboration rather than a new direction. “I share this context so the council and the public understand this is not a new direction, but a codification of years of community input and collaboration,” said Roz Randorf the 3rd District City Councilor. Public comment highlighted both support and concern. One speaker questioned whether the strategy could unintentionally prioritize short-term rental or profit-driven development over long-term housing for residents. “This strategy then could be seen by some as a Trojan horse… for opportunities to make money in lieu of providing housing for primary housing for residents,” the speaker said. Others voiced support, saying the plan provides a necessary framework to move forward with downtown investment. Community leaders noted it aligns with Duluth’s broader comprehensive plan and could help guide funding and development decisions. The discussion also touched on broader challenges facing downtown, including housing affordability, infrastructure needs, and public amenities. Ultimately, council members voted unanimously to table the resolution. The move allows time to convene a “committee of the whole” to further review the plan and gather additional feedback before taking final action. If adopted, the strategy would serve as a roadmap for development decisions and could help the city secure future funding opportunities.
