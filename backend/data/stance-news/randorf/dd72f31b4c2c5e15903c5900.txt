DULUTH — City councilors didn’t deliver the fiscally conservative property tax proposal Monday night that Mayor Roger Reinert initially sought, but they pledged to work with his administration toward a budget that holds the municipal levy in check. Reinert had proposed a 4.13% increase in next year’s levy, with 1.43% of that margin offset by the growing value of Duluth’s property tax base, largely due to new construction. The net effect, if the mayor had his way, would necessitate a net rate increase of about 2.7%. But Tomanek said she would not characterize the upward adjustment as creating “a buffer” or as an effort to provide the council with more "wiggle room.” Rather, she suggested the higher tax cap simply would provide the council with “time to evaluate the budget” in greater detail. Tomanek also thanked 2nd District Councilor Deb DeLuca for suggesting additional language to the amendment, clarifying that the intent is not to rush toward a higher tax rate. “I’m always thinking about balance. How can we balance funding our city, our core services that we’ve heard so much about, the things that we need to support all of our citizens and yet not tax people out of their homes?” Tomanek explained. ADVERTISEMENT The amendment calling for the city to authorize up to an additional 1% in the levy increase, beyond what Reinert had proposed, narrowly passed 5-4. Voting in the majority were councilors Awal, Tomanek, DeLuca, Wendy Durrwachter and Janet Kennedy. Voting in the minority were councilors Arik Forsman, Roz Randorf, Lynn Marie Nephew and Tara Swenson. The resolution could allow for the council to increase the 2026 levy by nearly $2.23 million above what Duluth collected in property taxes this year. Nephew said she trusted city staff and the mayor’s administration had done their work in proposing a lesser levy and warned that a higher tax burden could price people out of housing, including renters, as landlords passed along those costs. Forsman noted that new construction added more than $620,000 to Duluth’s tax base in 2025, with just four projects accounting for 45% of that local growth. He said that fact reflects the importance of making it easier to build housing and bring economic development projects to the city, in light of the hard budget decisions that councilors will continue to confront. Forsman suggested fellow councilors bear in mind the financial importance of further development going forward.
