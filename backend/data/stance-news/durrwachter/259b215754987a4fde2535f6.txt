The Trust Project What is this? DULUTH — City Councilors approved a budget Monday night that modestly exceeded a proposal brought forward by Mayor Roger Reinert in September. The council had previously proposed to tack up to an additional 1% increase onto the mayor’s budget, but revised the increase to three-quarters of that amount. ADVERTISEMENT Councilor Arik Forsman thanked Councilor Azrin Awal for working to ratchet down the request and noted that the city’s overall levy increase of less than 5% still compares favorably to other comparable jurisdictions in the state. “So, I feel we are still being good stewards of taxpayers’ money,” he said. Whereas, city administration initially recommended an increase of just over 4.13%, that will now rise to 4.89%. That equates to an increase of $323,810 on top of the $1.793 million city administration proposed. New construction in Duluth should cover the equivalent of a 1.43% increase, leaving owners of preexisting properties to pick up an increase of 3.46%. The amended budget passed by a 7-2 vote, with councilors Janet Kennedy and Tara Swenson dissenting. Forsman noted that many one-time adjustments the city has made heading into 2026 will not be available as additional savings in the following year, when a more challenging deficit is projected. But Awal praised the changes made to the city administration’s proposed budget, including: ADVERTISEMENT An additional $151,810 to help the fund blight mitigation efforts; $82,000 in supplemental staffing support for the city’s property, parks and libraries division; And $90,000 for city boards and commissions, including additional staff services and direct compensation to members. The city will provide $45,000 for per diem payments to be divvied between members of the city’s numerous boards and commissions. In her role as council personnel chair, Awal said she found many people serving on those bodies “feel like they’re being pushed aside or they’re not appreciated for their efforts.” MORE LOCAL NEWS Photos and video: LSRX 250 locomotive explores the shore 1d ago Park Point tries to shore up dunes by planting beachgrass 1d ago Analysts believe polls show ‘thin margins’ for upcoming elections 2d ago While she acknowledged the per diem payments the city will be able to offer don’t adequately compensate board and commission members for their work, Awal said it helps highlight the city’s appreciation for their efforts. The per diem payments will also be linked to meeting attendance to encourage board and authority members to show up for meetings, helping ensure a working quorum will be present to conduct business. Reinert continued to advocate for the original levy proposal his administrative team brought forward in September. “Tonight’s budget vote has actually grown the projected budget deficit next year from $5.4 million to $5.8 million. And as Councilor Forsman mentioned, we’ve got big work ahead of us next year,” Reinert said. “We face serious structural challenges that for too long have been ignored,” he said. “Our ongoing expenses are roughly three times our revenues.” Reinert suggested the pace of recent property tax increases cannot be sustained into the future. ADVERTISEMENT To illustrate his point, Reinert noted that in 2018, the average price of a Duluth home was about $185,000, and the owner of such a residence paid about $2,000 per year in taxes. Eight years later, the average price of a home had risen to $295,000, sticking the typical local homeowner with an annual tax bill of nearly $4,000. In other words, Reinert said that property taxes on Duluth homes had essentially doubled in eight years. While city taxes account for only about 20% of property taxes, the mayor said: “We have to control what we can control, because that trajectory is not sustainable for our residents and our businesses and cannot continue without serious financial consequences.” This story originally mischaracterized opposition to the city budget vote Monday. Voting against the resolution were Councilors Tara Swenson and Janet Kennedy. It was updated at 12:01 p.m. Dec. 16.
