DULUTH — A Monday morning forum turned into a somewhat lopsided affair when only one of the candidates running to represent the city’s 2nd District showed up to field questions. Diane Desotelle and Chris Adatte are running for an open seat that Councilor Mike Mayou vacated last month after purchasing a home outside the district. ADVERTISEMENT Adatte previously indicated his intention to participate in the forum but did not show up for the event. He declined to respond to subsequent News Tribune inquiries Monday. Desotelle, a retired city engineer and educator, said she hoped her opponent was well, even though he was unable to attend. other city council races Duluth City Council at large candidates talk taxes, spending, Right to Repair Sep 15, 2025 Swenson, Clanaugh square off in 4th District Duluth City Council race Sep 15, 2025 She said the city has more work to do if it hopes to keep talented college graduates in the community. She noted that about 60% of Duluth’s population is not earning what would be considered a living wage. Desotelle suggested the city’s talented college population is both a blessing and a challenge, with many homes being converted to student housing. She noted that many properties that might otherwise serve as “starter homes” for small families are now catering to students instead. “I’d like us to consider how we can better densify student housing on college campuses where students are spending most of their time and maybe open up some of those opportunities for people to find starter homes, because housing is a big issue for Duluth,” Desotelle said. Despite current budget shortfalls, she suggested the city not shrug off some of the very strengths that make it a desirable place to live, including parks and libraries. ADVERTISEMENT “I just don’t think those things should be the first to go, because if we lose those, we lose part of who we are,” Desotelle said. As for a referendum question on the ballot this fall for a tenant “Right to Repair” measure, authorizing local renters to execute unaddressed repairs in their residences, Desotelle expressed support. Diane Desotelle. Contributed / Diane Desotelle She said the 6,000 petition signatures in support of the policy change show that residents are interested, even if that number of certified signatures has subsequently been whittled about in half upon review of certified voters. “I think it gives renters some sense of empowerment, that they can fix a screen door, they can fix a lock and just get it done,” Desotelle said. “We need to put more pressure on our landlords who are not keeping housing up to snuff.” Desotelle also called on the city to step up its efforts to provide quality child care for working parents, especially when many are working in service industry jobs that may not pay well. She suggested that senior volunteers coming in to help at day care facilities might be part of the answer “to help build community and really assist in loving our children and making sure young people have a lot of good adults in their lives as they grow and move forward.” About the Duluth City Council The Duluth City Council’s regular meetings are on the second and fourth Mondays of each month at 6 p.m., with agenda meetings at 5:15 p.m. each Thursday preceding a regular meeting, unless otherwise scheduled, at City Hall, Room 330. Meetings are also livestreamed and recorded for later viewing at duluthmn.gov/city-council/city-council-meetings-events/council-meeting-media.
