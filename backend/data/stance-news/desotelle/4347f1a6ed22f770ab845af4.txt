Submit a Video or Picture Two candidates advance in Duluth District 2 City Council primary By WDIO --> Duluth voters went to the polls on Tuesday to select who they want to fill the empty Duluth City Council District 2 seat. With 100% of the precincts reporting, Diane Desotelle and Christopher Adatte advance to the General Election in November. Desotelle received 80% of the total votes. Duluth City Councilor Mike Mayou, who represented the second district, announced in April he would not seek reelection. RELATED STORY: Councilor Mike Mayou will not seek reelection Pierre Tournier received the least amount of votes and will not proceed to the November ballot. The City Council selected Deb DeLuca, the first female executive director of the Duluth Seaway Port Authority, to temporarily fill the District 2 seat until the winner of the November election is sworn-in in January 2026. CHRIS ADATTE Chris Adatte, Duluth Distric 2 City Council candidate WDIO speaks with Chris Adatte, candidate for Duluth City Council District 2 On why he is qualified he is qualified to run: "I work for A.G. O' Brien Plumbing and Heating. We're a service residential commercial contractor and I just spend a lot of time over the span of my career just working in the commercial space and I see the struggles that contractors, developers, even people on the public sector side for the city just have getting through a project and it's not one individual's fault, it's just the process itself is a little clunky, right? And that, I think, just gives me a unique opportunity to come in as like a public official, if I were to be elected, and go mitigate some of these issues to help encourage more business to come in town which would boost our revenue. So, I just see the problems in construction. I'm kind of hyper-focused on fixing and smoothing that process out because I see that and a lack of resources around town is kind of like the root of all our problems." On what he sees as the key issues: "Infrastructure — last summer a 15 inch sewer main collapsed, you guys actually covered the story, and sewage went into Chester Bowl, the creek. I live right next to Chester Bowl and that obviously wasn't great. And just in my conversations with the city, it's like there is miles and miles of pipes, infrastructure, critical infrastructure that needs to be maintained and that requires resources to fix it. So, obviously people in the district want their lead services replaced, which is happening. They want sewer, storm, all their infrastructure to be working safely, properly, well-funded. Housing's a big one. I don't think anyone in the district wants to see their taxes go up. There's obviously a concern with the unhoused downtown. That isn't necessarily in my district, but it impacts the constituents in the district. And again, the root of all these problems is funding. So, it really kind of just ties back into my theme of just making it easier for people to come here and start a business, add onto a business, renovate their business, hire people, grow their business, pay taxes, put that in the city's coffers, fund all the issues we have. So, that's what I think the people want. District 2, they love our parks. We have Hartley, we have Chester, we have Tischer. All those green spaces are wonderful, but they also take resources to parks and rec to keep them clean, keep the trash bins or the rubbish bins empty. It takes resources. We want to get more resources to the city, to take care of that stuff." On how goals can be accomplished: "Getting them the resources to grow the population, number one. You gotta get more people to come here. So, we need the housing on all levels, from affordable to high-end to mid-range, market rate, whatever. A rose by any other name, right? We need the housing available, and then that will bring the jobs, which people will move, relocate here to work them, or they'll live in a neighboring municipality and maybe their spouse will work in Duluth. Either way, when more goods and services exchange hands and our free market local economy is strong, they're paying taxes. Every construction project when it's built- we're doing in our district &#8211; there's a booster station for domestic water that provides all the domestic water up to Woodland. It's called the Woodland Booster Station. All the construction workers on that job site will go to local restaurants, local shops, local grocery stores in the district, patronize those businesses. Those businesses then will pay their employees, pay their vendors, again, goods and services exchange. It's almost like a micro-economy. And when you have more people paying into those systems, that all makes its way back to the county, to the state, to the city, which in turn comes back down in the form of reinvestment. " Final message to voters: "Give me a chance. Give me a chance. I think the town has more or less been on the same path with the same talking points. It's gotten a lot better under the current administration, but it's just, let's not just use empty platitudes to get what we need done. Let's have an uncomfortable conversation, albeit a respectful one. Let's bridge divides, right? It's not just the city's fault. It's not just the private sector's fault. I want to bring people together as a liaison, bridge the gap, have better communication. Let's not be afraid of growth. I think there's a lot of opportunity in it&#8211; better jobs &#8211;household median incomes will raise. The high tide raises all the boats. S,o take a chance on me. What you see is what you get. I want to focus on things that I can tangibly control, not empty talking points about things that are gonna give the same result&#8211;just things that I can actually fix." DIANE DESOTELLE Diane Desotelle, Duluth City Council District 2 candidate WDIO speaks with Diane Desotelle, a candidate for Duluth City Council District 2. On why she is qualified he is qualified to run: "I am a civil engineer by profession. I've done a lot of environmental engineering. I'm a land use planner and I'm an educator. I've been in several different positions, including working for the city and the state of Minnesota, and I've been a teacher in the public schools. I've been around the block a few times and I'm ready to give back. I've got a lot of experience talking about land uses&#8211;what we should use those lands for, protecting our environment, protecting our open spaces. My strengths are environmental engineering, but I understand the goal of sustainability. That is the economy, the environment, and our social fabric. And all of those three things need to come in balance with each other. And I have the experience of writing plans, ordinances, and working in local government as well as state government to understand the process. And that's also giving me a strength." On what she sees as the key issues: "When it comes to the environment, it actually focuses on those three legs. The economy&#8211; if we're not taking care of our open spaces, our tourists come to town to enjoy those spaces from the lake up into our open spaces and our trails. We're very fortunate to have a large group of nonprofits helping the city keep those open spaces intact, but a lot of us do not understand that some of those aren't permanently protected. And we have been working for years. When I was with the city, I was the very first city natural resources coordinator, which is looking at 25% of our lands and saying, &#8216;Which of those open spaces do we want to protect more permanently for parks, open space, and our health and our economy?' And those spaces, we've protected now over 2,000 acres. We have more to do, so that connection then is connected with where we want to focus development ties all together. So, I understand where those systems come together, and being an engineer&#8211;being a civil engineer&#8211;I know where the pipes and the infrastructure is, and we've talked about where should we extend it out, where should we do infill as far as developing and expanding our economy." On how goals can be accomplished: "By talking to everybody— listening both sides. One thing that's been really exciting about campaigning is my concentric circles of who I know, even beyond my professional, has expanded. I've done a lot of volunteer work too with a Chum and Stepping On Up, and those partners working on our homeless issues. So, I understand where that work is going and the positive things that are happening and where the gaps are. But, I'm not going to know everything, but I need to know the experts in the field or people that have lived that life to be able to talk about those issues, understand them better so we can bring it into good policy, that we feel that as staff and as government in the city, that we can actually enforce and carry through, and not just say, &#8216;Wow, that's a great idea, but I don't know how we're going to do it.' It's going to be hard with budgets this year." Final message to voters: "We're coming to this with joy, with excitement. We think working at the local level is very important because that's our voice there, and that local level voice expands to the state, expands to the national level. And, I want Duluth to be an example of how we can build, live sustainably, have a good economy, and have people that can live, work here, and enjoy the open spaces." District 2 includes the Congdon, Endion, and Kenwood neighborhoods, as well as a portion of the Chester Park neighborhood.
