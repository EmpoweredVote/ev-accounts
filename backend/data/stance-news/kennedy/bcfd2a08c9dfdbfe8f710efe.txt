Operation Metro Surge ran from early December through mid-February and put roughly 3,000 ICE and Border Patrol agents on Twin Cities streets – what DHS itself called the largest immigration enforcement operation in American history. Nearly 3,800 people were arrested. A federal judge found ICE had violated at least 96 court orders in January alone. Renée Good and Alex Pretti were shot and killed by federal agents; Victor Manuel Díaz died in ICE custody in Texas after being detained at a Coon Rapids restaurant. On Jan. 23, more than a thousand labor unions backed a general strike and tens of thousands of people walked out. In June, the John F. Kennedy Library Foundation gave its Profile in Courage Award to the People of the Twin Cities, sharing the year's recognition with Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell. Four Minnesotans accepted it in Boston on the community's behalf. Three of them open MinnPost Festival. Imam Yusuf Abdulle co-founded the Somali American Leadership Table and leads the Islamic Association of North America; he convened a community under direct political attack and led protests through the worst of it. Carolina Ortiz , associate executive director of COPAL, helped build the Immigrant Defense Network into a coalition of more than 100 organizations and helped train some 30,000 constitutional observers to document what agents were doing. Zena Stenvik , superintendent of Columbia Heights Public Schools, spoke publicly about her own students being detained and held the line on schools as protected ground. Moderated by Daniel Bergin – the Twin Cities PBS filmmaker and executive producer behind "Jim Crow of the North," who has spent more than three decades documenting Minnesota's history of racial injustice and the resistance to it – the panel will get into what those 10 weeks actually demanded of a mosque, a school district and a volunteer network built in days, and where the people who carried it think Minnesota goes from here.
