Now, many fear deportation and are deleting their social media profiles . One student from the Middle East said to the Minnesota Star Tribune, “It’s just disappointing. You come to the land of freedom, and you’re caged. … If anything, I’m less free here than I was in my country.” A painting contractor is the first person convicted of wage theft in Minnesota , according to the Minnesota Reformer. A 2019 law made theft of over $1,000 in wages a felony. Fluoride is in the news, thanks to U.S. Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy saying he plans to tell the CDC to stop recommending the mineral be added to drinking water. The Marshall Independent reports that even if federal recommendations change, residents in southwest Minnesota will likely still get fluoride in their water because it’s naturally present in the region’s groundwater. Ancora Holdings Group, LLC, a minority stockholder in U.S. Steel, has said that it is suspending its opposition to the U.S. Steel/Nippon merger after President Donald Trump has directed the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States to review the deal and make a recommendation, according to the Mesabi Tribune. Cuts to federal funding are all over the news, but one project will not go under the DOGE knife. The $3 billion Fargo/Moorhead Flood Diversion project still has all of its federal funding, according to KVRR, and project leaders don’t think the funding is in jeopardy. A run for a seat in the U.S. Senate isn’t in the cards for Democratic U.S. Rep. Ilhan Omar , MPR News reports. With fellow Democrat Sen. Tina Smith retiring after 2026, several candidates will vie for Smith’s seat. Support the news you rely on You rely on MinnPost to report on the things that matter to you.
