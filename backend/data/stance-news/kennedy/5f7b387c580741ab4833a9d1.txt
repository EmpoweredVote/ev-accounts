Editors' note: We asked our reporters to summarize the impact of the Trump administration's first 100 days on their coverage areas. Various grants by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services have been cut by the Trump administration, affecting health services and research across Minnesota. Many of them focused on health care outreach and research regarding marginalized populations, including Blacks, Native Americans, transgender people and other members of the LGBTQIA+ community. Trump 2.0: How the president’s first 100 days have impacted Minnesota The cuts have recently been delayed and corresponding layoffs of staff at the Minnesota Department of Health have been paused by a temporary restraining order by a federal district court in Rhode Island. Meanwhile, Republicans in Congress are proposing $880 billion in cuts to HHS – which will likely mean steep reductions in Medicaid, the health program for low-income Americans known as Medical Assistance in Minnesota. As those actions take shape in Minnesota, Trump appointee Robert F. Kennedy Jr., the secretary for Health and Human Services, has been scrutinized over comments regarding everything from vaccines to autism to substance use and psychiatric medication .
