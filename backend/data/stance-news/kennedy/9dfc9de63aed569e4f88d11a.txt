Sign up Every year, thousands of parents hear the heart-wrenching news that their child has been diagnosed with a rare disease, news that upends their world. Rare diseases do not discriminate, claiming thousands of lives each year as children and families in Minnesota and across the country wait desperately for a cure. And while there are numerous issues for Congress to address, including funding for the nation’s biomedical research and public health system, one solution stands out as a potential lifesaver: the Rare Pediatric Disease Priority Review Voucher Program (PRV) . Kids cannot wait — they need action now. The PRV program, established by the Federal Drug Administration over a decade ago, has been instrumental in driving the development of therapies for over 40 rare pediatric conditions. These include conditions like ASMD, sickle cell disease, Niemann-Pick disease, and many others that, without this program, would likely have never attracted investment. Importantly , the program costs Minnesota taxpayers nothing , yet it provides immense hope to families — and has improved health outcomes for thousands of children living with rare diseases. Thanks to the PRV program, once unimaginable treatments are now a reality. The first-ever treatment for Rett syndrome , a condition that affects 6,000-9,000 patients in the U.S., almost all of whom are women, was approved in 2023. Today, children with Rett syndrome have a treatment option that improves many aspects of their condition. This would have been impossible without the PRV program. As a nation committed to proven therapies, preventive medicines and innovative solutions that prioritize the welfare of its citizens, we must support policies that deliver optimal health outcomes. For the estimated 30 million Americans living with rare diseases, the Rare Pediatric PRV is a vital tool in making this vision a reality. Yet, tragically, this policy is under attack, and its reauthorization is now at risk. If Congress fails to restore this program, thousands of children and families could lose a lifeline. The concept behind the PRV program is simple: If a manufacturer develops a therapy for a rare pediatric disease, they earn a transferable priority review voucher, which grants expedited FDA review for another product. This voucher can either be used for another product or sold to another company. The policy acknowledges the significant challenges in developing treatments for rare pediatric conditions, such as the need for multiple clinical trial sites and enhanced safeguards for young participants. By providing this incentive, the program accelerates the development of life-saving treatments for children with rare diseases. Congress has an obligation to support the Give Kids a Chance Act and provide American families with solutions and hope. Currently, an estimated 30 million Americans live with a rare disease, the majority of whom are children. While our nation’s innovators are unparalleled, fewer than 5% of rare diseases currently have FDA-approved treatments. Lawmakers cannot allow the defeat of a successful law that has facilitated life-saving breakthroughs for America’s children and far exceeded its goals. Providing children with a chance at life creates hope and incurs no cost to Minnesota taxpayers. But if Congress fails to restore the Pediatric PRV program, it will cost young lives. This cannot be allowed to happen. Our children deserve better. Erica Barnes is executive director of the Minnesota Rare Disease Advisory Council, Annie Kennedy is chief of policy advocacy and patient engagement for the EveryLife Foundation for Rare Diseases, and Dominique Pichard, M.D., is chief scientific officer for the International Rett Syndrome Foundation.
