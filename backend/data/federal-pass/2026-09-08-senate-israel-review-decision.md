## Two rulings that outlive this batch

Both were put as questions on the review page and both were answered yes. Cite
them rather than re-litigating.

**1. A House-era cosponsorship counts as a sitting senator's stance.** Jim Banks
served in the House until January 2025, so his cosponsorship of the Israel
Security Assistance Support Act is on H.R. 8369 and not on the Senate twin
S. 4337. A Senate-only search silently dropped him and the gap showed only when
the count came up one short. A large share of the chamber arrived from the House,
so the alternative would have discarded their entire pre-Senate record. Same
shape as gun-policy's H.R. 698.

**2. Blocking outranks conditioning.** Sanders and Welch sign both S.J.Res. 138
(chair 3's instrument — prohibiting a named sale) and the §502B human-rights
request (chair 2's instrument — forcing a vote on conditioning arms to an ally).
Both chairs are evidenced; the batch seats chair 3, on the reading that a senator
who would prohibit the sale is not merely asking for conditions on it. Sanders
sponsors 24 such disapprovals and Welch cosponsors 12, so the blocking half is
not a marginal signal for either. This is the same precedence CC_0074 set when it
ruled chair 3 understates a signatory of an assault weapons ban.
