## 🔴 Source defects found

### A is over-long by one, and the marker is a NULL DISTRICT — not a vacate date

Indiana keeps a departed member in the list and blanks the district rather than removing the row.

| Row | Honorific | District | Reality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Andy Zay | Senator | `null` | **Resigned effective 2026-01-08** on appointment to chair the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission. **Nick McKinley** took SD-17 on **2026-02-09**. |

The rule is therefore `district != null` — Georgia's `dateVacated == null` in another dress. **Do
not de-duplicate on name or on district.** That turnover also *dates* both sources: each shows
McKinley independently, so both are current to at least 2026-02-09.

### A publishes no service-start date of any kind

`getLegislatorDetails` — the richest per-member endpoint the site has — returns
`lpid, honorific, firstname, lastname, statephone, district_id, party, busemail,
contact_form_url, caucus_page_url, bills[], committees[]`. **No service-start. No portrait URL.**

So every term is written open-ended at `start_precision = 'unknown'`. This is the GA-2 pattern —
all 235 Georgia terms are `unknown` — and it is what the 18 Indiana rows already in production
carry. **No date is invented.**

### 🔴🔴 The same host answers curl with a React shell, at HTTP 200

`getLegislators` answers `curl` with real JSON. `getLegislatorDetails` **and both KMZ URLs**
answer `curl` with **HTTP 200 and 691 bytes of `<!doctype html>`** — with a browser user agent,
with a `Referer`, and with `X-Requested-With`. The same KMZ URLs answer an in-page `fetch()`
with **1,694,603** and **2,430,264** bytes beginning `PK\x03\x04`.

A script trusting `%{http_code}` would have written a 691-byte file named `senate_2021.kmz` and
reported success. **Only a full decode catches it** — check the magic bytes and the length.

## Rulings

A legislature has no charter, so these stand in for the charter rulings a city wave carries.

### R1 — Full repair of the 18 (Cantrell, 2026-09-10)

Indiana's 18 existing seats hang on **18 pseudo-chambers**, one per district, `official_count` 0,
spread over **18 separate `State of Indiana` government rows**. Swept across every state,
**Indiana is the only one where a legislative chamber count exceeds 2.**

Create the two real chambers, repoint all 18 offices, normalise their titles to
`Representative`/`Senator`, then delete the 18 emptied pseudo-chambers. Nothing else references
them: `meetings.meetings`, `essentials.discovered_sources` and `essentials.source_outlets` hold
**zero** rows against any of the 18 chamber ids.

### R2 — Reuse the 92, leave the orphan offices (Cantrell, 2026-09-10)

Production holds **672** politicians sourced `indiana_discovery`, **671** of whom hold an office
with no district, no chamber and no government — unreachable by any address, and nothing errors.
**92 of the 150 sitting legislators are in that cohort**, each matching exactly one row, carrying
zero stances and zero photographs between them.

Seat those people rather than creating duplicates. Their orphan offices are **recorded as a debt,
not cleaned here**: retiring a seventh of 671 makes the cohort harder to reason about, and closing
a term without flagging the office vacant pushes `essentials.offices_missing_terms` unflagged
drift up — the count CI watches.

⚠ **Consequence:** after IN-2 each of those people holds **two** offices, the real one and the
orphan. Expected, not drift.

### R3 — The host government is chosen, not assumed (this session)

There are **22** government rows named `State of Indiana`, all `type` STATE, `state` IN,
`geo_id` NULL — indistinguishable by attribute. Every other state has exactly **one**.

`e00dba00-b293-499c-ad67-6f52ab8f4d7c` hosts the legislature: it is the only one carrying
correctly modelled statewide chambers (Comptroller, Secretary of State, Treasurer, Utility
Regulatory Commission, with real `official_count` values). The other 21 are an import artefact and
are **left alone** — consolidating governments is a larger repair, and half-doing it is worse than
scheduling it. After `CC_0088`, 17 of them hold no chamber at all.

### R4 — The 132 new districts match the CURRENT loader, and the legacy 18 are not touched (this session)

`scripts/load-state-tiger-boundaries.ts` writes `state` as a **lowercase** abbreviation (line 719)
and labels sldl/sldu as `State House District N` / `State Senate District N`. That is why GA, FL,
CO and NC are all lowercase — 1,910 legislative district rows across 15 states. Indiana's legacy 18
are uppercase `IN` from an older loader (only CA, IN and TX are, 319 rows).

The 132 new rows match the **current** loader, so a future loader run on Indiana is a no-op rather
than a duplicate-maker.

⚠ **The legacy 18 are deliberately NOT case-normalised.** Live read paths compare `d.state = $1`
case-sensitively against an upper-cased argument (`essentialsBrowseService.ts:100,996` →
`districtQueries.ts:218`). Those queries filter to statewide `district_type`s and so cannot see a
`STATE_LOWER` row — but proving that for every caller is a bigger claim than a seeding wave needs
to make. Indiana therefore carries both cases, and every read must keep using `lower(d.state)`.

### R5 — Eight people have two person rows each, and this wave does not merge them

Eight seats hold **both** a `ballotready` row (which has the office) and a duplicate
`indiana_discovery` row: HD-46, HD-63, HD-96, HD-99, HD-100, SD-37, SD-39, SD-44. That is why 92
matched but **84** are reused. Merging them is a decision about identity, not about seating, and is
not made inside a seeding wave.

⚠ **HD-100 is the one to read twice.** Production's `ballotready` row is **Robert B Johnson**; the
General Assembly calls him **Blake Johnson**. Confirmed the same person — Ballotpedia files him at
`Robert_Blake_Johnson`. A surname-only match would have passed this without noticing the first
name differed at all.
