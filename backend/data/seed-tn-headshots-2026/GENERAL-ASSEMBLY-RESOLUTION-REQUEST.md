# Tennessee General Assembly portraits — the licence, and the request for full-resolution originals

**Status: the licence is SETTLED. This request is about PIXELS, not rights.**
Ruling: Cantrell, 2026-09-24 — **ship under the editorial-use grant, and ask for the originals
anyway.** Store at native size (`--max-upscale 1.0`) until better files arrive.

This is deliberately *not* the MN-5 / Philadelphia shape. Those two are blocked waves waiting on a
permission we do not have. Tennessee already granted the use we make. Nothing here blocks the
import.

Research: [`.planning/research/2026-09-24-tennessee-legislature-portraits.md`](../../../.planning/research/2026-09-24-tennessee-legislature-portraits.md).

## What Tennessee publishes

🔴🔴 **THE POLICY IS IN THE IMAGE FILES, NOT ON ANY PAGE.** `capitol.tn.gov` publishes no
disclaimer, terms of use, copyright notice or photo policy anywhere — measured, with controls, over
all 100 URLs in its own `sitemap.xml`. The rights statement travels inside each portrait as a PNG
`tEXt` chunk:

> `(C)2012 State of Tennessee  Editorial or personal use only, all other uses require written`
> `permission`

Photographer, where named: **Jed DeKalb**, Chief Photographer for Tennessee State Government.
19 of 131 portraits carry a `Copyright` field; 14 name DeKalb. Nothing contradicts them — the other
112 were re-saved by tools that strip metadata (`GIMP 2.10.8`, `GIMP 2.10.22`, `Adobe Photoshop
CS6`).

**We are relying on the grant, not on silence.** The state said editorial use is permitted. A
directory entry showing an officeholder beside their seat, district and record is editorial use.
That is a reading, and the first paragraph of the letter below states it plainly so the General
Assembly can correct us if we have it wrong.

## Why write at all, then

**Resolution.** Every portrait the site serves is **400x400** (two exceptions: 400x405, 381x400).
Our display frame is 600x750 at 4:5. After the crop, 400x400 becomes 320x400, so reaching the
standard frame would cost a **1.88x enlargement on all 130** — worse than the single worst frame in
the North Carolina wave, applied to an entire state. Per the ruling we store at native size instead
and invent nothing.

These are downsized derivatives. The state holds the originals — DeKalb's studio negatives and
their digital successors. This is the **MN-6 shape**, where asking produced 1050x1350 files and took
that wave from *all 133 flagged for upscaling* to **zero**.

🟢 **The GA is also a possible answer to the party problem.** The only known source above 400x400 is
the four caucus sites, which would make a member's portrait provenance depend on their party — the
one thing this repo refuses. Originals from the General Assembly dissolve that question entirely.

**Subject:** Request for full-resolution copies of official General Assembly member portraits

> Empowered Vote is a non-profit civic information service. We publish a free, non-partisan
> directory that lets a resident enter an address and see every official who represents them, from
> their city council to Congress, each with a portrait so the person is recognisable.
>
> We have seated all 132 seats of the 114th General Assembly on that directory — 99 House districts
> and 33 Senate districts, including House District 84 as vacant.
>
> **First, so that you can correct us if we have read it wrong.** The member portraits published on
> capitol.tn.gov carry a rights statement in their file metadata: "(C) State of Tennessee.
> Editorial or personal use only, all other uses require written permission." We read our use as
> editorial — each portrait appears beside that member's own directory entry, identifying the
> person who holds the seat. The site is free to the public, carries no advertising and sells
> nothing. If you read that differently, please tell us and we will act on your answer.
>
> **What we are actually writing to ask for is better copies.** The portraits served on the member
> pages are 400 by 400 pixels. We display portraits at 600 by 750, so using the published files
> would mean enlarging them almost twofold, which visibly degrades them. We would rather not do
> that to the members' photographs.
>
> If the General Assembly holds the original, full-resolution files, we would be grateful for
> copies of the set — or for whatever subset is easy to send. We are glad to work to whatever is
> convenient: a shared folder, a drive, or a pointer to a directory we can fetch from ourselves,
> slowly and once.
>
> Two further things we would ask while we are in touch:
>
> - **The credit you want carried.** We record a credit with each image and display it. If there is
>   a single line that covers the set — the photographer's name, or "Photograph courtesy of the
>   Tennessee General Assembly" — please tell us and we will use it.
> - **We would crop each portrait to a common 4:5 frame**, which is how they are displayed beside
>   one another. No other editing is applied: no retouching, no recolouring, nothing that changes
>   what the photograph depicts. If cropping is not acceptable to you, say so and we will display
>   them uncropped instead.
>
> - **What sits beside the portrait, said plainly now rather than discovered later.** Alongside the
>   office and the district, we publish each member's positions on policy topics wherever we can
>   evidence them — each one a specific stated position, with the bill, the vote or the quotation we
>   drew it from cited next to it, so a reader can check our work. We do not publish a position we
>   cannot cite, we do not infer one from party membership, and we correct what we get wrong. Today
>   75 of the 131 seated members carry at least one such position. So the portrait does appear
>   beside a page that asserts what we have found that member's positions to be. We would rather you
>   weighed that before answering than met it afterwards, and we are glad to show you the live
>   record for any member first.
> - **The portrait itself is never used as a mark of approval.** It identifies the person, and
>   nothing on the site suggests that the General Assembly, its members or its staff endorse
>   Empowered Vote, or any candidate, party, campaign, product or cause. That is the only sense in
>   which we say the image carries no endorsement — it is not a claim that the page beside it is
>   silent about the member's own positions, because it is not.
>
> If this belongs with another office — the photographer's, or each chamber's communications staff —
> we would be grateful for the referral rather than a dead end.

## While we wait

- **The 130 portraits ship**, under the editorial-use grant, at **native size**. Per the ruling of
  2026-09-24, nothing is enlarged and nothing is held back.
- **Do not source from the caucus sites** as a resolution fix. `tnhousegop.org` serves 2048x2560 at
  exactly 4:5, which is tempting, and taking it would make provenance depend on party across four
  separate sites run by political organisations rather than by the chamber. MN-6 refused a caucus
  copy for the same reason with a grant already in hand.
- **Do not read a stripped file as unencumbered.** 112 of 131 carry no `Copyright` field. They are
  the same photographs by the same publisher; the metadata was dropped by an editor, not by the
  state.
- ⚠ **`Copyright = x-default` is an empty field, not a claim.** Three rows carry it. Read the value,
  never the presence of the key.
- ⚠ **`robots.txt` on both `capitol.tn.gov` and `wapp.capitol.tn.gov` ends `User-agent: * →
  Disallow: /`.** Named crawlers are allowed; a generic agent is not. That is not a copyright term,
  but it is a stated wish about automated fetching. The extractor run should be **slow and
  one-pass** — the measurement sweep that produced this file used a 0.35s delay across 132 pages and
  is the pace to keep.
