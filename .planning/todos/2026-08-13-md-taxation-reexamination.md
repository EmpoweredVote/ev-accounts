## 🔴🔴 WHY IT WAS WORTH REOPENING: 1736's STATED REASON WAS WRONG
1736 blanked all 13 on one premise: chair 2's "only difference from chair 1 is MAGNITUDE", so
sponsorship "cannot prove moderately". **That misreads the chair text.**
- ch1 "Significantly raise taxes on wealthy people and large companies to fund **MORE** public services"
- ch2 "Moderately raise taxes on wealthy people and large companies to fund **EXISTING** services"
🔑 The second half is **not an adverb, it is a PURPOSE test**, and a purpose is documentable from where
a bill sends its revenue. Same discriminator that settled Elo-Rivera in 1740.

## 🔑 WASHINGTON — THE ONE ROW WHERE EVEN THE ADVERB IS IN A STATE DOCUMENT
**HB0695 (2020), and he is its SOLE sponsor.** All three of chair 1's clauses, each from a document:
| clause | evidence |
|---|---|
| "large companies" | the tax reaches only businesses with **≥$100.0M global annual gross revenues** |
| "fund **MORE** public services" | revenue distributed to **The Blueprint for Maryland's Future Fund**, whose recommendations the fiscal note says need **an additional $2.8B state + $1.2B local by FY2030** |

estates. Co-sponsorships on a technical/portability measure; noted, not hidden.

## 🔴🔴 TWO DEFECTS IN THE MD TOOLING — both title-level traps that reading caught
1. **THE CANDIDATE LISTS CONTAIN CROSSFILES, NOT ONLY SPONSORSHIPS.** Alonzo Washington's 293 on-topic
   candidates include **102 Senate bills from sessions before 2023, when he was a Delegate and could not
   sponsor a Senate bill** — they are the Senate companions of his House bills, which mgaleg member
   pages also list. **SB0002 (2020) sits in his list and is sponsored by Senators Miller and Ferguson.**
   🔑 A candidate list entry is a **reading-queue item, never a sponsorship** — verify against the
   bill's own sponsor line, **in the right chamber**. Generalises the surname trap in
   [[md_sponsor_identity_traps]]. ⚠ Earlier MD passes drew citations from these same lists; they
   claimed to verify by fetching each bill, which is what would have caught this.
2. **"Business Relief and Tax Fairness Act" IS NOT A TAX BILL.** Its synopsis is about **fees collected
   by the State Department of Assessments and Taxation for filing** business documents. It was about to
   be scored as counter-evidence — a tax cut for business — across Muse (12), Benson (16),
   Waldstreicher (10) and others, purely on "Relief" and "Tax Fairness" **in the title**. Off-axis.
⚠ Also caught by reading: a first-cut `/surcharge/` net matched **court fees, civil-case surcharges,
eviction filing surcharges and development impact fees** — none a tax on the wealthy. And
`/pass.?through entit/` matched the SALT workaround as if it were a tax rise. Cf.
[[detector_first_cut_overfires]].
