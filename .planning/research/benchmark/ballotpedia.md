## Access Status

**`accessible` with a discoverability caveat.** Ballotpedia is a public wiki — no signup, captcha, paywall, rate limit, or region gate encountered at any step for any of the three addresses. The sample ballot tool is functional and returns real Monroe County May 5, 2026 primary data. This is the most straightforwardly accessible product in the benchmark set.

However — and this is the access-flavored finding that belongs in the narrative, not the score — **the real sample ballot lookup tool is buried.** The obvious URL (`https://ballotpedia.org/Sample_Ballot_Lookup`) is a marketing article with **no form on it**. The "Ballotpedia's Sample Ballot Lookup Tool" article (`Ballotpedia%27s_Sample_Ballot_Lookup_Tool`) is also pure marketing copy with a big "FIND YOUR SAMPLE BALLOT" button that is just a hyperlink back to the marketing article. **The actual ballot-entry widget lives at `https://sblv3.ballotpedia.org/address`**, embedded as an iframe with `id="sample-ballot"` on the Ballotpedia **homepage**. A naive user clicking "What's on my ballot?" from the footer lands on a wiki article that tells them to "enter your address" with no visible input field; the iframe widget is only reachable by scrolling the homepage or by noticing the `/Sample_Ballot_Lookup` link does not itself contain an input. This is a **product discoverability failure** that a human reviewer should see exactly as Playwright did. Noted in narrative; does not change the `accessible` score because nothing *blocked* measurement — the widget is reachable, it is just obscured.

**D-02 classification:** Access gap avoidance was trivially successful. The ballot data is real, populated, and measurable. Anything missing is a **product gap**.

## Narrative Notes

1. **Bio/encyclopedic depth is Ballotpedia's editorial moat — and it is real.** `08-candidate-page-houchin.txt` captures career prose (Salem IN, IU undergrad, GW grad, Contend Communications founder, Dan Coats regional director, Indiana State Senate D47 2014-2022), committee structure (Education and the Workforce + 3 subcommittees; Financial Services + 3 subcommittees; Rules), tenure lineage (predecessor Trey Hollingsworth, successor Gary Byrne in the vacated state senate seat), compensation ($174,000), and certified 2024 general-election vote totals. **This is the deepest narrative bio in the benchmark set** — comparable to Wikipedia's best political biographies. EV's Phase 112 audit found 0/51 bios; this dimension is EV's biggest non-intentional gap for local/state candidates and a real competitive moat for Ballotpedia at the federal level. Plan 07 should rank Ballotpedia Dim 3 = 3 and flag it in plan 115's gap report. The VoteSmart and Ballotpedia bios are both 3 but editorially different: VoteSmart surfaces personal data (family, religion, birth date), Ballotpedia surfaces career/committee/succession narrative prose.

2. **Address-lookup discoverability is the Ballotpedia UX surprise of this spot-check.** The sample ballot tool exists and works well — but finding it requires surprising user effort. The obvious wiki article `/Sample_Ballot_Lookup` is pure marketing copy with no input form. The "Ballotpedia's Sample Ballot Lookup Tool" article is also marketing copy with a big "FIND YOUR SAMPLE BALLOT" button that is just a link back to the marketing article. The actual widget lives at `https://sblv3.ballotpedia.org/address` and is embedded as `<iframe id="sample-ballot">` on the Ballotpedia homepage. A user clicking "What's on my ballot?" from any navigation footer lands on a wiki article that tells them to enter an address with no visible input field. **The real tool is effectively invisible to anyone who doesn't scroll the homepage or stumble into it via the homepage iframe.** This is a product discoverability failure at Ballotpedia's scale (679k articles, professional staff) and is the most surprising finding of plan 05. A naive human test would have concluded Ballotpedia "does not have a ballot lookup tool" because the most obvious URL for finding it does not contain one. Plan 07 should NOT score this as a `blocked` case (nothing blocked measurement once we found the widget) but the narrative should flag Ballotpedia's discoverability weakness as UX friction.

3. **Endorsements are surfaced loudly and prominently, which is Dimension 10's pivot point.** Houchin's wiki has a dedicated `Endorsements` section (`08-candidate-page-houchin.txt`) showing "President Donald Trump (R) 2024." Race pages carry explicit "Race ratings" infoboxes with Cook Political Report / DDHQ / Inside Elections / Sabato's Crystal Ball — these are third-party partisan handicappers, and surfacing them as first-class data is an explicit editorial choice. **Ballotpedia's Dim 10 score is 0 and this is not a borderline call: party labels, endorsement lists, and partisan forecast ratings are all central features of the editorial product, not side surfaces.** Per METHODOLOGY §6, EV intentionally omits all three (no party labels, no endorsement aggregation, no race ratings), and under D-10.5 "same ruler" Ballotpedia scores 0 on Dim 10 and EV scores 3. **Unlike VoteSmart which is by-mission partisan (explicit interest-group disclosure product)**, Ballotpedia's mission statement says "firmly committed to neutrality" and "100 percent objective" — but the surface features (party tags, endorsements, race ratings) make neutrality a claim, not an operationalization. Plan 07 narrative can note this asymmetry: VoteSmart is partisan-framed and transparent about it; Ballotpedia is partisan-framed while claiming neutrality. The score is the same but the editorial self-awareness is different.

4. **Race coverage is 79-93% of baseline — second-highest in the benchmark set.** Ballotpedia's ballot tool surfaces federal, state legislative, county-wide, county council, township trustee, and township board contests for the Kirkwood address. This is broader than VoteSmart's product-scope-limited 21%, comparable to Vote411's 93%, and infinitely above BallotReady's 0%. Dim 1 = 2 because of the council-district superset imprecision (shows all 4 when voter can only vote in 1) and incomplete candidate-level drill verification for local races. A generous reading could score 3; a strict reading scores 2; the honest midpoint is 2.

5. **Data freshness is the strongest in the benchmark set.** The tool surfaces a live 23-day countdown to May 5, 2026 at capture time, candidate wiki pages cite 2024 certified results, the copyright footer reads "© 2026 Ballotpedia, Inc." (vs VoteSmart's stale "© 1992-2021"), and the ballot tool explicitly commits to updates: "As we get closer to election day and find additional races and candidates, we will update your sample ballot accordingly." **Dim 9 = 3 without qualification.** Plan 07 should flag Ballotpedia as the freshest-data product.

6. **Candidate Connection is Ballotpedia's Dim 5/6 surrogate, and it is an editorially honest opt-in model.** Unlike VoteSmart's "Political Courage Test" which fills in non-participants' positions via inference (tweets, roll-call votes, interest-group scorecards), Ballotpedia's Candidate Connection survey leaves the slot empty when the candidate declines — non-participants get a missing ✔ marker on the race page, not inferred positions. This is more honest about the editorial gap but also means stance/issue data (Dim 5) and Q&A (Dim 6) are sparse: present when candidates opt in, absent otherwise. Plan 07 should note that Ballotpedia's Dim 5 = 1 and Dim 6 = 2 are **opt-in-sparse**, not broken.

7. **Legislative record (Dim 7) is the sharp contrast with VoteSmart.** Houchin's wiki shows committee assignments and election-history vote totals (the 2024 general numbers are certified and full) but **no roll-call votes, no bill sponsorship list, no voting record page**. VoteSmart captured 31 pages of Houchin roll-call votes through 2026-03-27; Ballotpedia captures 0 roll-call votes on the same candidate. **Ballotpedia's editorial model is encyclopedic narrative; VoteSmart's is legislative tracking.** For a voter who wants "how did my rep vote on HR 7084?" VoteSmart is the only product that answers; for "who is Erin Houchin and what is her career?" Ballotpedia is the answer. Dim 7 = 1. **This is a product-scope decision by Ballotpedia, comparable to VoteSmart's decision not to cover township trustees — two different products with two different editorial identities.** Plan 07 narrative should frame it this way.

8. **Withdrawn candidate tracking (E4) is a Ballotpedia-unique editorial surface.** IN-9's race page lists "Withdrawn or disqualified candidates: James Davidson (D), Cody Voyles (D), Emilee McCartney (D)" — candidates who filed but later exited. This is editorial diligence no other benchmark competitor shows. Worth logging as derived-extra E4 for plan 07. Relevant to EV's `essentials.politicians` + `candidacies` data model consideration (see `project_candidates_vs_politicians.md` in memory).

9. **County council D1-D4 superset is the same imprecision pattern Vote411 had.** The Kirkwood voter sees all 4 county council district races, but can only vote in D1 because council members are elected by single-member districts. This is the same kind of sub-county-level imprecision Vote411 exhibited with council races — the geofence model is district-aware for state-level boundaries (HD 46 vs HD 61 change correctly across addresses) but not for sub-county sub-boundaries. Plan 07 should note this as a shared weakness pattern across ballot-tool competitors: state-level precision is good, sub-county precision is a superset because the underlying district data is sparse.

10. **The "Ballotpedia Sample Ballot Lookup Tool" has a meta-documentation layer — a wiki page about Ballotpedia's OWN quality benchmarks for sample ballot tools (`Eight_Quality_Benchmarks_for_a_Sample_Ballot_Lookup_Tool`).** This is interesting editorial transparency: Ballotpedia publishes the rubric by which it tests its own ballot tool. Worth noting in plan 07 as a methodological asset — it is itself a free benchmarking reference. Not captured in this drill but linked prominently from the Sample_Ballot_Lookup wiki article.

## Blockers

**None.** No signup wall, captcha, paywall, region gate, rate limit, or geo-block was encountered at any point in the 6 scrape runs. All three addresses successfully submitted through the `sblv3.ballotpedia.org/address` widget, all three returned real race lists, candidate wiki pages loaded cleanly, race wiki pages loaded cleanly.

**Important per D-02:** Ballotpedia is `accessible`, not `partially blocked` like VoteSmart. Nothing gated the data. The only friction encountered was a **discoverability problem**, not an access problem — the real sample ballot widget lives at a non-obvious URL (`sblv3.ballotpedia.org/address`) and the most obvious wiki entry points (`/Sample_Ballot_Lookup`, `/Ballotpedia%27s_Sample_Ballot_Lookup_Tool`) are marketing articles without embedded forms. A determined human user (or Playwright) eventually finds the widget via the homepage iframe; a casual user may not. This is logged in Narrative Note #2 as a UX weakness but does not affect the `accessible` classification.

**Human-fallback reproduction steps (for independent verification):**
1. Visit `https://ballotpedia.org/` (homepage, NOT `/Sample_Ballot_Lookup`).
2. Scroll down — the sample ballot widget is an embedded iframe ~halfway down the page.
3. Or directly visit `https://sblv3.ballotpedia.org/address`.
4. Enter `200 W Kirkwood Ave, Bloomington, IN 47404`, select the Google Places autocomplete suggestion, click "View my ballot."
5. Select "Election on Tuesday, May 5th 2026" from the election-choices page.
6. Click "GO" on the introduction page.
7. Click the "Ballot Overview" link in the top nav (or visit `https://sblv3.ballotpedia.org/ballot` in the same session — direct URL without session context resets to the address form).
8. Confirm: 13-16 race contests visible, including U.S. House IN-9, Indiana House D61, Monroe County Assessor/Prosecuting Attorney/Sheriff/Clerk/Recorder/Circuit Court 5/9, Monroe County Commissioner D1, Monroe County Council D1-D4, Bloomington Township Board (3 seats) + Trustee.

**Non-blocker UX friction worth logging per D-02 transparency:**
- Direct URL navigation to `/ballot` or `/election-choices` without a prior address-submission in the same browser context resets the user to the address-entry screen. Session state is cookie-based, not URL-queried. A determined Playwright/human can always re-submit the address.
- The `sblv3` subdomain is only discoverable via the homepage iframe or direct URL knowledge — the sample-ballot wiki articles do not link to it directly (they embed it, but only if JavaScript and iframes render correctly; our initial r1-r3 runs completely missed it because we navigated directly to the wiki articles instead of the homepage).
- The "Ballot Overview" link in the top navigation is how a logged-in ballot session walks race-by-race; our r6 drill captured the overview list but did not drill every race due to session-state fragility under repeated direct URL navigation. **11-13 of 14 baseline races verified at list level; candidate-level depth verified at full depth only on US House IN-9 via the candidate wiki route.**
