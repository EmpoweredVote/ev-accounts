## Narrative Notes

1. **EV's essentials surface is structurally different from every competitor — it is a "who represents you" tool with a grafted "who's on your ballot" tab.** Every competitor in the benchmark set (BallotReady, Vote411, VoteSmart, Ballotpedia) is a **ballot-lookup** product that organizes around "here are the races on your ballot." EV's Representatives tab is the **default view** and is organized around "here are the people who currently hold power over you" — sitting officials across Local/State/Federal/Cabinet/SCOTUS tiers, including non-elected officials (Cabinet secretaries, SCOTUS justices, Court of Appeals judges, appointed commissioners). The Elections tab is a **secondary** surface that mirrors the competitors' organization. This is a **product-philosophy difference** worth flagging for plan 07's narrative: EV is trying to do both jobs from one surface, and the Representatives-first default means a Kirkwood voter lands first on a list of 60+ sitting officials (half of whom they cannot vote for in the May 5 primary) before finding the 13-race ballot view. The UX cost: more cognitive load. The UX benefit: the product is useful year-round, not just in the 4-week window before an election.

2. **The bio gap is EV's biggest non-intentional weakness and it scores a hard 0.** AUDIT-REPORT-112 recorded `0 of 51 linked candidates have bio` and the captures confirm it: Houchin, Pierce, and Bolden all render a generic **office** description ("The U.S. House of Representatives is one of two chambers for the federal legislature...") in the slot where a biographical narrative should appear. Ballotpedia scored 3 here for encyclopedic prose; VoteSmart scored 3 for personal/family/religion data; EV scores 0 for having no candidate-specific biography at all. **This is the dimension where the same harshness applied to competitors bites EV hardest**, exactly as METHODOLOGY §7 said it should. Plan 115's gap report should flag Dim 3 as priority #1 — this is a substantive product gap, not an intentional omission, and it scores the same 0 the rubric would give a competitor who dropped in a generic lorem-ipsum placeholder.

3. **Legislative record is EV's quiet strength but caps at 2 because the local level has no data.** Pierce's profile showing "**Voted in 100% of roll calls**" with a specific example vote ("Voted Nay on Shooting ranges. — passed") is genuinely impressive — it is more than VoteSmart's lofted-metric approach (plan 04 Dim 7 = 3 but without roll-call-percent grounding), even though VoteSmart captures 31 roll-call pages to EV's single-example summary. Federal and state legislative data is clearly wired via Congress.gov + LegiScan + Open States. But **Bolden's profile has zero legislative record surface** — no committee memberships, no votes, no bills — because Bloomington City Council is a municipal body not covered by the federal+state data pipeline. Same pattern would apply to Monroe County Council, Township Trustee, Circuit Court judges, and every other local-tier race on the actual May 5 ballot. For a Monroe County voter whose day-to-day representation is primarily local, the legislative-record dimension has a **federal-is-great / local-is-empty** shape. Score 2 is the honest midpoint.

4. **The Elections tab vs Representatives tab split is an architectural choice with a real bug.** For Kirkwood, the Elections tab correctly separates "primary races on your ballot" from the "representatives who hold power over you" default view — this is architecturally cleaner than any competitor's conflation of the two concepts. But the bug is that the Representatives view for Kirkwood surfaces Monroe County Council **District 4** members (Crossley, Deckard, Feitl, Henry) for a voter who lives in Council District 1, and the Elections view for Kirkwood surfaces the **District 4** Democratic primary (Jennifer Crossley) instead of the **District 1** primary (Peter James Iversen, per AUDIT-REPORT-112 candidate-linkage table). **This is a concrete, falsifiable miss**: the Kirkwood voter actually votes for CC D1, and EV shows them CC D4. Either the geofence-to-council-district binding is wrong for downtown Bloomington, or the query is returning "members of the council" rather than "the member who represents this address." Logged as narrative because it's the most actionable finding of the self-audit. Plan 115 should file this as a concrete bug, not just a coverage gap.

5. **Antipartisan enforcement is real and visible in the captures.** Across 36 page dumps of every representative card and every profile, there is **zero** party-label rendering on sitting officials' cards. The only party labels in the entire evidence set appear on the Elections tab as race headers ("STATE REPRESENTATIVE, DISTRICT 61 — DEMOCRATIC PRIMARY"), which is the structural necessity of Indiana's closed primary law. No endorsements, no interest-group ratings, no red/blue color overlays anywhere. The FEC total raised number ($1,094,344 for Houchin, $9,698 for Pierce) is rendered as a neutral transparency signal with no partisan framing, no donor breakdown, no "who gave the most" ranking. This is the **operational definition of antipartisan** per METHODOLOGY §6 and it is honestly earned — the product deliberately omits features the rubric's Dim 10 inversion rewards omitting. Score 3 is the rubric's intended outcome, not a privilege.

6. **Rural address geocoding is brittle — same failure mode Vote411 had.** `7333 W Mt Tabor Rd, Bloomington, IN 47404` returns "We couldn't find that address. Please enter a full street address..." plus empty `Local representative data is not yet available for this area` / `State representative data is not yet available for this area` / `No upcoming elections found`. Vote411 (plan 03) had the **same failure** on the same address.
