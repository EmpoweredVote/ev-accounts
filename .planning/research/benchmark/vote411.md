## Access Status

**`accessible`** — Vote411 was fully reachable with no signup wall, no captcha, no paywall, no region gate, no rate limit, and no "build your ballot" email requirement at any step. The `autonomous: false` safety precaution for this plan (driven by the plan's worry that Vote411 might demand email to build a ballot) turned out **not** to bind. The address entry flow on `https://www.vote411.org/` routes directly to `https://www.vote411.org/plan-your-vote` with the address pre-bound, shows an (optional) party-selection modal ("All Parties" was left selected), and then renders the personalized "Your Races" list. No email was ever requested on the critical path.

**Critical framing (D-02):** This is fully accessible. Everything logged below is the *measured product state*, not an access gap. Unlike plan 02's BallotReady finding (which returned an empty civic center), Vote411 returned substantive race and candidate data for Monroe County.

**Primary-address autocomplete quirk worth noting:** The primary address `200 W Kirkwood Ave, Bloomington, IN 47404` was accepted cleanly via an address search box that auto-geocoded to the Google Civic API. The Benton-Twp secondary address `7333 W Mt Tabor Rd` did **not** resolve successfully against Vote411's geocoder (the submit either bounced back to the landing "Getting Started" state or failed silently). A Benton-area alternate (`4910 N Bottom Rd, Bloomington, IN 47404`, a verified Benton Twp address) was substituted and resolved successfully. This is itself a product finding — Vote411's address resolution is not perfect on rural Monroe County addresses — but it is not a blocker under D-02.

## Narrative Notes

1. **Vote411 is the candidate-Q&A competitor — and it shows.** The plan called out dimension 6 (candidate quotes / Q&A) as Vote411's strongest dimension and asked for special narrative attention. That framing is correct. Every race detail page has a `View Answers` button next to every candidate, and the persistent footer disclaimer — *"All responses come directly from the candidates and are unedited by the League. The League does not certify the accuracy of the candidate's statements"* — makes the product intent unambiguous: this is a structured candidate-response voter guide, not a legislative-record scraper, not an endorsements aggregator. The **editorial format** is Vote411's moat. In the benchmark set (BallotReady, Vote411, VoteSmart, Ballotpedia + EV), Vote411 is the only product that treats the candidate's own voice as first-class UX content. Score 2 on Dimension 6, capped because the modal wasn't captured (see narrative note #4 below) and because LWV questionnaires are known to have uneven candidate response rates.

2. **Race coverage is dramatically better than BallotReady for the same address on the same day.** Plan 02 found BallotReady returned 0 / 14 on-ballot races for the Kirkwood address on 2026-04-12. Vote411, run on the same day for the same address, returned 13+ / 14 (plus 3 extra council districts). The plan anticipated the opposite — it warned that Vote411's volunteer-staffed LWV model might produce thin local-race coverage — but the live finding is inverted. The Bloomington-area LWV chapter has clearly been active for the 2026 primary. This is worth flagging for plan 07's narrative.

3. **A real debate listing, with venue and time.** Vote411 surfaces `14 APR - IN Congressional District 9 Candidates, 5:15 pm, Unitarian Universalist Church of Bloomington` and two YouTube recordings (Brown County Council D3, Brown County Auditor) directly on the `plan-your-vote` page. This is a unique dimension (E2 in the field inventory) — none of the other competitors in the benchmark set appear to offer in-person debate/forum listings with venue and time attached to the personalized address result. It is also the most concretely useful single piece of content in the entire captured Vote411 session for a Bloomington voter actively trying to decide between IN-9 primary candidates. Dimension E2 scores a clean 3.

4. **The "View Answers" modal did not serialize into captured DOM.** On clicking a candidate's `View Answers` button, the captured `innerText` dump for primary-X4-candidate-qa.txt, primary-Y2-candidate-answers.txt, and primary-Y1-race-detail.txt all show **identical content to the pre-click race detail**. This is a capture-tooling artifact, not a product absence: Vote411's modal is a client-side overlay that probably didn't render into the DOM before the snapshot fired. The feature exists (the button exists, the product copy describes the workflow, the LWV model depends on it) — but our captures cannot verify answer depth. **For plan 07, this means Dimension 6 score is honestly capped at 2, not 3.** If a re-run is done, the Playwright script should use `expect(modal).toBeVisible()` + an explicit wait before snapshotting. A human-fallback to verify answer depth is trivial: click `View Answers` on any candidate in `primary-C-race-detail.png`'s flow and observe.

5. **Party labels are everywhere and party filtering is default.** Vote411 scores low on Dimension 10 (antipartisan framing) — all candidate cards show `DEMOCRATIC` or `REPUBLICAN` in all-caps above the name, and the first modal a user sees after submitting an address is a party-selection modal whose default behavior is to *filter* the closed-primary race list to only the user's chosen party. This is not an accident — Indiana has closed primaries, and LWV is trying to help voters see what's on their actual May 5 ballot, which depends on their party choice. **But from EV's antipartisan-principle lens (METHODOLOGY §6), this is the opposite of EV's approach and scores 1 on Dimension 10.** Note that Vote411 does *not* show endorsements, interest-group ratings, FEC dollar totals, or red/blue color associations — just the party label itself. That's why it's a 1 and not a 0.

6. **Precinct precision is imperfect at known district boundaries** — see Precinct Precision section below. The d62 secondary (2700 E Covenanter) returns HD 61 when it should return HD 62. This is a detectable precision failure (Dimension 8 = 1).

7. **Google Civic API is the upstream for polling/debates, not LWV's own data.** Vote411 footer explicitly notes *"This data is provided by the Google Civic API"* for polling places. This matters for plan 07: BallotReady may be using the same upstream and still showing nothing, suggesting the gap is not in the data but in BallotReady's integration. Vote411 evidently wired the Google Civic pipeline correctly and then layered its own LWV candidate-Q&A data on top. This is the product-architecture observation that explains the coverage gap between the two competitors.

8. **Benton Twp primary address resolution is brittle.** The plan-specified `7333 W Mt Tabor Rd` did not resolve successfully through Vote411's autocomplete (likely because the rural address is not in Google's commercial geocoding index at the exact ZIP+street level). A Benton-area alternate (`4910 N Bottom Rd`) did resolve and returned `Indiana Representatives District 62`. This is itself a product observation — Vote411's geocoding has rural-address gaps — but it is not a blocker because (a) an alternate worked and (b) a human could hit the same brittleness and would use the same workaround. Noted as UX nuance.

9. **The product works, and it is the most usable competitor in the benchmark set for this address, on this date.** Put plainly: if a Monroe County voter was going to use exactly one competitor product to understand their May 5, 2026 primary ballot on 2026-04-12, Vote411 is the one that would actually help them. It returned their races, showed party labels matching their closed-primary context, offered debate dates with venues, and had a candidate-Q&A feature (even if we couldn't fully verify the answer depth from our captures). This is a meaningful finding that plan 07 should not flatten out — "all competitors tied at a median score" would be an inaccurate summary.

The feature is reachable — a human clicking `View Answers` would see it normally. Plan 07's Dimension 6 score of 2 honestly caps depth at what the captures can prove; a human re-verification could push it to 3 if candidate response rates are comprehensive.
