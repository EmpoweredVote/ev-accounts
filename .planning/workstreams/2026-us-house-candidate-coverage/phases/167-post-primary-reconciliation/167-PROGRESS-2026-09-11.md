# Phase 167 — post-primary reconciliation: progress, 2026-09-11

Worked overnight 2026-09-11. **4 of 17 clusters complete, 48 of 263 stale rows resolved.**
Nothing has been applied to production — every migration is authored, dry-run, and waiting.

## Why the phase was overdue

The seeding phases deliberately wrote the FULL pre-primary field in late-primary states with
`provisional_until` set to each state's primary date, so primary voters saw their real options.
Phase 167 is the gate that culls the losers. It was planned in July, date-gated to Aug–Sep, and
never started: on 2026-09-11, **263 candidate rows across 17 states and 53 races were past their
re-verification date and still marked active**, the oldest by 34 days. A live check of the public
API returned **seven candidates for TN-07 and eight for the U.S. Senate race in Tennessee** —
whole pre-primary fields, seven weeks before the general.

Essentials does disclose it (the race carries the date and the view says "past its re-verification
date and may be out of date"), so voters were warned rather than misled.

## 🔴 The finding that changes the phase

**Culling losers is the smaller half of the job. Our fields are also missing candidates.**

| cluster | missing from our data, on the certified general ballot |
|---|---|
| VT At-Large | Phoenix K. Altair (I), Suzanne "Suz" Seymour (I), Ryan P. Walton (I) |
| WY At-Large | Jeffrey Haggit (Constitution) |

Both states publish a certified GENERAL-election candidate roster, and both rosters hold names we
do not have. The cause is structural: independents and minor-party candidates never appear in a
primary — they petition straight onto the general ballot, with deadlines *after* the primary and
after our seeding runs. **A results-driven cull can only remove names; it can never discover one.**

**Method change for the remaining 13 clusters:** where a state publishes a certified general-election
candidate roster, reconcile against that. It answers "who is on the ballot" directly, and it is the
only source that surfaces a missing candidate. Fall back to primary results only where no such
roster exists.

Neither missing-candidate case is fixed here. Seeding a candidate needs the Wave-1/2 methodology
(politician record, external_id scheme, headshot, federal 24-topic stance set), not a cull
migration. Both are filed as todos, and **both races keep `provisional_until` set** rather than
being marked verified while incomplete.

## Method, as it now stands

Copied from `1842_wa_2026_primary_certification_pass.sql` and extended:

1. **Prove certification against the jurisdiction's own document, and cross-check with a second
   one.** HI: summary.txt vs histatewide.pdf, agreeing candidate-for-candidate. AK: the OFFICIAL
   RESULTS summary PDF vs an independent aggregation of the precinct CSV, agreeing to the vote.
2. **Raw jurisdiction documents only.** A web search summary for HI CD-2 returned a garbled
   Democratic field that listed the Republican nominee among the Democrats — the same failure mode
   1842 recorded for the Kitsap sheriff race.
3. **Diff against the certified general roster where one exists** (new — see the finding above).
4. **Refuse to cull an unsettled cut line.** Hold the row, write a todo, keep the race flagged.
5. **Never clear `provisional_until` on a field known to be incomplete.** The flag is the only
   thing telling a reader the race is not verified; clearing it without doing the work is the one
   outcome worse than being late.
6. **A withdrawal needs both columns.** `essentials.is_live_candidate` drops a row on
   `candidate_status = 'withdrawn'` OR `result = 'not_nominated'` — `result = 'withdrew'` is
   neither. 1857's post-verify caught this before it shipped.
7. No hard deletes, and no `politicians.is_active = false`. The phase text calls for the second
   path, but `is_active` drives the reps feed and a sitting officeholder who loses a primary still
   holds the seat until January. 1842 did not use it; neither do these.
