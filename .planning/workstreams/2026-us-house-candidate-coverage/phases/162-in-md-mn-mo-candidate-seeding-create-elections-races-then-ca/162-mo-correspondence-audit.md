## Background (from named sources)

Missouri's General Assembly, in a 2025 special session called by Governor Mike Kehoe (announced Aug 29, 2025), enacted a mid-decade congressional redistricting plan (HB 1) whose stated partisan goal — per multiple named sources — was to dismantle the state's sole Democratic seat, old MO-5 (Kansas City, Rep. Emanuel Cleaver), and move the delegation from 6-2 to 7-1 Republican. Governor Kehoe signed the map into law **September 28, 2025**. The mechanism: split Kansas City / Jackson County three ways (among the new 4th, 5th, and 6th) and stretch the new 5th ~200 miles east through a dozen-plus mostly-very-red counties.

**Litigation + referendum status (re-verified 2026-07-04, same-day as execution — within the 7-day freshness window):** The 2025 map is **currently in effect** and is the operative map for the **August 4, 2026 primary**.
- A state trial judge ruled the map constitutional (Mar 12, 2026) and kept it in effect despite the referendum campaign (Mar 27, 2026).
- The **Missouri Supreme Court upheld the map 4-3 on March 24, 2026** (nothing in the state constitution bars mid-decade redistricting).
- On **May 12, 2026** the Missouri Supreme Court **unanimously** ruled that the mere submission of referendum signatures does not automatically prevent the map from taking effect, and rejected two further challenges (including a compactness claim). The map "that targets Rep. Cleaver" is in effect.

**⚠ UNSETTLED — a hard decision point falls INSIDE this phase's execution window (see Notes for downstream consumers):** A citizen veto referendum ("People Not Politicians," 305k+ signatures submitted Dec 9, 2025) is in signature verification. SoS Denny Hoskins has stated **"the map will not be frozen until I certify the referendum,"** with county clerks certifying signatures by **July 27, 2026** and his office's certification expected shortly after. If the referendum qualifies, it goes on the **Nov 3, 2026** ballot, and the map's status for the **general election** (as opposed to the already-locked Aug-4 primary) is genuinely uncertain — Votebeat has reported MO "could be stuck using an invalid congressional map." This is the classic D-01 premise with an added twist: unlike TN (where only the polygon is stale and the new map is settled), MO carries live risk that the map could REVERT to the OLD boundaries for November — which would invert the staleness logic. The seed-but-withhold approach (D-01b) is the correct hedge either way.

## Severity Rubric

*(Cloned verbatim from the TN audit, 161-01.)*

A district is scored **SEVERE** if either condition holds:
1. **>25% of the district's population moved to a different congressional district** between the old (pre-2025, currently-live-in-`essentials.districts`) map and the new (2025) map, OR
2. **The district's core anchor city/county changed** — i.e., the metro area or county that defined the district's political and geographic identity under the old map is no longer the anchor under the new map (either lost entirely to another district, or a new metro anchor was added that wasn't previously present).

Otherwise a district is scored **NOT-SEVERE**.

**Evidence basis:** No shapefile/GIS diff was performed (per 162-RESEARCH.md's Don't-Hand-Roll guidance — a qualitative county-level breakdown from named reporting is sufficient and avoids installing any new GIS dependency). Severity is derived from (a) explicit county-reassignment reporting in named sources, (b) a quantitative proxy — the shift in each district's Cook rating / 2024 presidential margin under the new boundaries vs. the old (Sabato's Crystal Ball + Inside Elections analyses), and (c) explicit "anchor changed" statements from named reporting (a district gaining or losing a metro core, e.g., a share of Kansas City).

## Notes for downstream consumers (162-02, 162-11)

- **MO-5 (2905)** is the unambiguous, expected-severe case (old Cleaver seat, dismantled) — matches the CONTEXT.md/RESEARCH.md pre-audit hypothesis exactly.
- **MO-4 (2904) and MO-6 (2906)** — flagged for adjacency review in the pre-audit hypothesis — both score **SEVERE**: each absorbs a major share of Kansas City (25% and ~35% respectively), a metropolitan anchor entirely absent from their old rural configurations. This is the anchor-added prong (identical to TN-6 gaining downtown Nashville).
- **MO-2 (2902) and MO-3 (2903)** are the **expansion beyond the pre-audit hypothesis** — the St. Louis-region domino. RESEARCH Pitfall 1 explicitly warned to "budget for a severe set larger than {2905}" (TN found 5/9 vs an assumed ~2). This audit confirms that pattern: MO lands at **5/8 severe**. The driver is the whole-county swap of St. Charles (~400k) from MO-2 into MO-3, plus MO-3's consolidation of Columbia — a substantial geographic reconstitution of both districts even though MO-3's partisan lean is unchanged (R+20). **Do not let partisan stability mask geographic movement**: the rubric scores boundary/population shift, and the wrong-race harm (a St. Charles voter served the new-MO-2 slate while actually a new-MO-3 constituent) is real for both.
- **MO-1 (2901), MO-7 (2907), MO-8 (2908)** are confirmed not-severe: St. Louis urban core, SW-Missouri/Springfield, and SE-Missouri/Bootheel anchors are all unchanged with no reported reassignment. MO-1 is monitored as the STL-region district nearest the 2/3 shuffle but shows no evidence of territory movement.
- **⚠ MO-SPECIFIC LEGAL FRESHNESS (harder than TN's):** The map is locked for the **Aug 4 primary**, but the veto referendum's signature certification (county clerks due **July 27**, SoS certification shortly after) and SoS Hoskins's statement that **"the map will not be frozen until I certify the referendum"** mean the **general-election** map is genuinely unsettled. If the referendum qualifies/freezes, the state could revert to the OLD boundaries for Nov 3 — which would **invert** this audit's staleness logic (the old polygons would become correct and the new-map races wrong). **162-02 must (a) proceed with the new-map field for now — it is correct for the Aug-4 primary and is the operative map today; (b) rely on D-01b withholding of the 5 severe districts as the hedge that keeps us from serving a wrong race under EITHER outcome; and (c) treat MO as a mandatory Phase-167 re-pull after the July 27 / referendum-certification decision resolves.** Re-verify this legal status before MO race authoring in 162-02 if more than a few days have elapsed since 2026-07-04.
- **Downstream contract:** the severe geo_id set `{2902, 2903, 2904, 2905, 2906}` is consumed by 162-02 (each severe race wires `election_id` to the withheld election row per D-01b) and 162-11 (verify.sql asserts each severe race's parent election is the withheld one + a negative coordinate-smoke sample proving zero races surface for a severe-district coordinate).
