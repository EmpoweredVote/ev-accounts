## Why we ask first, and how this differs from South Dakota

South Dakota's profile pages carry an explicit **"Use by Permission Only"** above every portrait, so
that wave asked because it had been told to. **Mississippi has told us nothing in either direction,
and that is the finding:** `legislature.ms.gov/terms-and-conditions/` has never been written — it
still contains the content-management system's placeholder text (*"This is an example of a general
page. Aenean ultricies mi vitae est… Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet…"*). The page a reader would check
for reuse terms is boilerplate.

⚠ **The disclaimer is not the terms page, and it was read in full to be sure.**
`legislature.ms.gov/disclaimer/` covers accuracy and liability only — nothing about copyright,
reuse or permission — and the footer carries a bare `© 2026` with no rights statement and no named
holder.

▶ So the posture is: **silence is not permission.** We ask before any use, and we do not treat a
non-reply as a yes.

## What is being asked for, measured

The Legislature publishes a portrait for every sitting member at

```
https://billstatus.ls.state.ms.us/members/house/<name>.jpg
https://billstatus.ls.state.ms.us/members/senate/<name>.jpg
```

Measured 2026-09-28: **178 files, all present, typically 675×900** — larger than our 600×750
publishing size, so nothing would be upscaled. The filename comes from each member's own
`<IMG_NAME>` element in `members/{house,senate}/<slug>.xml`. A control proved the directory does not
soft-404; a second proved the GlobalSign chain fix is still needed to reach the host at all.

## The email

**Subject:** Permission request — Mississippi legislator portraits for a free civic lookup service

Dear Mississippi Legislature,

I am writing to ask permission to display the official portraits of the members of the Mississippi
House and Senate on Empowered Vote, a free, non-profit service that helps residents find out who
represents them.

I am sending this to the webmaster address because it is the only one your contact page publishes.
If permission is not yours to give, I would be grateful if you could pass this on, or tell me which
office to write to instead.

Nothing has been copied or published. I am asking before any use. Your website states no terms
either way — the Terms and Conditions page has not been written, and the disclaimer covers accuracy
rather than reuse — and I would rather ask than treat that silence as permission.

**What the service does.** A resident enters their address and sees the people who represent them:
their state senator, their state representative, their city council member and their county
supervisor, each with a name, office and district. We have already built Mississippi's legislative
districts and seated all 174 members from your own member pages. The portrait is what lets a
resident recognise the person the page is describing.

**What I am asking for.** Permission to display each member's official portrait beside that
member's entry. We would keep our own copy rather than link to yours, so that traffic from our site
never falls on your servers. The files you publish are about 675 by 900 pixels, which is more than
we need; if you would rather we used different files, or a particular credit line, please say and we
will. We will credit the Legislature in whatever form you specify, and we will remove any portrait
on request, without argument.

**One thing you should know before you decide.** Empowered Vote also publishes policy positions for
officials where we have researched them and can cite a source for each one — how someone voted, or
what they sponsored, on a given issue. Those positions are claims about the individual, and they sit
on the same page as the portrait. We hold no positions at all for any Mississippi legislator today,
but we expect to research them. I am telling you this before you answer so that your decision about
the photograph is an informed one.

To be clear about scope: a grant of permission to use the photograph is not an endorsement of
anything else on the page, and I would not represent it as one. It covers the portrait and nothing
else. Our editorial record stands or falls on its own sourcing, and is not something I am asking you
to approve.

**One small thing that may be useful to you either way.** Representative Grace Butler-Washington's
member record points at a file named `butler-washinton.jpg`, which returns a not-found error. The
photograph itself is on your server, under the correctly spelled `butler-washington.jpg`. Whatever
you decide about my request, the District 69 page is probably showing a broken image.

If it would help, I am happy to send the list of the 174 members we have seated, or to answer any
question about how the images would be stored and served.

With thanks for your time,

Chris Cantrell
Empowered Vote
chris@empowered.vote
