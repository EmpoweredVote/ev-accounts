▶ **Same ND-5 posture as the Wichita letter** (Cantrell, 2026-09-27): an official portrait a
government publishes so residents can recognise the person who holds an office is ordinary press
material, the service is free and non-profit, and the worst outcome is being asked to take them

not begun is misleading.

## What the county actually publishes, weighed correctly

🟢 **Sedgwick County asserts nothing over its web content.** There is no `©` and no "all rights
reserved" anywhere in the markup of the home page, the department pages, or a commissioner contact
page. `/policies-and-disclaimers/` has five children — HIPAA privacy, non-discrimination,
open-source software usage, website privacy, and a disclaimer — and **the disclaimer is about
external links**: it says the county does not endorse linked sites and disclaims responsibility for
their accuracy. It says nothing about reuse of the county's own material.

🔴 **BUT THERE IS A REAL RESTRICTION, AND IT IS SCOPED TO SOMETHING ELSE. READ THIS BEFORE
RE-DERIVING IT.** The county brand guide (`/media/73810/sc_brand-guide_aod.pdf`, 16 pp, linked from
the Media Room as "county logo and graphics standards") says:

> Per Resolution 1-1985, the Sedgwick County seal/logo is the property of Sedgwick County and may
> not be reproduced without permission. The same goes for all other seals, logos and symbols in this
> guide.

**The guide never uses the word "photograph" or "photo".** So the county has a considered,
resolution-backed restriction on its *marks* and has said nothing at all about *photographs of its
officeholders*.

▶ **This is the KS-5a shape exactly.** `portal.kansas.gov` carries a genuine ban on republication
and derivative works, and defines "the Site" as the Kansas.gov website — a different site under a
different operator, so it never reached kslegislature.gov. Here the restriction is real, is the
county's own, and covers the seal rather than the portraits. **Do not flatten "there is a
restriction somewhere on this domain" into "the portraits are restricted."** That is the error the
first Wichita draft made in the opposite direction.

⚠ **This is a weaker claim against us than Wichita's, not a stronger one.** `wichita.gov/copyright`
reserves all rights over all content; Sedgwick County reserves its seal. The letter is therefore
pitched as a notice rather than a permission request — but it still carries a real ask (better
files, a credit line) and a real offer (removal on request).

▶ **And the alternative is worse on both counts, as it was in Wichita.** If the county objected, the
fallback would be a news or campaign photograph, which carries a **stronger and more actively
enforced** copyright than a portrait a county publishes so residents can recognise their
commissioner. A substitute would buy no licence safety and serve both the officeholder and the voter
worse. The official portrait is the photograph the officeholder chose to be recognised by.

⚠ **THE LETTER ASKS ABOUT THE PHOTOGRAPH AND NOTHING ELSE.** The Wichita draft had to be corrected
for inviting a government to say whether it would rather its portraits "did not appear beside that
kind of content", which handed a government a say over whether we cover its own elected officials.
The compass disclosure stays — in full, before the ask — so the county's decision about the
photograph is informed. It is disclosure, not a negotiation over the editorial record.

## The email

**Subject:** Sedgwick County officeholder portraits on Empowered Vote — notice, and two small requests

Dear Ms Gibbs,

I am writing about Sedgwick County's official portrait photographs of its ten elected officeholders
— the five County Commissioners, the County Clerk, the County Treasurer, the Register of Deeds, the
Sheriff and the District Attorney — which we are now displaying on Empowered Vote, a non-partisan
voter information service.

The county's website carries no copyright notice and no photography policy, so there is nothing here
for me to ask permission against. I would rather tell you plainly what we are doing than rely on
that silence, and give you the chance to say otherwise.

**What we are.** Empowered Vote publishes a free, non-partisan civic reference. For each elected
official we show the office they hold, the district they represent, and how to contact them, so that
a resident who enters their address can see who represents them. We have just added Sedgwick County
and the City of Wichita to it.

**What we have done with the photographs.** We copied the ten portraits onto our own servers,
cropped each to a 4:5 frame, and display them beside the officeholder's name and office. We made no
other edit — no retouching, no colour change, no compositing, and nothing is enlarged. We host our
own copies rather than linking to yours, so that our pages place no load on the county's site and do
not break if a URL changes.

**We have read your brand guide, and we have not reproduced the county's marks.** I note Resolution
1-1985 and the guide's statement that the seal, logo and other county symbols may not be reproduced
without permission. We are not using them. The crops are head-and-shoulders frames: the Sheriff's
Office star that appears beside Sheriff Easter in the budget book is not in our frame, and neither
is his shoulder patch. If you consider anything still visible in these crops to fall under that
resolution, tell me and we will re-crop or remove.

**We also publish sourced policy positions for elected officials, and we intend to do so for these
ten.** I want this in front of you before you decide about the photographs, because a portrait will
sit beside that record. Alongside each official we publish, where we can evidence it, where that
person stands on specific policy questions — placed on a five-point scale, with the vote, bill,
resolution or public statement that supports it cited and visible to the reader. Nothing is inferred
from party affiliation. Anything we cannot evidence is left blank rather than guessed, and a blank
is a perfectly normal outcome. None of the ten has a position published today; our plan is to
research and publish them. I am happy to show you the live record for any official before you
decide.

To be straightforward about scope: **that coverage is not what I am writing to you about.** These
are elected officials, the record is drawn from public sources, Empowered Vote is non-profit and
makes no money from any of it, and we have no interest in characterising anyone as other than what
the evidence shows. Residents being able to identify the people who represent them, and see where
they stand, is the whole purpose of the service. I am telling you about it so that your decision
about the photographs is an informed one, not to ask whether we may cover Sedgwick County.

**No endorsement is implied or claimed, in either direction.** Our use of a portrait does not
suggest that the county, the Board, or any officeholder endorses Empowered Vote, and it equally does
not mean the county has reviewed, approved or agrees with anything we publish. To be explicit, this
is **not** a claim that the page around the portrait is silent about that official's positions — it
is not, and the paragraph above says what it carries. The positions are ours to source and ours to
answer for, and if we ever get one wrong I want to hear about it.

**Two small requests:**

1. Several of these came out of the budget book because it is the highest-resolution version the
   county publishes — the website serves some of these portraits at 100 × 133 pixels, and the budget
   PDF carries the same sittings at around 726 × 816. If you hold the original files, we would be
   grateful for them; the portraits would simply look better. We do not enlarge photographs, because
   enlarging adds no detail and only makes a file look better than it is, so a better original is
   the only thing that improves them.
2. Please tell us how you would like the photographs credited. We attach a credit line to each image
   and display it, so whatever you specify will travel with the picture.

**Disclosure: the photographs are already displayed.** The Sedgwick County pages are live and all
ten portraits are on them now, served from our own servers. I would rather tell you that than write
as though the use had not begun. **If you would prefer we take them down, say so and we will** — we
would show the office and the name without a picture instead. Nothing about that would be a
difficulty for us.

I am happy to answer any question about the service, to show you the pages, or to speak by phone.
