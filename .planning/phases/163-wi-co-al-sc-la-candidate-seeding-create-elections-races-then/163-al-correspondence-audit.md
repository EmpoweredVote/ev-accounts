## Background (from named sources)

Following the U.S. Supreme Court's April 29, 2026 ruling in *Louisiana v. Callais* (which narrowed Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act), Governor Kay Ivey called a special legislative session in which the Alabama Legislature passed a bill reviving the state's 2023 GOP-drawn congressional map (1 majority-Black district) for use in the 2026 cycle, contingent on the federal injunction against that map being lifted. On May 11, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court vacated the district court's block on the 2023 map and remanded for further proceedings; Ivey immediately called a special primary for August 11, 2026 in the four districts whose lines move under the 2023 map — **Districts 1, 2, 6, and 7** — nullifying the May 19, 2026 primary results in those four districts only (Districts 3, 4, 5 kept their May 19/June 16 results). On May 26, 2026, the three-judge federal panel reiterated that the 2023 map is intentionally racially discriminatory and blocked its use; on June 2, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court stayed that ruling on a 6-3 vote (*Allen v. Milligan* shadow-docket order), allowing the 2023 map to govern the August 11 special primary and the November 3 general election. Alabama's Attorney General and Governor publicly celebrated the ruling; the *Milligan* plaintiffs (NAACP Legal Defense Fund) stated the fight is "not over," but as of this audit no further stay, reversal, or injunction has issued.

**Litigation status (re-verified 2026-07-05, same-day as this audit's execution):**
- All About Redistricting (Loyola Law School's authoritative tracker) shows the current map-status period as **"Congress, Jun 02, 2026 – Present"** with Plan Status **"In litigation"** but *in effect* — i.e., the 2023 map is the live, operative map for the 2026 cycle as of this audit date, with the underlying discrimination finding stayed (not reversed) for this cycle only.
- Democracy Docket's *Milligan v. Allen* case tracker's most recent entries (June 2–3, 2026) confirm the Supreme Court's stay is the last action; a fresh DuckDuckGo news search on 2026-07-05 for "Alabama congressional map special primary August 11 2026 news" surfaced no reporting of any subsequent stay, reversal, or emergency order between June 3 and July 5, 2026.
- **Conclusion: the 2023 map is still operative for the August 11 special primary and the November 3 general election as of this audit's execution date (2026-07-05), squarely within the 7-day freshness window used by the 161/162 audits.**

## Severity Rubric

*(Cloned verbatim from the TN audit, 161-01, itself cloned by the MO audit, 162-01.)*

A district is scored **SEVERE** if either condition holds:
1. **>25% of the district's population moved to a different congressional district** between the old (2024-remedial, currently-live-in-`essentials.districts`) map and the new (2023-reinstated, 2026-operative) map, OR
2. **The district's core anchor city/county changed** — i.e., the metro area or county that defined the district's political and geographic identity under the old map is no longer the anchor under the new map (either lost entirely to another district, or a new metro anchor was added that wasn't previously present).

Otherwise a district is scored **NOT-SEVERE**.

**Evidence basis:** No shapefile/GIS diff was performed (per 163-RESEARCH.md's Don't-Hand-Roll guidance — a qualitative county-level breakdown from named reporting is sufficient and avoids installing any new GIS dependency). Severity is derived from (a) explicit county-level old-vs-new comparison paragraphs in Wikipedia's "2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Alabama" article (fetched via `Special:Export` raw wikitext, revision timestamp 2026-07-04T19:04:15Z, itself citing Alabama Arise's official explainer for the county-reassignment claims), (b) Alabama Reflector's June 2, 2026 report that Black Voting Age Population (BVAP) in AL-2 drops from 48.7% to 39.9% under the new map and that election officials had "less than a day to reassign voters in 14 counties whose districts will change" statewide, and (c) explicit "anchor changed" language cross-checked against the Alabama Arise Aug-11 special-primary explainer (fetched directly, 2026-06-08 publication date).

**Critical methodological finding of this audit (see Notes for downstream consumers):** the fact that a district was assigned an August 11 special primary is a *procedural* signal (any line movement legally requires re-running that district's primary) — it is **not** the severity rubric itself, per 163-RESEARCH.md's Pitfall 1 warning. This audit tested that assumption directly against the four special-primary districts and found it does **not** hold uniformly: only one of the four (AL-2) crosses the severity threshold on the evidence.

## Notes for downstream consumers (163-04, 163-11)

- **AL-2 (0102)** is the unambiguous, expected-severe case — every named source (Alabama Reflector, the SCOTUS briefing itself, the plaintiffs' public statements, Figures' own statement) converges on AL-2 as the map's central target: it loses its entire majority-Black Mobile-area constituency, and district BVAP drops 48.7%→39.9%, ending its status as a Black-opportunity district. This matches the RESEARCH.md pre-audit hypothesis.
- **AL-3/4/5 (0103/0104/0105)** are confirmed not-severe, matching the RESEARCH.md pre-audit expectation exactly — these three districts were never part of the special-primary set and no source reports any boundary change to them at all.
- **The central, non-obvious finding of this audit: AL-1, AL-6, and AL-7 (0101/0106/0107) — three of the four Aug-11 special-primary districts — score NOT-SEVERE.** This directly tests and refutes 163-RESEARCH.md's Critical-Question-1 prediction that "AL-1/2/6/7 will score SEVERE" as a bloc. The special primary is a *procedural* trigger (Alabama law requires re-running a district's primary whenever ANY portion of its lines move, regardless of magnitude — confirmed by Alabama Arise's explainer, which frames the redo purely in terms of "votes cast... were voided" for districts whose lines changed at all) — it is not itself evidence of a severity-rubric-crossing boundary shift. When each of the three is examined on the county-level evidence:
  - **AL-1** *gains* population (full Mobile County, previously split) and only cedes two secondary Wiregrass counties (Coffee, Dale) — its Gulf Coast anchor is strengthened, not weakened.
  - **AL-6** cedes only half of one county (Elmore) — explicitly described by Wikipedia's sourced breakdown as "the only change."
  - **AL-7** cedes only one small county (Lowndes, ~9k, ~1% of the district) while gaining two others (Monroe, Conecuh) — a net population *gain*, not loss, with its Black Belt anchor fully intact.
- This is the mirror image of the TN/MO audits' "don't under-scope" finding (both of which found MORE severe districts than predicted). Here, rigorous per-district evidence-gathering finds FEWER severe districts than the pre-audit hypothesis — which is exactly what the audit process is designed to test for in both directions. The evidentiary bar applied (BVAP-drop + anchor-loss for AL-2; named "only change" language + sub-25%-population-moved estimates for AL-1/6/7) is the same rubric applied identically across all seven districts; it was not relaxed for the three non-severe special-primary districts.
- **Confidence caveat:** exact census-block population figures for the specific county/partial-county reassignments were not available without a GIS/shapefile diff (out of scope per RESEARCH's Don't-Hand-Roll guidance); the population-moved percentages above are qualitative estimates built from named-source county population figures and Wikipedia's county-level district descriptions, not a certified apportionment calculation. If more precise figures later emerge (e.g., during 163-11's gate work or Phase 164.1's polygon refresh), AL-1's and AL-4's borderline-adjacent estimates should be spot-checked, though the anchor-based rationale (which does not depend on precise percentages) independently supports the NOT-SEVERE calls for 0101/0106/0107.
- **Re-verify litigation status before AL race authoring in 163-04** if more than a few days have elapsed since 2026-07-05 (the freshness window used by 161/162). The underlying discrimination finding against the 2023 map was never overturned on the merits — only stayed for this cycle — and the *Milligan* plaintiffs have stated the fight continues; a further emergency order remains theoretically possible before the November 3 general election, though none has issued as of this audit.
