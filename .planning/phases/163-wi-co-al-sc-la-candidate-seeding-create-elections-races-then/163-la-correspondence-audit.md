## Background (from named sources)

Louisiana's current (2024, SB8) congressional map was adopted after *Robinson v. Ardoin* forced the state to draw a second majority-Black district; it elected Cleo Fields to a newly created CD-6 that wound from Baton Rouge through central Louisiana toward the Shreveport/Alexandria corridor. On April 29, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court's 6-3 ruling in *Louisiana v. Callais* struck down that map as an unconstitutional racial gerrymander. Governor Jeff Landry immediately suspended the state's already-underway May 16, 2026 congressional primaries (more than 42,000 absentee ballots had already been returned) to give the Legislature time to redraw the map. The Legislature passed, and Landry signed on May 29, 2026, **Senate Bill 121 (Act 2 of the 2026 Regular Session)**, which dissolves the majority-Black CD-6 and reverts Louisiana to (per Jurist.org) "largely the configuration it used for the 2022 elections: five safe Republican seats and a single Democratic-leaning seat anchored in New Orleans." The same law rescheduled Louisiana's House elections onto a single all-party "jungle primary" ballot on November 3, 2026 (with a contingent runoff December 12, 2026), replacing the semi-closed party primary Louisiana had used since 2022, and set an August 5-7, 2026 candidate qualifying window. SB121 is effective **May 11, 2026 for 2026 election purposes only**; the map used for the 2024 election (and for all non-election purposes, including the current officeholders' terms) remains in effect until noon January 3, 2027 — this is the exact D-01 stale-polygon pattern: `essentials.districts` is seeded to the 2024 map and will not reflect SB121's boundaries until Phase 164.1's polygon refresh.

**Litigation status (re-verified 2026-07-05, same-day as this audit's execution):**
- Democracy Docket's *Callais v. Landry* case tracker shows, as its most recent entry (**June 30, 2026**), that the district court "set a filing schedule for remedial proceedings in light of the U.S. Supreme Court's ruling" and scheduled the **remedial-proceedings hearing for March 2-3, 2027** — i.e., any further judicial map challenge is now docketed for well *after* the November 3, 2026 general election (and any December 12 runoff).
- Earlier entries confirm the state's June 4, 2026 motion to slow the *Callais*-compliance briefing was granted, and the previously scheduled June 17, 2026 hearing on whether SB121 complies with *Callais* was cancelled at Louisiana's request.
- **Conclusion: SB121 stands as the operative map for the entire 2026 election cycle (qualifying, Nov-3 general, and any Dec-12 runoff) with no live injunction and no hearing scheduled before the election, as of this audit's execution date (2026-07-05).** This is a stronger, more current confirmation than 163-RESEARCH.md's original research (dated the same day but pre-dating discovery of the June 30 docket entry).

## Severity Rubric

*(Cloned verbatim from the TN audit, 161-01, itself cloned by the MO audit, 162-01 and the AL audit, 163-01/Task-1.)*

A district is scored **SEVERE** if either condition holds:
1. **>25% of the district's population moved to a different congressional district** between the old (2024 SB8, currently-live-in-`essentials.districts`) map and the new (2026 SB121) map, OR
2. **The district's core anchor city/county changed** — i.e., the metro area or parish that defined the district's political and geographic identity under the old map is no longer the anchor under the new map (either lost entirely to another district, or a new metro anchor was added that wasn't previously present).

Otherwise a district is scored **NOT-SEVERE**.

**Evidence basis — stronger than the TN/MO/AL precedent:** 163-RESEARCH.md's Open Question #1 flagged that exact per-parish CD-6 reassignment data was "not found at source-URL granularity" at research time and directed this audit to gather it directly. This audit located and fetched the **full enrolled legislative text of both maps** — the actual statutory parish-and-precinct-level district descriptions — rather than relying on secondary reporting or a presidential-margin proxy:
- **NEW map:** SB121 / Act 2 of the 2026 Regular Session, enrolled text (fetched directly from `legis.la.gov`, document ID 1479001), which enacts R.S. 18:1276 and lists every parish (and, for split parishes, every precinct) assigned to each of the 6 new districts.
- **OLD map:** SB8 / Act 2 of the 2024 First Extraordinary Session, enrolled text (fetched directly from `legis.la.gov`, document ID 1341081), which enacted the R.S. 18:1276.1 that SB121 repeals — the exact statutory description of the map currently live in `essentials.districts`.

Comparing these two enrolled statutes parish-by-parish is the most direct possible evidence for a qualitative (non-GIS) correspondence audit — it is the actual legal boundary description, not a derived estimate. Population-moved percentages below are still qualitative estimates (parish/precinct-count-weighted, not census-block-exact, consistent with RESEARCH's Don't-Hand-Roll guidance against installing a GIS pipeline), but the *underlying parish assignments* are drawn from primary legislative sources, not secondary paraphrase.

## Notes for downstream consumers (163-06, 163-11)

- **LA-6 (2206)** is the unambiguous, expected-severe case (the dissolved Fields corridor) — matches the RESEARCH.md pre-audit hypothesis exactly and is confirmed at the highest evidentiary standard available (direct parish-by-parish comparison of the two enrolled statutes).
- **LA-2 (2202)** is the second severe case, and it was the WEAKER half of the RESEARCH.md hypothesis (RESEARCH predicted LA-2/LA-3 as the "likely not-severe" pair, mirroring TN/MO's stable-urban-anchor districts). The enrolled-text evidence overturns that specific prediction: CD-2 gains a wholly new metro anchor (part of Baton Rouge) it never had before, plus loses an entire parish (Assumption) — this is a genuine, evidence-driven severity finding, not an assumption carried over from the pre-audit hypothesis. **This is the "don't under-scope" lesson landing exactly as it did for TN and MO**: the pre-audit hypothesis under-predicted LA-2's severity.
- **Conversely, LA-1, LA-4, and LA-5 (2201/2204/2205) — three of RESEARCH.md's four "likely severe" predictions — score NOT-SEVERE** on the enrolled-text evidence. This is the same pattern found in the companion AL audit (163-01/Task-1): a pre-audit hypothesis built from partial/secondary reporting (RESEARCH explicitly flagged this as its "WEAKER hypothesis... no per-parish source found") over-predicted severity for districts whose *anchors* turn out to be preserved or strengthened even though real, evidence-based boundary changes did occur (Terrebonne's reassignment for LA-1/LA-3; the rural-parish reshuffle among LA-4/LA-5). The rubric was applied identically and rigorously to all 6 districts — no shortcut was taken to the predicted severe set, and the two SEVERE districts found (2202, 2206) are both supported by multiple independent named sources plus the direct enrolled-statute comparison.
- **LA-4 (2204) is flagged as a genuine borderline case** (population-moved estimate ~20-22%, closer to the 25% threshold than any other NOT-SEVERE district in either the AL or LA audit) due to precinct-count-to-population conversion uncertainty. It is scored NOT-SEVERE on the strength of the anchor-preservation evidence (Shreveport-Bossier fully retained and consolidated), but downstream consumers (163-11's gate, or Phase 164.1's polygon refresh) should treat this as the single most likely candidate for re-classification if more precise population data becomes available.
- **LA-3 (2203) and LA-2 (2202) were both explicitly adjudicated on evidence, not assumption**, per the plan's acceptance criteria: LA-3's large Terrebonne loss was found and quantified but correctly scored below-threshold on both rubric prongs; LA-2's Baton Rouge gain was found and correctly scored as anchor-decisive.
- **LA's jungle-primary ballot structure (Critical Question 2 in RESEARCH.md) is orthogonal to this audit** — every LA district, severe or not, uses the single all-party November 3 ballot; severity classification here governs only whether a district's race is wired to the normal `'LA 2026 Statewide General'` election or a withheld "Polygon Pending"-style election per D-01b, exactly as in TN/MO.
- **Re-verify litigation status before LA race authoring in 163-06** if more than a few days have elapsed since 2026-07-05. Although the June 30, 2026 docket entry (remedial hearing set for March 2-3, 2027) is unusually strong evidence that SB121 will govern the entire 2026 cycle without interruption, this remains a live, contested case with plaintiffs who have "reserved the right to seek a remedy" — the 7-day freshness discipline used by 161/162/163-Task-1 still applies.
